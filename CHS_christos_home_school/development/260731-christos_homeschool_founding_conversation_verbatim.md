## ROUND 1 — WORKER — verbatim

> I read the Napoleon piece before answering, and it changed what I
> think the core asset here is. So let me start there.
>
> **The overlay is the product.** That piece is genuinely good — and
> more importantly, it has a *form*: establish the standard, steelman
> the aspiration, evaluate the execution, assess the alternatives,
> extract the lessons. That's a repeatable protocol, not a one-off. And
> critically, it isn't confirmatory. You give Roberts real ground on
> meritocracy and legal reform, then take the grapeshot passage apart,
> name "living off the land" as plunder, and note that Britain was
> profiting from the slave trade in the same breath. That's analysis.
> There is nothing else like it in the homeschool market: families
> using Peterson Academy or PragerU today *consume* that content, and
> nobody is teaching their children to interrogate it. I'd build the
> business around that before anything else.
>
> Now the three things I'd push back on, hardest first.
>
> **"Homeschool/charter" is two incompatible businesses, and you have
> to pick.** A charter school is a public school, and a Bible-centered
> public school is not legally available. Oklahoma tried it with St.
> Isidore, a virtual K-12 Catholic charter; the Oklahoma Supreme Court
> held the contract violated the state's nonsectarian requirements and
> the federal Establishment Clause, and the U.S. Supreme Court
> deadlocked 4–4 in May 2025, leaving that ruling in place. Montana is
> thinner still: the state's only ESA is special-needs-only and was
> struck down in December 2025, with current participants continuing
> through June 30, 2026 pending appeal, and there's no universal choice
> program. So the charter path costs you the moral center, the
> timeline, and the autonomy — and buys you a funding stream that may
> not exist. Run it as a private homeschool program under the
> 508(c)(1)(a), sell direct to families, and skip state authorization
> entirely. Marilyn's credential is still the crown jewel there — not
> for authorization, but for scope-and-sequence, transcripts, and
> records that transfer when a student applies to college.
>
> **CPP as the ontological center is the one I'd change.** I want to be
> careful here, because this is your standard, not mine. You built a
> seven-criterion promotion ledger, a five-seat adversarial panel, a
> withheld-challenge protocol, and a founder decision that holds three
> finished papers off release over a single unverified clause.
> Yesterday you registered a defect against yourself rather than bank a
> clean integrity round you hadn't earned. That discipline is why the
> programme is credible.
>
> Teaching CPP to a fifteen-year-old as the ontological frame of their
> physics education is a much stronger claim than anything that
> apparatus permits you to make in a paper. A student can't evaluate
> CPP against the Standard Model; they'd receive it as settled. And
> there's a concrete cost: that student sits for AP Physics or walks
> into freshman mechanics and finds the vocabulary doesn't map.
>
> But there's a version that's better than what you proposed. Teach
> standard physics to standard competency — then use CPP as the
> capstone case study in *how a theory actually gets built and
> constrained*. Show them the promotion ledger. Show them a defect that
> would have doubled every number and was caught by a print statement.
> Show them a reviewer with four confirmed fabrication events and the
> rule that exists because of it. Show them a founder holding his own
> papers. No other program on earth can put a live research programme's
> adversarial record in front of a student in real time. That teaches
> epistemology by demonstration rather than slogan, it's honest about
> CPP's status, and it's a far stronger differentiator than "our
> physics is different."
>
> **The third-party dependency is a real business risk.** Peterson
> Academy and Liberty Classroom are subscriptions; a family would pay
> you *plus* several hundred dollars a year before your curriculum
> works. Commentary and criticism sit on solid fair-use ground, but you
> can't embed or re-host their video, and a curriculum whose spine is
> another company's paywalled catalog breaks when they change terms or
> pricing. Get written permission where you can, and build enough
> original overlay material that no single source removal takes out a
> unit. (PragerU Kids is the friendliest of the three on classroom use
> — worth confirming directly.)
>
> **Two smaller ones.** An AI tutor talking to minors needs real safety
> and privacy design up front — COPPA applies under 13 — not bolted on
> later. And you already have four full-time workstreams. The honest
> question for Marilyn is whether she's the *operator* or a credentialed
> advisor, because scope-and-sequence across thirteen grades is
> thousands of hours and it will not come out of your margins.
>
> **What I'd do first:** ship the overlay library as a standalone
> product — one subject, one semester, one grade band. It's already
> proven, it needs no accreditation, it tests whether families will pay,
> and it's the piece nobody else has. The full K-12 program can be built
> on top of a thing that's already working.
>
> One craft note, since you'll be producing these at volume: make sure
> some overlays conclude that the source was right and the Christian
> prior needed adjusting — including, occasionally, against PragerU. If
> every analysis lands on "insufficiently Christian," students learn a
> template instead of a method, and a skeptical parent will spot it
> immediately. The Napoleon piece doesn't make that mistake. Keeping it
> that way at scale is the whole game.

## ROUND 2 — WORKER — verbatim

*(Delivery mechanics — the apply-and-push block for this patch — omitted
as non-substantive.)*

> **On pointers rather than duplication — agreed, and I think it's a
> bigger idea than it looks.** You already have nineteen content modules.
> CHR, CAP, CWR, CEA, CFE, CRF, CMD, CMT. The homeschool isn't a content
> project; it's a sequencing layer over a corpus that mostly already
> exists. That changes the build estimate by an order of magnitude, and
> it's what makes a real MVP reachable this year instead of in three.
>
> But it makes one artifact load-bearing: **the curriculum map.** A file
> in CHS that lists units in order and, for each unit, points at source
> material by module code and filename, with the learning objective and
> the overlay attached. Two properties matter. Pointers have to survive a
> file being renamed, so reference by a stable ID you assign rather than
> by a path — the CPP corpus already taught you that lesson during the
> SSCA migration, when 126 path references had to be swept. And the map
> has to be the single place sequence is defined, or order will drift
> into three files and disagree.
>
> That map is also what the AI tutor consumes. The tutor isn't trained on
> the corpus in any meaningful sense; it's handed the map and retrieves
> against it. Build the map well and the tutor becomes a much smaller
> problem — mostly retrieval and pacing rather than anything exotic.
>
> **On CPP as the layer below conventional physics — here I have to be
> precise, because we're closer than last round but not in the same
> place.**
>
> What I proposed: CPP as the *object* of study — the case study in how a
> theory actually gets built and constrained.
>
> What you're describing: CPP as the explanatory *substrate* that makes
> conventional physics easier — the layer below.
>
> The second is still an ontological claim delivered to a student as
> explanation. "Why does the field behave this way? Because underneath,
> X." A fifteen-year-old receives that as fact, because it answers the
> why-question, and the why-answer is exactly where belief attaches.
>
> Two specific costs. First, physics education research is fairly brutal
> on this point: the hard part of intro physics isn't installing correct
> models, it's dislodging plausible incorrect ones. Students arrive with
> impetus intuitions and much of the teaching is demolition work. A
> substrate model that isn't the consensus account is a candidate for
> precisely that kind of persistent alternative conception — and a
> stickier one than the naive intuitions, because it's coherent and it
> came from a trusted authority. Second, CPP is under active revision by
> your own hand. Candidate B sits at PARTIAL with PR7 clause 2 open. If a
> student's understanding of *why* electromagnetism works rests on a
> layer you revise in 2028, their scaffolding moves under them. When CPP
> is the object of study, a revision is the lesson. When it's the
> substrate, a revision is a retraction.
>
> **That said — your claim might simply be right.** "CPP framing makes
> conventional physics easier to learn" is an empirical claim about
> student outcomes, and it is testable. You are the last person who needs
> telling to test a claim before building on it. Teach one unit both
> ways to a small cohort, use a standard instrument — the Force Concept
> Inventory is the obvious choice, with decades of normative data behind
> it — and look at the numbers. If CPP framing produces better
> *standard*-physics outcomes, you have something genuinely valuable and
> a defensible thing to advertise. If it doesn't, you found out on twelve
> students rather than on a cohort.
>
> And there's a version that gets most of what you want without the cost:
> offer the CPP framing explicitly labelled as a live research
> programme's proposal rather than as the answer. "Here is the standard
> account. Here is how one active programme proposes the substrate might
> work. Here is what would distinguish them." A student who learns that
> is getting a better education than one who learns either account alone
> — and it's honest about epistemic status, which is the thing your
> entire review apparatus exists to protect.
>
> **Last: the entity question is still open, and it's a different
> question from the folder.** When a family pays for this, which entity
> takes the money, signs the terms, and carries the liability?
> Renaissance Ministries holds the 508(c)(1)(a); Hyperphysics Institute
> is the research body. Worth deciding before the first dollar rather
> than after.

## Provenance of the worker's factual claims

  → Alternatives Assessed → Lessons Extracted) is read off the article's

**Searched and verified:**
- *St. Isidore of Seville Catholic Virtual School.* Oklahoma Supreme
  Court (2024) held the charter contract violated state nonsectarian
  requirements and the federal Establishment Clause; U.S. Supreme Court
  affirmed by an equally divided court, 4–4, 22 May 2025 (Barrett
  recused), leaving the state ruling in place. An equally divided
  affirmance carries no precedential force nationally — it settles
  Oklahoma, not the country. The worker's claim that a Bible-centered
  *charter* is legally unavailable rests on the general principle that
  charter schools are public schools by statute in every state, which
  the case affirmed rather than created.
- *Montana.* The Special Needs Equal Opportunity ESA is
  disability-restricted (IDEA-qualifying), was ruled unconstitutional in
  December 2025, and is stayed for existing participants through
  30 June 2026 pending appeal. Montana has no universal school-choice
  program. Montana's 2023 "community choice schools" statute was
  separately halted in state court.

**Asserted from general knowledge, NOT independently verified — flagged
as the weakest claims in the reply:**
- Peterson Academy and Liberty Classroom subscription pricing and terms
  of service. The worker did not read either terms-of-service document.
- The characterisation of PragerU Kids as the most permissive of the
  three on classroom use.
- The fair-use characterisation is a general statement of the
  commentary-and-criticism principle, not a legal opinion, and the
  worker is not a lawyer.

## Standing craft constraint proposed by the worker

Overlay analyses must sometimes conclude **for** the source and
**against** the Christian prior, including against PragerU, or the form
degrades from method into template. Recorded here as a proposed
editorial constraint on the overlay line, not as an adopted rule.

## Cross-module note — RESOLVED Round 2

Raised at filing: the overlay line already operates as **CHR — Christos
Historical Review**, so CHS and CHR appeared to overlap.

**Founder ruling, Round 2:** CHR is content; CHS is the program that
directs progress across topics. Material stays in its home module and CHS
references it by pointer rather than duplicating it. This generalises
beyond CHR to the whole module set — CHS is a sequencing layer over the
existing corpus, not a content silo.

**Consequence:** the CHS curriculum map becomes the load-bearing artifact
of the module (Decision 8), and it is also the retrieval surface the AI
tutor consumes.
