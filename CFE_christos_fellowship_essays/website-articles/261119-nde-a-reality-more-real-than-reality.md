# 261119 - NDE - A Reality More Real than Reality

# More Real Than This:


### An Engineer's Near-Death Experience

*Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 19 November 2026*

I recently listened to the account of a Spanish engineer named Alanis Marina, translated into English on The Other Side NDE channel. Her story is worth careful attention for three reasons. She was a committed skeptic who did not believe in God or in any life after death. Her experience did not come from a car wreck, an operating table, or a drug, but from a simple breathing exercise at a meditation retreat. The conclusions she drew from it are a mix of insight and error that I think deserves an honest sorting.

I stand on the side that what she experienced was real: her consciousness left her body, entered a realm more solid than this one, and returned. I also believe that several of the conclusions she drew from it are wrong.

## Her Story

In April 2015, Alanis was facing her father's terminal cancer. She described herself as the kind of engineer who wants to control everything. She had never been drunk or taken a drug, because anything that loosened her grip on her own mind had no place in her life. She did not believe in God, because no one could prove God to her, and she believed death was the end. Yet she was troubled. She watched other people face death calmly, and something in her asked whether it could really all just stop.

She took a vacation at a meditation retreat in the Gredos mountains of Spain, mostly for rest and because she had read that meditation was good for mental clarity. One of the leaders, a woman from Venezuela who taught breathing and what she called Tantra (in her usage, connection with life and death), offered an exercise she said would remove their fear of death. Alanis was suspicious. She asked whether they would have to take a drug or inhale anything. The answer was no: the exercises were gentle, with no hyperventilating and no breath-holding.

In one movement, the participants threw their heads back suddenly while pushing the breath out downward. At that moment Alanis felt herself shoot out of her body, straight up, faster than she could think, "as if launched from a slingshot." She passed through a white light that became a light of many colors, heard a sound she could not describe, and found herself in another world.

What struck her was that she did not arrive there as a visitor. She had no memory of this life at all. She felt she had always been there. The feeling was one of complete rightness: no crack through which doubt could enter, a love she calls union or communion, and a certainty she says does not exist on earth. She said the most frequent thought she had after returning was that, over there, she had felt more like herself than ever before.

There were people there, and their faces had no wrinkles or blemishes, but what held her was their gaze: life, kindness, and a settled certainty that was never arrogance. She looked at them, open-mouthed, not in surprise but in recognition: this is my element, and I am like that too. In front of her sat a woman on something like a throne, with two small children beside her.

Then she felt something pulling her from behind. When she turned, everything behind her was dark, and a tremendous force drew her backward while the bright world receded. She heard a voice saying, "Come back." She protested that she was trying to go back home, to where she belonged. Then a thought arose in her own mind: it is you asking yourself to come back, so relax and trust. She let go, and what she calls "the odyssey of the return" began.

First came a physical crushing, as if a steamroller were squeezing her head into a space too small for it. Then came a wave of emotions she had not felt on the other side: doubt, fear, pain. She watched images of that world being erased from her memory by something outside her, and she fought to hold on. The feelings and a few images survived. Then her "resume" was filled back in: your name is this, you live here, you are an engineer, you are in Gredos doing an exercise with this woman. She opened her eyes on the floor. Someone was striking her chest, and the retreat leader was saying, "Come back, come back, come back."

Only after her identity returned did she realize the woman on the throne was her own mother, who is still alive. There, she had not felt her as a mother but as an equal; there were no distinctions of old and young, parent and child.

For days afterward she barely ate. Ordinary things looked lifeless and fake to her, like a cardboard movie set of New York. For a year or more, her inner voice carried an overwhelming certainty it had never had before. A few months later her father died, and she went through it with serenity, quietly telling him at the end that this was all a dream and he would wake in a more beautiful reality. About a year later, she read Eben Alexander's *Proof of Heaven* and found striking parallels to her own experience. Some time after that, physicians who heard her story explained what had probably happened to her body.

## What Happened to Her Body

The doctors told her that she had probably suffered a vagal event, which they called a "presyncope of death" caused by a pinch of the vagus nerve. The underlying idea is sound, but as a physician I would correct the description. The vagus nerve is not literally pinched in a maneuver like this. What happens is a reflex. Throwing the head back hard can stimulate the pressure sensors in the neck (the carotid sinus), and forcefully bearing down on an exhalation works like straining on the toilet, briefly reducing the blood returning to the heart. Either can trigger a powerful vagal response that sharply slows the heart, sometimes to a pause of several seconds, and drops blood pressure. The brain loses its blood supply, and the person collapses. That is a vasovagal (reflex) syncope, and the word "presyncope" is not quite right, since presyncope means nearly fainting while staying conscious. Alanis lost consciousness completely and, by her own account, dropped "like a sack of potatoes." Also, the vagus nerve does not run "all the way down to the sphincter"; its branches to the bowel end around the middle of the colon, and nerves from the base of the spine supply the lower bowel.

I cannot tell from her account whether her heart actually stopped. Someone struck her chest, which suggests the people around her believed it had, but no one reports taking a pulse. A brief reflex pause is common in fainting and usually resolves on its own when the person lies flat.

The important point is this: the physiology explains how she lost consciousness. It does not explain what she experienced while unconscious. A fainting brain is a brain starved of blood; it is expected to produce confusion, fragments, and grayness, not the most lucid, ordered, and real-seeming experience of a person's life. The mechanism is a doorway, not a description of the room.

## What She Got Right

**Consciousness is not the body.** Alanis watched her father go, in an instant, from a person with opinions and personality to an empty body, and she asked the right question: who was the one in there? Her experience answered it for her. We are a body inhabited by a spirit, and when the body can no longer hold the spirit, the spirit goes where it came from. Scripture said this long before she did: *"Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it."* **(Ecclesiastes 12:7)**

**The other world is more real than this one.** Her sense that earth looked like painted cardboard afterward is common in these accounts, and it agrees with Scripture's ranking of the two: *"For the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal."* **(2 Corinthians 4:18)** C. S. Lewis pictured the same thing in *The Great Divorce*, where heaven is so solid that the visiting ghosts cannot bend its grass.

**We will be known, and we will know ourselves.** Her repeated phrase, "I felt more like myself than ever," is one of the most important things she said. It matches Paul: *"For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known."* **(1 Corinthians 13:12)**

**Here we walk by faith, not certainty.** She said that on earth there can be faith and trust, but not certainty. That is exactly the condition Scripture describes for this life: *"(For we walk by faith, not by sight:)"* **(2 Corinthians 5:7)**

**Earthly relationships are transformed there.** She saw her mother as an equal, without the distinctions of parent and child. Jesus told the Sadducees that the earthly arrangements of family do not carry over in the same form: *"For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven."* **(Matthew 22:30)** And her description of faces free of the marks of fear and regret fits the promise that *"God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes"* **(Revelation 21:4)**.

**This world tends toward decay.** As an engineer, she noticed that a skyscraper takes years to build and minutes to demolish, and that the emotional world here leans toward fear, separation, and violence, just as the physical world leans toward disorder. Paul said creation itself was *"made subject to vanity"* and waits to be *"delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God."* **(Romans 8:20–21)**

**Motive matters, and humility about our neighbor matters.** She urged people to watch where their actions come from inside, and to stop playing God over others. That is true as far as it goes: *"for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart"* **(1 Samuel 16:7)**, and the humility she describes is what Jesus asks of us before we correct anyone: *"Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye."* **(Matthew 7:5)**

## Where Her Interpretation Goes Wrong

**"This isn't real; it's a dream with no consequences."** This is the conclusion I disagree with most. Her analogy is that in a dream you lose an arm, then wake and find both arms intact, so nothing really happened. She concluded we should not give this world too much weight. But a vivid glimpse of a greater reality does not make the lesser one unreal. God looked at this creation and called it *"very good"* **(Genesis 1:31)**. It is fallen and dense, but it is not a stage set. And Scripture is clear that what we do in the body carries forward: *"For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he hath done, whether it be good or bad."* **(2 Corinthians 5:10)** *"Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap."* **(Galatians 6:7)**

**"There is no judgment, and a God who judges is a bad God."** She did not feel judged on the other side, and from that she concluded that judgment itself belongs to the density of earth. The absence of judgment in a few seconds of her experience is not evidence that there is none. Eben Alexander's guide told him something similar, that there was nothing he could do wrong. This theme recurs in near-death accounts, and I think it is the point where these experiences most need to be tested against Scripture rather than taken as the last word: *"And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment"* **(Hebrews 9:27)**. A glimpse of the love of God is not the whole of the encounter with God. She also slides from "we should not judge one another" to "God does not judge," and those are not the same claim.

*"Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again."* **(Matthew 7:1–2)** This passage is commonly read to mean that we should never judge anyone. But Jesus's point, as the next verses show, is hypocrisy: condemning the speck in a brother's eye while ignoring the beam in our own **(Matthew 7:3–5)**. Elsewhere He commands the opposite of never judging: *"Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment."* **(John 7:24)** Scripture, taken as a whole, teaches us to judge between right and wrong, good and evil, Godly and unGodly, in every moment, both in ourselves and in others. We judge so that we can resist what is wrong and do what is right, and so that we can fellowship together, building up and encouraging one another to live God's way. The warning of verse 2 still stands: the measure we use on others will be used on us, so we must apply it to ourselves first. And God judges what we have done in the body; we will be rewarded according to our faithfulness in managing His creation according to His will.

**"Only intention counts, not outcome."** God looks on the heart, and He judges mercifully the good intentions that fail under pressure. But Scripture does not set deeds aside. The same passage that tells us God sees the heart also says each person will receive according to what he has done.

**"It was me calling myself back."** She concluded that the voice saying "come back" was her own deeper self. But when she opened her eyes, the retreat leader was standing over her, saying, "Come back, come back, come back." The simplest reading is that she heard the woman in the room. That is itself remarkable, since it suggests some awareness of the room while she was unconscious. But it should make us careful with the meanings she drew from it in the moment. An experience can be real while the interpretation layered on it in the moment is mistaken.

**A note on the Alexander comparison.** Alanis said Eben Alexander also met a relative who was still alive. That is not correct as he tells it. The young woman who guided him was his biological sister Betsy, whom he had never met; he was adopted, and she had already died before he reconnected with his birth family. He recognized her later from a photograph. Alanis's encounter with her living mother therefore has no parallel in Alexander's account.

## The Piece She Is Missing

The deepest reason this world is not cardboard is what I have come to believe is at the center of the Christian message, though conventional Christianity rarely puts it front and center: God does not watch our lives from outside. He inhabits every person and experiences each life from within. *"For in him we live, and move, and have our being"* **(Acts 17:28)**. *"In all their affliction he was afflicted"* **(Isaiah 63:9)**.

If that is true, then nothing here is a dream without consequence. Every cruelty is felt by the One who lives inside the person who suffers it, and inside the person who commits it. That is why Jesus could say, *"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me"* **(Matthew 25:40)**, and why Paul warns us, *"grieve not the holy Spirit of God"* **(Ephesians 4:30)**. God's law is not the arbitrary scorekeeping of a "bad god." It describes how to live without wounding the One who feels everything we do. Judgment is not an outside accountant reviewing our ledger; it is the truth about what our choices did to Him, brought into the light. I have written more fully about this in the [Premise Change handout](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/10/31/260725-premise-change-handout/) and in [Reconciliation of Man with the Father](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/07/25/260725-reconciliation-of-man-with-the-father/).

Alanis felt the love and the union of the other side, and she felt the pain and density of this side. What she did not see is that the same God is present in both, and that the pain of this side is also His. That changes her conclusion. The right response is not "don't give this life too much weight." It is: give this life its true weight, because God is living it with you, and carry the love you glimpsed there into every choice you make here.

## Why Her Testimony Carries Weight

Skeptics will say the whole thing was a fainting brain producing a dream. I do not think that holds up, and here is why.

She was not primed for it. She did not believe in God or an afterlife, and she was actively guarding against anything that might alter her mind. She expected nothing from an exercise she considered a mild relaxation technique; she suspected she had been taken for a fool.

The experience was more lucid than waking life, not less. A brain deprived of blood for seconds produces confusion. She reports order, clarity, identity, and an overwhelming sense of being more real than ever, and she remembers the return in sequence: the crushing, the flood of negative emotion, the erasing of images, the reloading of her identity.

It matches a large body of independent testimony. Her accounts of light, color, sound, the absence of memory of this life, the people with luminous faces, the reluctance to return, and the lasting conviction afterward are the same features reported by thousands of people across cultures and belief systems, many of whom, like her, had never read about such things beforehand.

It changed her. It changed her inner voice for more than a year, changed how she faced her father's death, and moved a convinced materialist to say that she now understands we are a body inhabited by a consciousness. Years later she chose to tell it publicly, despite her fear of how people like her former self would hear it.

## A Caution

Please do not try to reproduce this. Inducing a faint by snapping the head back and bearing down is not a spiritual practice; it is a way to stop your heart for a few seconds and fall hard onto whatever is beneath you. People with heart rhythm problems, carotid disease, or a history of fainting can be seriously injured or killed. Alanis did not seek this experience, and God does not need us to manufacture a near-death to reveal Himself. He has already spoken in His Son, and He meets us in prayer, in Scripture, and in the love we show one another.
