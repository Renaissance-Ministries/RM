## Key Decision: Don't Build Own DME — Use Existing Dealers

The group reversed course on becoming their own DME supplier. The math doesn't work yet:
- Own DME is California-only (not nationwide as previously assumed)
- 9–12 months of paying rent + wages before first billable claim ($45–60K sunk)
- Facility must be fully operational before even applying (CMS eliminated grace period Jan 2026)
- Break-even requires ~30 units/month sustained — current volume is zero
- **Decision: sell through existing DME dealers (Rifton's model), apply for own DME later when volume justifies it**

## Pricing Clarity

**Wholesale to DMEs does NOT cap future billing.** Trade prices to dealers are not "usual and customary" charges. Direct-to-consumer cash sales ARE what set the floor. So:
- Wholesale freely to DME dealers at any price — no billing consequences
- Sell to institutions and schools — no billing consequences
- Be deliberate about direct-to-family pricing — that's the one that follows you

**Working economics through other DMEs:**
- MSRP $12,000 → Medicare pays 75% = $9,000 → DME takes 30% = $2,700 → Ideomotion gets $6,300 → minus $3,500 COGS = **$2,800/unit profit**
- At 15 units/month: $42,000 revenue, minus $16K payroll = ~$26,000 net margin

## Competitor Intel — Rifton's Model Confirmed

Rifton sells through 4 named DME dealers (Adaptive Mall, SpinLife, Rehabmart, Medical eShop) alongside internal direct sales. They do NOT own a DME supplier. They publish LMN templates and funding guides and let local dealers handle state-specific insurance machinery.

**Key finding:** Rifton reimburses at $3,000–$5,000. Biodex does NOT reimburse at $12,900. Ideomotion at $12,000–$16,000 trying to reimburse is a combination neither competitor does.

Rifton uses caregiver injury argument inside medical necessity letters: "the device has the potential of saving future medical costs of caregivers injuring their backs during lifting and transfers."
