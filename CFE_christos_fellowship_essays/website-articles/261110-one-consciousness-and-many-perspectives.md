# 261110 - One Consciousness and Many Perspectives

# One Consciousness, Many Perspectives

*By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 11 November 2026*

One consciousness looks at the world from countless perspectives. I believe that is true, and I believe the Bible says it, but the Bible also says something the mystics leave out: the one who looks is a Person, the perspectives are real persons He made, and there is a right way for them to live together.

## A sentence I had been trying to write for forty years

Today a friend showed me a short piece he had posted online. Over a few photographs, he wrote a poem: individuality may be real at one level and not at another, like waves on the ocean: each wave is its own, yet all are the same water. Perhaps, life is not many separate consciousnesses having experiences, but one consciousness experiencing reality from billions of points of view.

I told him that concept/idea/realization/truth was also the center of my writing. He said in a few lines what I'd spent millions of words circling. He told me the insight came to him in an altered state, and so did mine, in March 1987. That did not surprise me. People who reach that place keep reporting the same thing. When many witnesses who have never compared notes describe the same landscape, it is reasonable to think the landscape is there.

## Where the mystic picture stops

I walked the Eastern, mystical road for years. Its central insight is true, but it stops there. Its whole program is to realize the oneness, and when you have realized it, you are left asking: now what? How should I treat my wife? How should I raise my children? What should a town or a nation require of its people? The mystic answer tends to dissolve the questions rather than answer them, because if the individual is finally an illusion, what is the purpose of life and the creation? Why should I treat people well, and how should I act in life? Every religion has a moral code. Buddhism, for example, teaches that insight into enlightenment and the end of the cycle of births and deaths can be reached only through a moral life. In my study of world religions, I have seen that almost all spiritual paths teach roughly the same moral standards, which is where the confusion lies, because each path ends in a very different goal, purpose, and God. Which path is the true path, if they are all very different?

That was not satisfying to me. It did not solve what I was trying to solve.

It was this confusion that kept me on the path of the spiritual seeker for over a decade as a formally declared member of a non-Christian religion or seeker. My search was over two decades long if you count the confusion of my adolescence after my strong Christian commitment and Bible study in childhood. None of the religions I studied had a coherent explanation of how to connect the spiritual teachings with the physical world. The altered state experience of 1987 gave me an insight, a new perspective. From that insight came my counseling, doctoring, philosophy, politics, and conviction in the truth of the Bible. My presidential campaign is an effort to teach the broader public, on the most public stage, the principles and insights I have learned through 40 years of commitment to living with Jesus Christ as the Lord of my life.

## What the Bible adds

Scripture affirms the oneness without dissolving the persons. Paul told the philosophers in Athens that God is the one in whom *"we live, and move, and have our being"* **(Acts 17:28)**. He wrote that there is *"One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all"* **(Ephesians 4:6)**, and of Christ that *"he is before all things, and by him all things consist"* **(Colossians 1:17)**. The whole creation is held together in one living consciousness. That is the wave and the water.

But the Bible never says the waves are the ocean. We are made, not the Maker. Each of us is a real perspective, given a real will, held responsible for real choices. Paul's picture is a body: *"For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ"* **(1 Corinthians 12:12)**. One body; many members; each member truly itself, and each meant to serve the others.

This is how I understand creation in the physics I have developed. God made points of consciousness and organized them. Everything that exists is those points in relationship. Unity is God's, and individuality is His gift.

Once persons are real, the moral law follows. If my neighbor is another perspective through which the one God is looking, then how I treat him is how I treat what God sees and loves. *"Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself"* **(Matthew 22:39)** stops being an arbitrary rule. It describes reality: my neighbor and I share the same source, and harm done to him is harm done within the one body. The mystic sees the oneness. The Bible tells us what to do about it.

## Why the books would fill the world

The last verse of John's Gospel says: *"And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written"* **(John 21:25)**.

I read that as more than a count of miracles. If God looks at His creation from every perspective at once, the perspectives are without number, and every life is one of them. To write them all down would fill the world. That is why I can write millions of words and still be saying one thing: I am describing the same reality from as many angles as I can reach.

A short poem cannot carry all of that, and neither can a long book. No one, myself included, can put the experience of standing before that reality fully into words. But the reader feels the search behind the words. If a sentence points someone toward the One who holds all things together, and then toward the neighbor He holds together with us, it has done its work.
