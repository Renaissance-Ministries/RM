## Key Discussions

### Charlie's Two-Tier Email
Charlie proposed two versions of the LiftWalker (basic non-disassemblable vs deluxe with accessories). Thomas's concern: cosmetic differentiation (colors, accessories) isn't enough — it looks like the same product at two prices, which creates audit risk. Thomas suggested the real differentiator should be structural (e.g., hydraulic vs motorized lift).

### Making the LiftWalker Shippable
Thomas proposed a rod-clamp assembly system so the LiftWalker can ship disassembled and be assembled once by the customer ("some assembly required" — not designed for repeated breakdown like the Traveler). This would solve the storage and shipping cost problems that make the LiftWalker expensive to sell at $6–7k.

**Action:** Isak to talk to Charlie and Leonard about maximum disassembly possible with the current LiftWalker frame design.

### Walker vs Transfer Device (Regulatory)
Thomas sees the product primarily as a transfer device (especially toileting). Isak laid out the regulatory implications:
- **Walker (ITJ):** minimal GMP burden, current classification
- **Transfer/patient lift (FSA):** 20–35 documented procedures, full quality system
- **Key insight from competitors:** Biodex threads the needle — markets caregiver safety benefits ("no patient lifting required") while classifying as a powered exerciser (BXB), not a patient lift. Rifton collapsed their dedicated transfer device (TRAM) into the E-Pacer gait trainer line in June 2025 — moving toward walker positioning.
- **Recommendation holds:** classify as walker, market transfer benefits in demos/videos without making it the regulatory identity.

### Competitor Pricing Reality Check
All competitors price between $5,670 and $12,878. At $16,000, the Traveler is the most expensive device in the category. Thomas raised serious concern about doctor willingness to prescribe at that price point when alternatives exist.

**DME economics at different MSRPs:**
| MSRP | Medicaid pays (75%) | DME keeps (30%) | Ideomotion gets | Minus COGS ($3,500) | Profit |

### The Core Sales Problem
Thomas articulated the fundamental challenge: "We're talking about creating a new medical paradigm. Nobody believes that someone in a wheelchair could learn to walk. You see a person in a wheelchair, you think 'that person can't walk.' I would never think otherwise."

Charlie's sales approach (therapy demonstrations showing wheelchair users they have muscle strength) works in person but is hard to scale. Competitors are well-established with documentation, supply pipelines, advertising, and staffing. "We have a dream. That's what we got."
