## Summary

Isak called Quality Team Home Care (a DME provider) to explore the process for selling Ideomotion's lifting walker through DME channels rather than directly to clinics.

**Key takeaways from the rep:**

1. **Billing codes are a prerequisite.** Without HCPCS/billing codes, the product has no application in their business. With codes, they'd be willing to talk.
2. **Reimbursement will likely be very low.** The rep estimated insurance would max out around **$1,000–$1,400** for a device like this — far below the $10,000–$12,000 price point. He's seen products where it costs him twice as much to buy as what insurance pays.
3. **Insurance will classify it narrowly.** Despite being a hybrid walker + patient lift, the rep expects insurers will categorize it as a standard patient lift and price accordingly. Dual-function billing (lift + walker codes stacked) is unlikely to close the gap.
4. **SADMERC system** is the reference for looking up allowable reimbursement amounts by code.
5. **Rifton E-Pacer** was acknowledged as a comparable product, but the rep has never sold Rifton and noted it's generally pediatric, not adult.
6. **Door is open** — the rep said to call back once codes and reimbursement numbers are in hand.

**Bottom line:** This confirms the concern flagged in earlier meetings — insurance reimbursement for this category of device is an order of magnitude below the Ideomotion price point. The DME channel likely cannot be the primary revenue path unless reimbursement codes are more favorable than expected.
