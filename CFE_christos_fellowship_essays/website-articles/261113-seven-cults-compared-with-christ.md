# 261113 - Seven Cults Compared with Christ

# Seven Groups Called Cults, Measured Against Christ

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND · Nov 13, 2026

## Introduction

One of those internet lists crossed my desk this week: "cults you have probably never heard of." It named seven groups: the Plymouth Brethren Christian Church, Scientology, the Unification Church, the Aetherius Society, the Church Universal and Triumphant, the LaRouche movement, and the Fundamentalist Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The list was written for amusement. Prayer batteries and fallout shelters make good copy. But each of these groups holds real people, and each, in its own way, answers the same questions every religion answers: Who is God? What is wrong with us? Who can fix it? In this essay, I measure each one against Christ, the same way I have been measuring the world's religions in my series on what happens after death.

### What I mean by "cult"

The word *cult* is used in two different senses, and the list mixes them. The first is **theological**: a group that claims the Christian name or the Christian Scriptures while denying something essential about who Jesus is or how a person is saved. The second is **sociological**: a group, religious or not, that gathers around a leader whose word cannot be questioned, controls its members' contact with the outside world, and punishes those who leave. A group can be a cult in one sense and not the other. A church can be orthodox on paper and controlling in practice; a movement can be heretical but leave its members free to walk away.

I will use both measures. The first asks whether a group tells the truth about God. The second asks whether it treats people the way God treats them. Jesus gave us both tests in one sentence: *"Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.* *Ye shall know them by their fruits"* **(Matthew 7:15-16)**. The doctrine is the claim. The fruit is the evidence.

One caution before I begin. The word *cult* is often thrown at any group the speaker dislikes, and the groups on this list all reject it. Their denials are the testimony of interested parties, and so are the accounts of their angriest former members. I have tried to weigh both by the kind of evidence each offers: court records and a group's own published teaching count for more than rumor, and many consistent testimonies count for more than one.

### The standard and the method

My standard is the Bible, which I believe is God's own testimony about Himself and about us. My method is Paul's on Mars Hill. He did not open by condemning the Athenians. He found an altar *"TO THE UNKNOWN GOD"* **(Acts 17:23)** and began with the truth they already held. Only then did he tell them what they lacked: that God *"hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained"* **(Acts 17:31)**.

So for each group I ask three questions. **What does it see truly? Where does it go wrong? How can a Christian speak with its followers?** For the LaRouche movement, which is political rather than religious, the questions bend a little, and I will say how when I get there.

### The premise that runs underneath

One teaching underpins every comparison, because it is the heart of my ministry and where I differ most from ordinary Christian teaching. I believe God does not watch our lives from a distance. He lives them with us, from the inside. *"In him we live, and move, and have our being"* **(Acts 17:28)**. In a real sense, every person's experience is God's experience of the world. That is why His law is not arbitrary: He keeps us from sin because He feels what sin does, in us and in those we wound. And it is why the Cross was necessary. A God who lives inside every life cannot simply wave away the harm done in those lives; He bore it Himself, in the flesh, and paid for it.

This premise is a sharp tool for examining cults. Almost every group on this list puts a man, or a hierarchy of masters, or an organization, between the soul and God. If God already lives in each person, no one needs a human gatekeeper to reach Him. The one Mediator is Christ **(1 Timothy 2:5)**, and He did not come to stand between the Father and us but to bring us home to Him.

I covered Scientology's teaching on death and rebirth in Part 6 of my afterlife series. Here I take it up again from the other side: not what it teaches about the next life, but what it does to people in this one.

## 1. The Plymouth Brethren Christian Church

**What it teaches.** The Brethren movement began in the late 1820s among Christians in Dublin and Plymouth who wanted to meet simply around the Lord's Table, without clergy or denominational walls. John Nelson Darby was its leading teacher. In 1848, the movement split into the Open Brethren, who stayed loosely connected, and the Exclusive Brethren, who held that fellowship must be guarded by strict discipline. The Plymouth Brethren Christian Church (PBCC) is the best-known Exclusive branch, sometimes called the Raven-Taylor-Hales Brethren after its successive leaders. It reports about 54,000 members in some eighteen countries and is led today by Bruce Hales, a Sydney businessman.

The PBCC is not a fringe theology. It holds the Bible as Scripture, preaches salvation by grace through Christ, and keeps the Lord's Supper weekly. What sets it apart is its **doctrine of separation**: the teaching that unity among believers is kept by separating from evil, and that the world outside the fellowship is evil. Members do business with outsiders but do not eat with them. They have historically avoided television, radio, and university, and they do not vote. The list I was sent says members may have no interaction with outsiders at all. That overstates it; the line is drawn at the table, not at the shop counter.

The church disciplines members by "shutting up" (a temporary separation within the fellowship) and "withdrawing from" (excommunication). Many former members describe families split for decades when one member left or was put out, and children who will not speak to their parents. The church says it does not stop families from contacting those who leave. On this point I weigh the evidence this way: the accounts are many, consistent, and come from several countries over fifty years. I take them as substantially true, while recognizing that each is the testimony of a wounded party.

There is also a doctrinal fracture that most people outside the Brethren have never heard of. In 1929, the American leader James Taylor Sr. began teaching that Christ became the Son only at His incarnation, and was not the eternal Son before it. The Brethren who refused this teaching broke away. The PBCC is descended from those who accepted it.

**What it sees truly.** The Brethren see that holiness is not optional. *"Let every one that nameth the name of Christ depart from iniquity"* **(2 Timothy 2:19)**. They are right that a Christian is not to be shaped by the world's entertainment, ambition, and appetites: *"be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind"* **(Romans 12:2)**. They are right to center their worship on the Lord's Supper, as the early church did. And they care for their own and for strangers in disaster; their Rapid Relief Team feeds emergency workers in many countries. Much of what an outsider sees in the Brethren is simply old-fashioned Christian discipline, and I respect it.

**Where it goes wrong.** The first error is that the Brethren separate from *sinners* where Scripture tells us to separate from *sin*. Paul anticipated exactly this mistake. He told the Corinthians not to keep company with a professing brother living in open sin, and then added, *"Yet not altogether with the fornicators of this world, or with the covetous, or extortioners, or with idolaters; for then must ye needs go out of the world"* **(1 Corinthians 5:10)**. Jesus Himself was accused of the very thing the Brethren forbid: *"This man receiveth sinners, and eateth with them"* **(Luke 15:2)**. He did not deny it. He told three parables to defend it.

This is where my premise bites hardest. If God lives inside the experience of the neighbor next door, then the table that neighbor sets is a table God sits at. To refuse it on principle is to refuse the very people Christ came to seek, and in whom He already dwells as the hidden Guest. Separation from evil is a duty. Separation from people for whom Christ died is a wall the Gospel came to tear down **(Ephesians 2:14)**.

The second error is how we treat families. *"If any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel"* **(1 Timothy 5:8)**. A discipline that leaves a mother unable to speak to her son has stopped being church discipline and become punishment. Paul instructed us to treat a disciplined brother as a brother, not an enemy: *"count him not as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother"* **(2 Thessalonians 3:15)**.

The third error is the authority given to one man. Jesus told His disciples, *"call no man your father upon the earth: for one is your Father, which is in heaven"* **(Matthew 23:9)**. When a single leader can decide who remains in fellowship with God's people worldwide, he has taken a place Christ reserved for Himself.

The fourth is the denial of the eternal Sonship. Jesus prayed, *"glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was"* **(John 17:5)**. John wrote that God *"sent his only begotten Son into the world"* **(1 John 4:9)**; He was the Son before He was sent. This is not a quibble. If the Father and the Son were not Father and Son from eternity, then love did not exist within God before creation, and God needed the world in order to love. The eternal Sonship is what makes the statement *"God is love"* **(1 John 4:8)** true of God in Himself.

**How to talk with them.** A Brethren member who trusts in Christ is my brother, and *witnessing* is the wrong word. The right conversation starts with the verse they love most, 2 Timothy 2:19, and reads on to verse 24: *"the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient."* *All men* includes the neighbor one will not eat with. I would ask a Brethren friend, gently, how he reads 1 Corinthians 5:10, and whether Jesus at Levi's table **(Luke 5:29-32)** was defiled or doing His Father's work.

For those who have left, often with nothing but their faith and a broken family, a Christian's job is simpler: open the door, set the table, and show them that the God they were taught to fear is the Father who ran down the road to meet His son **(Luke 15:20)**.

## 2. Scientology

**What it teaches.** L. Ron Hubbard, a science-fiction writer, published *Dianetics* in 1950, and the first Church of Scientology was founded in Los Angeles in 1954. Scientology teaches that each person is a *thetan*, an immortal spirit who has lived many lives and is weighed down by painful mental images called *engrams*. In *auditing*, a trained auditor questions the member, often using an electrical meter (E-meter, which measures galvanic skin response, or GSR), to bring those memories to light and discharge them. The list says auditing is meant to uncover past lives. That is part of it, but the larger aim is to clear any buried trauma, from this life or earlier ones, on the way to the states Scientology calls *Clear* and *Operating Thetan*.

The list is right that Scientology opposes psychiatry. It treats psychiatric drugs and diagnoses as harmful and campaigns against them through an affiliated group. It does not reject all counseling; it offers its own and charges a great deal for it.

Two practices matter most for this essay. The first is **disconnection**: a member may be required to cut off a family member or friend the church considers hostile. The second is how it treats critics and those who leave. Many former members, journalists, and some court proceedings over several decades describe surveillance, harassment, and high-pressure fundraising. The church denies these accounts and calls its critics apostates. As with the Brethren, I weigh the volume and consistency of the testimony and conclude that the pattern is real.

**What it sees truly.** I covered Scientology's view of the spirit in Part 6 of my afterlife series, and I will not repeat it all here. In short: it sees that a person is more than his body, that old wounds shape present choices, and that people long to be free. Each of those is true, and Scripture says so.

It also sees, in its opposition to psychiatry, a real danger that many in the healing professions have noted. Much of modern psychiatry treats the soul as a set of chemicals and the troubled person as a brain to be adjusted. As a naturopathic physician, I share some of that concern. Medication has its place, but a culture that medicates every sorrow has forgotten that some suffering is spiritual and calls for repentance, forgiveness, and love, not only a prescription.

**Where it goes wrong.** I named the doctrinal errors in Part 6: no personal God at the center, sin recast as trauma, salvation sold by the course, and the endless wheel of rebirth that Scripture denies **(Hebrews 9:27)**. Here I add what Scientology does to people in this life.

Disconnection inverts the Gospel. The Christian discipline that comes closest to it, removing an unrepentant member from fellowship, has one purpose: restoration **(Galatians 6:1)**. It never asks a son to stop speaking to his mother because she said something unkind about the church. And treating those who leave as enemies to be watched and silenced is the fruit Jesus told us to look for. *"By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another"* **(John 13:35)**. Love does not keep files on the people who walked away.

Under my premise, the deepest error is the price tag. If God lives in every person's experience, He is already present in the wounded soul before anyone buys a course. Healing does not have to be purchased level by level; it begins the moment a person turns toward the One who has been with him all along. *"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest"* **(Matthew 11:28)**. Christ did not post a fee schedule.

**How to witness.** Many Scientologists joined because they wanted to stop hurting and to help others. Many who leave have lost money, years, and family. Come to both with patience. The bridge is the word Scientology uses most: *freedom*. I would ask, *How much has your freedom cost you so far, and how much more will it cost?* Then I would tell him about the freedom already paid for: *"If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed"*** (John 8:36)**. And to the one disconnected from family, I would add that the Father does not disconnect. He waits at the end of the road.

## 3. The Unification Church (Family Federation)

**What it teaches.** Sun Myung Moon founded the Holy Spirit Association for the Unification of World Christianity in Seoul in 1954. In the 1990s it was renamed the Family Federation for World Peace and Unification, though most people still call it the Unification Church. Its scripture alongside the Bible is Moon's *Divine Principle*.

The *Divine Principle* retells the Fall. It teaches that Eve was seduced by Lucifer in a sexual sense and then joined with Adam before God's time, so that mankind was born into Satan's lineage rather than God's. God's plan was for Jesus to restore that lineage by marrying and founding a sinless family. Because Jesus was crucified before He could marry, the teaching says, He accomplished only *spiritual* salvation; *physical* salvation, the restoration of the family line, was left undone. A second messiah, the Lord of the Second Advent, would have to complete it. In 1992 Moon publicly declared that he and his wife, Hak Ja Han, were that messiah, the "True Parents" of humanity. Members enter God's restored lineage through the Blessing, the church's well-known mass weddings, and work off the debt of past sin through *indemnity*, sacrificial conditions they perform themselves.

Moon died in 2012, and Hak Ja Han has led the movement since. In 2026 the movement suffered two heavy blows. Japan's courts, acting on years of civil judgments over coercive fundraising, ordered its Japanese corporation dissolved; the Supreme Court made that final in June. And in August a Seoul court sentenced Han to two years in prison for bribery and illegal political funding tied to former President Yoon's government. The church is appealing and calls both cases persecution. Defenders of religious liberty have raised serious objections to the Japanese ruling, which rested on civil wrongs rather than any criminal conviction of the church. I share some of that concern for liberty. But the Seoul conviction is a criminal judgment on the leader's own conduct, and the Japanese civil record of families pressured into ruinous donations stretches back decades.

**What it sees truly.** The Unification Church sees that the family is at the center of God's purpose. It is right. God's first command was *"Be fruitful, and multiply"* **(Genesis 1:28)**, and the first institution He made was a marriage. It sees that sin is not only an individual matter but something passed down through generations, which Scripture also teaches **(Romans 5:12)**. It sees that the Kingdom of God is meant to be realized on earth, not only in heaven: *"Thy kingdom come.* *Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven"* **(Matthew 6:10)**. And it has always opposed atheistic communism, which has done enormous harm to the souls and bodies of millions.

**Where it goes wrong.** The central error is the claim that Jesus failed. Scripture says the Cross was not an accident that interrupted God's plan; it *was* the plan. Jesus was *"delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God"* **(Acts 2:23)**. He did not leave His work half done. His last word on the cross was *"It is finished"* **(John 19:30)**. And Scripture explicitly says that His one sacrifice accomplished everything: *"by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified"* **(Hebrews 10:14)**. There is no remainder for a second messiah to complete.

This is exactly where my premise matters. I have argued that the Cross was necessary because God lives in every person's experience and therefore feels every sin; the debt is real, and only He could pay it. If the Cross was a failure forced on God by unbelief, then the debt was never paid by God at all, and the Unification answer is that we must pay the remainder ourselves, through indemnity. That is the old religion of works in new clothing. The Gospel's answer is that the debt was paid in full, by the only One who could feel all of it.

The second error is the man himself. Jesus warned, *"if any man shall say unto you, Lo, here is Christ, or there; believe it not"* **(Matthew 24:23)**. When He returns, no one will need to be told: *"as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be"* **(Matthew 24:27)**. A messiah who must be identified by his own followers, and who is later convicted in court for buying political favor, has failed the test of fruit.

The third error concerns the Fall. Scripture places the first sin in disobedience, not in a sexual union with an angel: Eve *"took of the fruit thereof, and did eat"* **(Genesis 3:6)**. Recasting the Fall as a corruption of bloodline makes salvation a matter of lineage and marriage arrangement, administered by the True Parents. Scripture makes it a matter of faith in the Son: *"as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God"* **(John 1:12)**, born *"not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh"* **(John 1:13)**.

**How to witness.** Unificationists already read the Gospels and love Jesus as they understand Him. The bridge is the family. I would agree with a Unificationist that God's heart is a Father's heart and that He longs for a restored family. Then I would ask, *Is God's family made by a Blessing ceremony, or by adoption through His Son?* Paul answers: *"ye have received the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father"* **(Romans 8:15)**. The True Parent of humanity is the Father Himself, and the family He is building is open to anyone who comes to Him through Christ, already complete, already paid for, with no indemnity owed.

## 4. The Aetherius Society

**What it teaches.** George King, a London taxi driver who practiced yoga, said that in 1954 he began receiving telepathic messages from a being on Venus called Aetherius. He founded the Aetherius Society in 1955. The society teaches that highly evolved beings it calls *Cosmic Masters* live on other planets and have come to Earth from time to time as great teachers. In its teaching, Jesus and the Buddha came from Venus, and Krishna from Saturn. King said he met "Master Jesus" in person on Holdstone Down in Devon in 1958 and received from him a set of teachings called *The Twelve Blessings*. The society holds to karma and reincarnation, practices mantra and yoga, and awaits a coming "Next Master" who will arrive openly from space.

The list is accurate about Operation Prayer Power, begun in 1973. Members pray and chant into specially built containers they call *spiritual energy batteries*. The stored energy is later released, under the guidance of the Masters, toward wars, earthquakes, and other disasters. The society credits these releases with easing several catastrophes. King died in 1997, and the society now honors him as a Master himself.

**What it sees truly.** The Aetherius Society sees that prayer is real and does real work in the world. Scripture agrees: *"The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much"* **(James 5:16)**. It sees that prayer should be offered for the world's suffering, not only for oneself, which is the spirit of Paul's command that *"supplications, prayers, intercessions, and giving of thanks, be made for all men"* **(1 Timothy 2:1)**. It sees that we share the universe with intelligent beings greater than ourselves, as the Bible does in its teaching on angels **(Hebrews 1:14)**.

The prayer battery is less absurd than the list makes it sound. Scripture itself pictures prayer as something gathered and kept. In John's vision the elders hold *"golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints"* **(Revelation 5:8)**. Then an angel offers that incense before the throne, fills a censer with fire from the altar, *"and cast it into the earth"* **(Revelation 8:5)**. Prayers are stored before God and, in His time, poured out on the world. The Aetherius Society has sensed something true about the weight and permanence of prayer.

**Where it goes wrong.** The difference is in who keeps the prayers and who decides what to do with them. In Revelation, the prayers are kept before the throne of God, and He acts on them. In the Aetherius Society, they are kept in a box, and the Masters, with the society's technicians, decide where to direct them. Prayer becomes an impersonal energy to be managed rather than a child speaking to his Father.

My premise sharpens the point. God does not need a battery to receive our prayers, because He is already inside the one who prays. *"The Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered"* **(Romans 8:26)**. Every prayer is heard as it is prayed, by the One who lives in the person praying. It does not have to be stored, transmitted, or directed by intermediaries.

The deeper error is what the society does to Jesus. It makes Him one Master among many, from one planet among many. But Jesus said, *"no man hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man"* **(John 3:13)**. He did not come from Venus; He came from the Father. And the source of the society's teaching is the problem Scripture warns about most plainly. Messages from beings who speak through a medium are exactly what God forbade: *"There shall not be found among you... a consulter with familiar spirits"* **(Deuteronomy 18:10-11)**. Paul went further: *"though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed"* **(Galatians 1:8)**. A being who brings a new gospel is not made trustworthy by being impressive, *"for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light"* **(2 Corinthians 11:14)**.

Finally, karma and rebirth replace grace. A soul working off its debts across many lives has no need of a Savior who paid them once **(Hebrews 9:27-28)**.

**How to witness.** Members of the Aetherius Society are usually sincere, peaceable people who want to help the world. Start where they are: agree that prayer matters, and show them Revelation 5 and 8. Then ask the question that decides everything: *Who receives the prayer?* If it is a Master on another planet, the Christian has a better offer, the Father who hears in secret **(Matthew 6:6)**. And John gave a test any seeker can apply to a spirit that claims to bring a message: *"Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God"* **(1 John 4:2)**. I would ask an Aetherian friend whether the Master Jesus of Holdstone Down confesses that He came in the flesh, died for sins, and rose bodily from the grave. If not, he is not the Jesus of the Gospels.

## 5. The Church Universal and Triumphant

**What it teaches.** This one is close to home. The Church Universal and Triumphant (CUT) still keeps its headquarters at the Royal Teton Ranch near Gardiner, in Montana's Paradise Valley, a few hours from where I write.

Mark L. Prophet founded the Summit Lighthouse in 1958, teaching that he was a messenger for the *Ascended Masters*: great souls of the past, such as Saint Germain, the Buddha, and Jesus, who have completed their earthly lives and now guide humanity from higher realms. The teaching descends from the I AM movement of the 1930s. After Mark's death in 1973, his wife, Elizabeth Clare Prophet, announced that he had become an Ascended Master himself and organized the movement as the Church Universal and Triumphant. She moved it to Montana in 1986.

CUT presents Jesus as an Ascended Master who fully realized his inner divinity and ascended at death. It teaches that every person has a *Christ Self*, a higher self that each of us can come to embody, and that Jesus was the great example of doing so. It also teaches that Jesus spent his missing years, from twelve to thirty, studying in India and Tibet. Its members practice *decrees*, rapid spoken prayers that call on the "violet flame" to transmute bad karma, and it teaches reincarnation, which the soul escapes through ascension.

In the late 1980s, Elizabeth Prophet warned of coming nuclear war, and members built and stocked fallout shelters on church land. On the night of March 15, 1990, hundreds, perhaps thousands, of members entered them. Some had quit their jobs and gone into debt. No attack came. Prophet had taught that the members' decrees could help avert catastrophe, and the movement took that view afterward. Membership fell sharply in the years that followed, and much of the land was sold. Prophet died in 2009, and a smaller church continues in the valley.

**What it sees truly.** CUT sees that God intends to dwell in us, not only above us. Paul calls this *"the mystery which hath been hid from ages and from generations...* *Christ in you, the hope of glory"* **(Colossians 1:26-27)**. Of all the groups on this list, CUT comes closest to the teaching at the heart of my ministry, that God lives in our experience from the inside. That is exactly why I must be careful to show where the two part ways.

CUT also sees that a prudent person prepares for danger. *"A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself"* **(Proverbs 22:3)**. And it sees that prayer can turn judgment aside, as Nineveh's repentance did **(Jonah 3:10)**.

**Where it goes wrong.** The first error is the Christ Self. CUT separates "the Christ" from the man Jesus and makes it a state of consciousness any soul may attain. Scripture never separates them. *"Who is a liar but he that denieth that Jesus is the Christ?"* **(1 John 2:22)**. The Christ is not a level of awareness; He is a Person who was born, died and rose.

Here is where my premise and CUT's teaching differ, and the difference is everything. I believe God lives in each person's experience. I do not believe that each person is, or can become, God. The indwelling is His gift and His humility, not our achievement. And because He lives in us, He feels the full weight of our sin, which is why the debt had to be paid by Him on the Cross. CUT's path runs the other way: the soul rises by its own decrees, works off its own karma, and ascends by its own merit. That leaves no place for the Cross, and none is needed in its system. In mine, the Cross is the only way the God who lives in us could forgive what we have done to Him from the inside.

The second error is false prophecy. Moses gave Israel a plain test: *"When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken"* **(Deuteronomy 18:22)**. A prediction that fails, and is then explained as having been averted by the followers' prayers, can never be tested at all.

The third error concerns the Masters themselves. Messages received from spirits through a human messenger are the very practice God forbade **(Deuteronomy 18:10-12)**, and the story of Jesus in India has no historical evidence behind it. The Gospels show Him in Nazareth, known to His neighbors as a local workman: *"Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary?"* **(Mark 6:3)**. Had He spent eighteen years in Tibet, they would not have been so surprised by His wisdom.

**How to witness.** Many people in Montana's Paradise Valley and beyond spent their savings and the best years of their lives preparing for a night that never came. Some stayed; many left disillusioned with faith altogether. A Christian should approach both with gentleness. The bridge is Colossians 1:27, which CUT members will recognize and love. I would tell such a friend: *You are right that Christ wants to live in you.** But He is not a higher version of yourself that you climb toward. He is a Person who came down to you, paid what you owed, and offers Himself freely.* *"Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us"* **(Titus 3:5)**. No decree is needed to earn it.
