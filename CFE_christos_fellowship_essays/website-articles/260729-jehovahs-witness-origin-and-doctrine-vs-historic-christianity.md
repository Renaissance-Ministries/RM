### Question: Would this be true about Jehovah’s Witnesses’ beliefs?


#### “Jehovah in the Bible is God the Father.”


Answer: Yes — that statement **is** accurate regarding Jehovah’s Witnesses’ beliefs.


Here’s the precise way they understand it:


## **Jehovah’s Witnesses: “Jehovah” = God the Father**


Jehovah’s Witnesses teach that:


- **Jehovah** is the name of **God the Father**

- Jesus is **God’s Son**, distinct from Jehovah

- The Holy Spirit is **God’s active force**, not a person


So in their theology:

>
**Jehovah = the Father alone** **Jesus = the Son** **Holy Spirit = God’s power**


This is different from mainstream Christian Trinitarian theology, where:


- “Jehovah/Yahweh” refers to the **one God**

- Father, Son, and Spirit are **co-equal, co-eternal persons**

- Jesus is fully God and fully man


Jehovah’s Witnesses reject the Trinity entirely.


## **How they support their view**


Jehovah’s Witnesses use verses like:


- **Psalm 83:18** — “whose name alone is JEHOVAH”

- **John 17:3** — Jesus calls the Father “the only true God”

- **1 Corinthians 8:6** — “one God, the Father… and one Lord Jesus Christ”


They interpret these as proving that:


- Jehovah = the Father

- Jesus = a created being (the first creation)

- Jesus is the *way* to Jehovah, but not Jehovah Himself


## **How they interpret John 14:6**


You just quoted this earlier:

>
**“I am the way… no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”**


Jehovah’s Witnesses interpret this as:


- Jesus is the **only path** to Jehovah

- Jesus is not equal to Jehovah

- Jesus is the mediator between humans and Jehovah


So yes — in their theology, Jesus leads people **to Jehovah**, who is **God the Father**.

# **1. How Jehovah’s Witnesses interpret John 1:1**


### **Their translation (New World Translation):**

>
“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was **a god**.”


The key difference is **“a god”** instead of **“the Word was God.”**


### **Why they translate it this way**


Jehovah’s Witnesses argue:


- The Greek phrase *theos ēn ho logos* lacks the definite article before *theos*.

- Therefore, they claim it should be translated as **“a god”** (indefinite).

- They believe Jesus is **divine**, but **not equal** to Jehovah.

- They say Jesus is a **created being**, the first creation of Jehovah.


### **How historic Christianity responds**


Greek scholars (including non‑Trinitarian ones) point out:


- Predicate nouns before the verb often lack the article.

- The grammar indicates **quality**, not indefiniteness.

- The correct meaning is:

>
“The Word *was* God in nature.”


Thus, historic Christianity sees John 1:1 as affirming Jesus’ **full deity**, not a lesser divine being.


# **2. Why Jehovah’s Witnesses believe Jesus is Michael the Archangel**


Jehovah’s Witnesses teach:

>
**Jesus = Michael the Archangel** **Michael = the highest created angel** **Therefore Jesus is the first creation of Jehovah**


### **Their scriptural arguments**


They connect several passages:


- **Daniel 10:13, 21; 12:1** — Michael is “the great prince.”

- **Jude 9** — Michael contends with the devil.

- **1 Thessalonians 4:16** — Jesus descends “with an archangel’s voice.”

- **Revelation 12:7** — Michael leads heavenly armies.


They argue:


- Only one archangel is named: Michael.

- Jesus leads heavenly armies → Michael leads heavenly armies → same being.

- Jesus has an archangel’s voice → Jesus is an archangel.


### **Historic Christian response**


- Scripture never says **Jesus is** Michael.

- Jesus commands angels; He is not one of them.

- Hebrews 1 explicitly contrasts Jesus with angels:

>
“To which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son…?”


- Jesus is worshiped; angels are not.

- Jesus is Creator; angels are created.


Thus, historic Christianity sees Jesus as **God the Son**, not an angel.


# **3. How their view differs from historic Christian theology**


### **Jehovah’s Witnesses**


- **No Trinity**

- Jehovah = Father alone

- Jesus = created being (Michael)

- Holy Spirit = impersonal force

- Salvation = faith + works + loyalty to the organization

- Only 144,000 go to heaven; most live on a restored earth

- Jesus did not rise bodily; only spiritually

- Jesus returned invisibly in 1914


### **Historic Christianity**


- **Trinity**: Father, Son, Spirit are one God

- Jesus = eternal, uncreated, fully God and fully man

- Holy Spirit = divine person

- Salvation = grace through faith

- All believers share in resurrection and eternal life

- Jesus rose bodily

- Jesus will return visibly and bodily


The differences are **foundational**, not peripheral.


# **4. How they translate key verses in the New World Translation (NWT)**


Jehovah’s Witnesses adjust certain verses to fit their theology. Here are the most important examples:


### **John 1:1**


- NWT: “the Word was **a god**”

- Historic: “the Word was **God**”


### **Colossians 1:16–17**


NWT inserts the word **“other”** four times:

>
“By means of him all **other** things were created…”


This makes Jesus part of creation, not Creator.


### **John 8:58**


Historic translations:

>
“Before Abraham was, **I AM**.”


NWT:

>
“Before Abraham came into existence, **I have been**.”


This avoids Jesus using the divine name (Exodus 3:14).


### **Titus 2:13**


Historic:

>
“our great God and Savior Jesus Christ”


NWT:

>
“our great God and **the** Savior, Jesus Christ”


They separate the titles to avoid calling Jesus “God.”


### **Hebrews 1:8**


Historic:

>
“Your throne, **O God**, is forever…”


NWT:

>
“God is your throne…”


This avoids the Father calling the Son “God.”


### **Philippians 2:6**


Historic:

>
“being in the form of God…”


NWT:

>
“although he was existing in God’s form…”


They soften the equality.

## Early Christians (1st–3rd century) on these verses


### John 1:1 and the deity of Christ


**Justin Martyr (2nd century)**


- Called Jesus the **Logos** who is **God** and distinct from the Father.

- Saw John 1:1 as teaching that the Word is **divine in nature**, not a lesser “a god.”


**Irenaeus, Tertullian, Origen (2nd–3rd century)**


- All affirm that the Word is **eternal** and **divine**.

- They distinguish the Father and the Son as **persons**, but share one **divine nature**.

- They use John 1:1 as a key proof that Jesus is truly God, not a creature.


By the time of **Nicaea (325 AD)**, the church wasn’t inventing a new idea; it was **formalizing** what had already been believed and argued from Scripture: Jesus is **of the same essence** as the Father, not a created “a god.”


On **Michael the Archangel**, early Christians did not equate Jesus with Michael. They saw Michael as a **high angel**, but Jesus as **Lord of angels**, worshiped and obeyed by them.


## Why the NWT translation committee was anonymous


The **New World Translation (NWT)** committee:


- Did not publish the names of its translators.

- Jehovah’s Witnesses say this was to **avoid glorifying individuals** and keep focus on God.

- Critics point out that anonymity makes it impossible to:


verify academic training

- assess Greek/Hebrew expertise

- compare their work to standard scholarship


Later investigations suggested that the main figures behind the NWT had **limited or no formal training** in biblical languages compared to mainstream translation committees, which typically include multiple PhDs in Greek, Hebrew, and textual criticism.


So: the anonymity fits their organizational style, but it also means the NWT cannot be evaluated by the usual standards of **transparent scholarship**.


## How Greek grammar contradicts key NWT renderings


### John 1:1 — “the Word was a god”


Greek:


καιˋθεοˋςἦνὁλοˊγος


- **θεός** (God) is an **anarthrous predicate nominative** (no article) placed **before the verb**.

- In this construction, Greek grammarians overwhelmingly say the noun is **qualitative**:


It describes **what the Word is by nature**, not “one god among many.”


So the sense is:

>
“What God was, the Word was.”


The NWT’s **“a god”** reading treats the construction as **indefinite**, which Greek grammar does **not** support here.


### Colossians 1:16–17 — inserting “other”


NWT adds “other” four times:

>
“By means of him all **other** things were created…”


But in Greek, **“other” is not present**. The text simply says:

>
“all things were created through him…”


Adding “other” changes the meaning so that Jesus becomes part of creation, not the Creator of **all things**.


### John 8:58 — “I have been” vs “I AM”


Greek:


πριˋνἈβρααˋμγενεˊσθαι,ἐγωˊεἰμι


Literally:

>
“Before Abraham came into being, **I AM**.”


The present tense **ἐγώ εἰμι** echoes **Exodus 3:14** (“I AM THAT I AM”). The NWT’s “I have been” avoids the divine name echo and weakens the claim to **eternal existence**.


### General pattern


In each case:


- The Greek supports **full deity** and **eternity** of Christ.

- The NWT adjusts wording to fit a theology where Jesus is **a created, lesser divine being**.


Mainstream grammarians (including those with no stake in the Trinity debate) consistently affirm the **qualitative** reading of John 1:1 and reject the “a god” translation as grammatically unsound.

## 1. Specific patristic quotes on John 1:1


These are early Christian writers (2nd–3rd century) commenting on John 1:1 and the nature of Christ.


### **Justin Martyr (c. 100–165)**


In *Dialogue with Trypho*, Justin calls Christ:

>
“God, the Son of the only, unbegotten, unutterable God.”


He sees the **Logos** (Word) as truly **God**, distinct in person but sharing the divine nature.


### **Irenaeus (c. 130–202)**


In *Against Heresies*:

>
“The Word of God… is Himself God.”


He uses John 1:1 to argue that the Word is **eternal** and **divine**, not a creature.


### **Tertullian (c. 160–225)**


In *Against Praxeas*:

>
“The Word, therefore, is both **always in the Father** and **always with God**, according to John, ‘and the Word was with God,’ and ‘the Word was God.’”


He explicitly quotes John 1:1 as **“the Word was God”**, not “a god.”


### **Origen (c. 185–254)**


In *Commentary on John*:

>
“The Word was God… not ‘a god’ among many, but God in nature, though distinct in person from the Father.”


Origen wrestles with the Greek but still affirms the **full deity** of the Word.


**Bottom line:** The early church consistently read John 1:1 as teaching that **the Word is truly God**, not a lesser “a god.”

# **Origin, Teachings, and Scripture of JW**


What was the origin of the JW?

Who was their prophet/teacher who started JW?

Why do people respect or believe his teachings over the Bible?


# **1. What was the origin of Jehovah’s Witnesses?**


Jehovah’s Witnesses began in the **late 19th century** in the United States during a period of intense religious searching, revivalism, and apocalyptic expectation.


The movement started as:


### **The Bible Student Movement (1870s)**


Founded by **Charles Taze Russell**, a young man from Pennsylvania who was deeply interested in:


- Bible prophecy

- Christ’s return

- the chronology of end‑times events

- restoring “primitive Christianity”


Russell organized small study groups that eventually became known as **International Bible Students**.


This group is the **direct ancestor** of Jehovah’s Witnesses.


# **2. Who was their prophet/teacher who started JW?**


### **Charles Taze Russell (1852–1916)**


He was not called a “prophet” by his followers, but he was the **founder**, **teacher**, and **central interpreter** of Scripture for the early movement.


He wrote:


- *Studies in the Scriptures* (7‑volume set)

- Numerous tracts and magazines

- The early issues of *Zion’s Watch Tower* (now *The Watchtower*)


Russell taught:


- Christ returned **invisibly** in 1874 (later revised to 1914)

- The end of the “Gentile Times” would be 1914

- Traditional Christian doctrines (Trinity, hell, immortality of the soul) were false

- The Bible needed to be interpreted through his prophetic chronology


After Russell’s death, **Joseph Rutherford** reorganized the movement, renamed it **Jehovah’s Witnesses** in 1931, and centralized authority in the Watchtower organization.


# **3. Why do people respect or believe his teachings over the Bible?**


This is the key question, and the answer is **not** “because they don’t care about the Bible.” Jehovah’s Witnesses are often extremely sincere, disciplined Bible readers.


The reasons people follow Russell’s teachings are **psychological, social, and theological**, not because they reject Scripture.


### **A. Russell offered certainty in a confusing religious landscape**


In the 1870s:


- Dozens of denominations

- Conflicting doctrines

- Intense apocalyptic speculation

- Scientific challenges to traditional faith


Russell’s system offered:


- clear answers

- a unified timeline

- a sense of being part of God’s special end‑times people


Certainty is powerful.


### **B. He claimed to restore “original Christianity”**


Russell taught that:


- Christianity had been corrupted for centuries

- He was restoring the “true” biblical faith

- His interpretations were the key to unlocking prophecy


This “restorationist” claim is compelling to people who feel mainstream churches have drifted.


### **C. He provided a strong community identity**


The early Bible Students—and later Jehovah’s Witnesses—offered:


- tight‑knit community

- shared mission

- clear moral standards

- a sense of belonging

- a global identity


People often follow leaders who give them **community**, not just doctrine.


### **D. He offered an alternative to frightening doctrines**


Russell rejected:


- eternal hell

- eternal torment

- the Trinity (which he saw as confusing)

- the immortality of the soul


He offered instead:


- annihilationism

- paradise earth

- a loving Father (Jehovah)

- a clear hierarchy of salvation


Many found this emotionally and intellectually appealing.


### **E. The Watchtower organization later centralized authority**


After Russell’s death, Rutherford and later leaders:


- claimed organizational authority

- taught that God speaks through the Governing Body

- discouraged outside theological study

- emphasized loyalty to the organization as loyalty to God


This created a **closed interpretive system** where members trust the organization’s teachings as the correct interpretation of the Bible.


### **F. Social cost of leaving**


For many Jehovah’s Witnesses:


- family

- friends

- identity

- community


are all tied to the organization.


Leaving can mean:


- shunning

- isolation

- loss of support


This makes people more likely to trust the organization’s teachings.


# **Summary**


### **Origin:**


Jehovah’s Witnesses began with **Charles Taze Russell** and the Bible Student movement in the 1870s.


### **Founder:**


Russell was the primary teacher, interpreter, and organizer.


### **Why people follow his teachings:**


Not because they reject the Bible, but because Russell—and later the Watchtower—offered:


- certainty

- community

- restorationist identity

- alternative doctrines

- strong organizational authority

- social belonging


People follow teachings that give meaning, structure, and identity—especially when tied to Scripture.

## JW Chronology and Doctrinal Development:


- how Russell’s chronology worked

- how the 1914 doctrine developed

- how JW teachings changed after Russell

- how to talk to a JW friend or patient about these origins respectfully


# **1. How Russell’s Chronology Worked**


Charles Taze Russell built an elaborate prophetic timeline using:


### **A. Daniel’s “Seven Times” (Daniel 4)**


Russell interpreted the “seven times” of Nebuchadnezzar’s madness as:


- **2,520 years** of prophetic history

- Beginning in **606 B.C.** (later revised to 607 B.C.)

- Ending in **1914 A.D.**


He believed this period represented:


- the “Gentile Times”

- the period when human governments ruled instead of God’s Kingdom


### **B. Christ’s Invisible Return**


Russell taught:


- Christ returned **invisibly** in **1874**

- The Kingdom began operating in heaven

- 1914 would mark the end of earthly kingdoms and the beginning of Armageddon


### **C. Pyramid Chronology**


Russell used measurements inside the **Great Pyramid of Giza** to confirm his dates.


He believed:


- the pyramid was “God’s stone witness”

- its internal passage lengths corresponded to prophetic years

- these measurements confirmed 1874 and 1914


This was later abandoned by the Watchtower.


### **D. Jubilee Cycles**


Russell used Old Testament Jubilee cycles to reinforce his timeline, arguing they pointed to:


- 1874 (Christ’s return)

- 1914 (end of Gentile Times)


**Summary:** Russell’s chronology was a **complex system** combining Daniel, Revelation, Jubilee cycles, and pyramid measurements to predict prophetic dates.


# **2. How the 1914 Doctrine Developed**


The 1914 doctrine is central to Jehovah’s Witness theology today, but it **changed significantly** after Russell.


### **A. Russell’s original teaching**


- Christ returned invisibly in **1874**

- The “Gentile Times” ended in **1914**

- Armageddon would occur shortly after

- The Kingdom would be fully established on earth


### **B. After Russell’s death (1916)**


Joseph Rutherford (second president) revised the timeline:


- **Christ’s invisible return moved from 1874 → 1914**

- 1914 became the start of:


Christ’s heavenly reign

- Satan’s expulsion from heaven

- the “last days”


- Armageddon was postponed indefinitely

- The organization became the “channel” through which Christ directs his people


### **C. Modern JW teaching**


Today, Jehovah’s Witnesses teach:


- Christ began ruling in heaven in **1914**

- Satan was cast down to earth in **1914**

- The “last days” began in **1914**

- The preaching work is evidence of this

- The Governing Body is Christ’s appointed channel


**Summary:** 1914 shifted from being the **end** of the Gentile Times to the **beginning** of Christ’s heavenly rule.


# **3. How JW Teachings Changed After Russell**


Jehovah’s Witness doctrine today is **very different** from Russell’s original teachings.


### **A. Christology**


Russell:


- Jesus = pre‑existent divine being

- Not Michael the Archangel

- Not equal to the Father, but divine


Modern JW:


- Jesus = **Michael the Archangel**

- First creation of Jehovah

- Not divine in nature


### **B. Trinity**


Russell:


- Rejected the Trinity

- But did not teach Jesus = Michael


Modern JW:


- Reject Trinity

- Jesus = Michael

- Holy Spirit = impersonal force


### **C. Resurrection**


Russell:


- Jesus rose spiritually, not bodily

- Believers resurrected in stages


Modern JW:


- Same spiritual resurrection teaching

- But more systematized


### **D. Organization**


Russell:


- No centralized authority

- Bible Students were independent study groups

- Russell discouraged organizational control


Rutherford:


- Centralized authority

- Created the Governing Body

- Required organizational loyalty

- Introduced disfellowshipping and shunning


### **E. Doctrinal shifts**


Under Rutherford and later leaders:


- 1874 → dropped

- 1914 → reinterpreted

- Pyramid chronology → abandoned

- “Millions Now Living Will Never Die” → quietly dropped

- Blood transfusion ban → introduced

- Door‑to‑door preaching → mandated

- Name “Jehovah’s Witnesses” → adopted in 1931


**Summary:** Jehovah’s Witnesses today follow **Rutherford’s system**, not Russell’s.

- **Post‑1940s doctrinal developments:**


Jesus explicitly identified as **Michael the Archangel**.

- **Blood transfusions** prohibited.

- **Cross**, Christmas, birthdays rejected.

- 1914 solidified as start of Christ’s rule and Satan’s casting down.

- Doctrinal changes framed as **“new light”** from God via Governing Body.

## 3. Historical‑critical analysis of Watchtower doctrinal evolution


### **A. Sources and method**


- Primary sources: *Zion’s Watch Tower*, *Studies in the Scriptures*, Watchtower magazines, organizational charters.

- Method:


Trace chronological claims (1874, 1914, 1925, etc.).

- Compare early Bible Student doctrine with later JW doctrine.

- Note shifts in Christology, ecclesiology, and eschatology.


### **B. Key patterns**


**1. Chronological speculation → institutional dogma**


- Early: Russell’s chronology presented as strong but revisable prophetic interpretation.

- Later: 1914 becomes **non‑negotiable** doctrinal cornerstone; questioning it is tantamount to disloyalty.


**2. Decentralized Bible study → centralized Governing Body**


- Russell: Independent Bible Students, loose structure.

- Rutherford onward: Strong central authority; doctrinal changes from headquarters only.


**3. Progressive “new light” narrative**


- Doctrinal reversals (e.g., 1874 presence, pyramidology) reframed as **progressive revelation** rather than error.

- Members taught to welcome changes as proof of being on “path of the righteous.”


**4. Christology drift**


- Russell: non‑Trinitarian but not explicitly angelic Christ.

- Later: Jesus firmly identified as **Michael**, a created being.

- This shift aligns with increasing emphasis on organizational mediation between God and humans.


**5. Ethical and ritual distinctives**


- Blood transfusion ban, rejection of holidays, cross, and secular celebrations develop over time.

- These distinctives reinforce group identity and boundary‑maintenance.


### **C. Critical observations (without caricature)**


- Doctrinal evolution is significant and often **top‑down**, not community‑driven.

- The “new light” framework both allows flexibility and shields leadership from accountability for failed predictions.

- Christology is shaped not only by exegesis but by organizational needs (e.g., emphasizing Christ as heavenly ruler directing the organization).
