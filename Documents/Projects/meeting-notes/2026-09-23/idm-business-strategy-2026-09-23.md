## 2. Two-Product, Two-Channel Strategy (Key Decision)

**The problem:** Selling the same product at two different prices (one direct, one through Medicaid) triggers audit risk. California caps Medicaid reimbursement at the lesser of your "usual charges to the general public."

**The solution:** Two genuinely different products, two MSRPs, two channels.

| Product | Price | Channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Traveler** | $12,000 | Direct to consumers, clinics, facilities, institutions | Portable, collapsible. Advertised on website, social media, email |
| **LiftWalker** | $8,000 (working) | Direct to consumers (economy option) | Non-portable, pallet-shipped. Proven 37-year track record |
| **Future Medicaid product** | $12,000 | Through DME dealers, Medicaid/EPSDT | TBD — needs to be a distinct third product. Charlie to design |

**Key finding from Claude research during call:** "One product with two price tags is not clean. Two genuinely different products and two MSRPs is clean." Rifton does exactly this — Pacer ($3-5K) is the reimbursement product, Tram ($7-10K) is the institutional product.

**Decision: Medicaid is deferred.** We don't pursue DMEs right now. We need a third product if we ever want a Medicaid track. Focus entirely on direct sales for now.

## 4. Marketing Strategy — Two Venues

### Venue 1: Salesperson Route (Local/Regional)
- Charlie and Joelle go to schools, developmental centers, special education programs, clinics, DADDs, adult foster homes
- They need backup materials: videos, assembly guides, maintenance docs, printed catalog
- High-touch, relationship-based (Charlie's proven model)
- **Bakersfield as test bed** (reaffirmed)

### Venue 2: Digital/National (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Email)
- Short-form video clips (5/10/20 sec) — third time this has been identified as critical
- Hook at the beginning, emotional storytelling, "say a lot with a little"
- Target: parents (per 9/17 finding — parents are the demand driver)
- Messaging: "Ask your doctor if Traveler is right for you" style
- Cold email campaign via EsoSoft (not MailChimp — avoids bot restrictions)

**Email compliance (CAN-SPAM + California):**
- Cold B2B emails are legal under CAN-SPAM with: visible opt-out, honor within 10 days, no deception, physical address included
- California CCPA/CPRA adds penalties if consumer data used without opt-in
- Tools: Woodpecker or Instantly (sends from individual mailboxes, looks like 1-to-1 emails)
- Bakersfield test: ~500K people, could do a targeted email campaign through EsoSoft

**Thomas's idea — the "you probably don't need this" email:**
> "You're probably not the person this is for, but this is for a person you may know in your life. If you know someone who needs help standing, transferring, walking — this is what we have. Let your friends know. We want you to know about it."

## 8. Private Insurance as a Separate Track

Thomas identified a third revenue path distinct from both Medicaid and direct sales:

- Sell direct to consumer
- Customer goes to their doctor, gets a letter of medical necessity
- Customer submits to their private insurance for reimbursement
- Customer uses financing to buy upfront, pays off when reimbursed

This is a customer-driven insurance play, not a manufacturer-driven one. Ideomotion never touches the insurance claim.
