# 261123 - History of Antisemitism

# The Oldest Hatred


### A History of Hostility Toward the Jews, from Persia to the Present

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND · 24 November 2026

## Introduction

The hatred of the Jews is older than Rome, older than the Church, and older than any modern ideology. It has survived the fall of every empire that practiced it, and it has changed its costume in every age. It was religious in the Middle Ages, racial in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and is often political today. Under each costume the accusation has been the same: the Jew is the hidden cause of the world's troubles.

I was prompted to write this by a cartoon that has been passing around online. It shows a round table labeled "People Who Hate Jews." Around it sit a Nazi, a radical Islamist, a communist, a conspiracy theorist, an academic elitist, an anti-Zionist activist, and several others who would agree on almost nothing else. The joke lands because it is true. No other hatred seats the far right, the far left, and the religious extremist at the same table.

Under the cartoon, a commenter offered a thoughtful list of reasons people hate Jews. Some sow division as a means to an end. Some are scapegoating, making sense of a complex world. Some are jealous or self-loathing, and some need someone to look down on. He ended by saying that the hatred "begins with Rome," which turned against the Jews after seventy years of Jewish resistance to Roman rule.

I agree with most of his list, and I will come back to it. But the history begins earlier than Rome, and the reasons go deeper than psychology. I stand on this position: hatred of the Jews is a distinct hatred, not merely one prejudice among many. Its persistence across three thousand years, many civilizations, and opposite ideologies demands an explanation that ordinary prejudice cannot supply. In what follows I trace that history era by era, then return to the question of why.

## Before Rome: The Charge of Separateness

The earliest form of the accusation appears in the Book of Esther, set in the Persian court of the fifth century BC. Haman, wanting to destroy the Jews, gives the king his reason:

*"There is a certain people scattered abroad and dispersed among the people in all the provinces of thy kingdom; and their laws are diverse from all people; neither keep they the king's laws: therefore it is not for the king's profit to suffer them."* **(Esther 3:8)**

That sentence holds the template for much of what followed. The Jews are scattered yet set apart. They live by a different law. Their loyalty is therefore suspect, and their destruction would be convenient for the state. Haman also offered the king money for the job, an early sign of how often greed travels with this hatred.

The history outside Scripture shows the same pattern. Around 410 BC, Egyptian priests at Elephantine, on the Nile, destroyed the temple of a Jewish garrison community. Letters from the Jews there survive, asking Jerusalem for help to rebuild it.

In 167 BC the Seleucid king Antiochus IV tried to erase Judaism outright. He banned circumcision, Sabbath observance, and the study of the Law, and he set up a pagan altar in the Temple in Jerusalem. The Maccabean revolt that followed is remembered at Hanukkah. Antiochus was the first ruler to try to destroy the Jewish faith rather than merely conquer the Jewish people.

In Hellenistic Egypt the hatred acquired a literature. The Egyptian priest Manetho, writing in the third century BC, retold the Exodus as the expulsion of a colony of lepers. Later Greek writers in Alexandria added that the Jews worshipped an ass's head and hated all mankind. The grammarian Apion collected these slanders, and the Jewish historian Josephus answered them in his work *Against Apion*. In AD 38, under the Roman governor Flaccus, the Greek mob of Alexandria attacked the Jewish quarter. That riot, which Philo of Alexandria recorded, was arguably the first pogrom.

The common thread of this period is not race, and it is not yet theology in the Christian sense. It is resentment of a people who would not bow to the gods of the city. In a world where religion and civic loyalty were one, a people who worshipped one invisible God and refused all others looked like a standing insult to everyone else.

## Rome: Contempt, Revolt, and Dispersion

The commenter is right that Rome changed the course of this history, though Rome did not start it. Pompey took Jerusalem in 63 BC. For about a century afterward Rome mostly tolerated Judaism. Jews were excused from emperor worship and allowed to send the Temple tax to Jerusalem. Roman writers despised them anyway. Cicero sneered at the Jewish community in a courtroom speech. Tacitus, writing around AD 100, called Jewish customs "base and abominable" and wrote that the Jews held profane everything Romans held sacred. Juvenal mocked Roman converts who kept the Sabbath and gave up pork.

Then came three wars in seventy years, which is the period the commenter had in mind:


 	- **The Great Revolt (AD 66–73).** Titus besieged Jerusalem and burned the Temple in AD 70. Josephus put the dead in the hundreds of thousands. Masada fell around AD 73.

 	- **The Diaspora Revolt (AD 115–117).** Jewish communities in Egypt, Cyrene, and Cyprus rose against Rome and were crushed.

 	- **The Bar Kokhba Revolt (AD 132–135).** Under Hadrian, Judea itself was largely depopulated.


Rome's response went beyond punishing rebels. After AD 70, the Temple tax was redirected to the temple of Jupiter in Rome, a deliberate humiliation called the *fiscus Judaicus*. After AD 135, Hadrian rebuilt Jerusalem as a pagan colony, Aelia Capitolina, and barred Jews from entering it. He renamed the province Syria Palaestina, after the Philistines, to sever the link between the people and the land. The name Palestine in its modern use descends from that decision.

So Rome did not invent the hatred, but it did two things that shaped everything after. It destroyed the center of Jewish life and scattered the people across the Mediterranean world. It also left behind a literature of contempt that the next civilization, the Christian one, would inherit.

## The Christian Centuries: A Confession

As a Christian, I have to tell this part of the history plainly. For more than a thousand years the main carrier of hatred toward the Jews in the West was the Church, or people acting in its name. That is a grievous irony. Jesus was a Jew, His apostles were Jews, and He told the Samaritan woman,

*"Salvation is of the Jews."* **(John 4:22)**

The turn began early. As Christianity separated from the synagogue, a genre of sermons and treatises "against the Jews" grew up. Around AD 170, Melito of Sardis accused Israel of murdering God, the charge of deicide that would echo for eighteen centuries. In 386 and 387, John Chrysostom preached eight sermons in Antioch against the Jews, in language that later persecutors quoted with approval. Augustine offered a restraint of sorts: the Jews should not be killed, because their survival in humiliation bore witness to the truth of Scripture. That doctrine preserved Jewish lives, but it also assigned them a permanently degraded place.

After Christianity became the religion of the Empire, Roman law began to restrict Jews. In the Middle Ages the restrictions became violence:


 	- **1096.** Crusaders on their way to the Holy Land massacred Jewish communities in the Rhineland, at Speyer, Worms, and Mainz.

 	- **1144.** In Norwich, England, Jews were accused of the ritual murder of a boy named William. This "blood libel," the claim that Jews murder Christian children for their blood, spread across Europe and produced massacres for centuries. Several popes condemned it, and it spread anyway.

 	- **1215.** The Fourth Lateran Council required Jews to wear distinguishing dress, which in practice became badges and hats.

 	- **1348–1351.** During the Black Death, Jews were accused of poisoning wells. Hundreds of communities were destroyed, including the burning of the Jews of Strasbourg in 1349, although Pope Clement VI pointed out that Jews were dying of the plague too.

 	- **Expulsions.** England expelled its Jews in 1290, France in 1306 and again in 1394, Spain in 1492, and Portugal in 1497. In Spain the Inquisition then hunted converted Jews suspected of secretly keeping their faith.

 	- **1516.** Venice confined its Jews to a walled quarter called the Ghetto, giving the world the word.

 	- **1543.** Martin Luther, disappointed that the Jews had not converted, wrote *On the Jews and Their Lies*. He urged that synagogues be burned and Jewish homes destroyed. Four centuries later the Nazis reprinted it.


The Middle Ages also created the economic stereotype. The Church forbade Christians to lend at interest, and the guilds shut Jews out of most crafts. Moneylending was one of the few trades left open to Jews, and kings used them as tax collectors and lenders, then expelled them to cancel their debts. The image of the Jew and money was produced by the very system that later condemned it.

The Apostle Paul had already warned the Gentile believers against exactly this arrogance:

*"Boast not against the branches. But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee."* **(Romans 11:18)**

*"As concerning the gospel, they are enemies for your sakes: but as touching the election, they are beloved for the fathers' sakes. **For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance."* **(Romans 11:28–29)**

The Church in those centuries forgot that warning. Much of it has since repented. The Catholic Church repudiated the charge of collective Jewish guilt for the death of Christ in *Nostra Aetate* in 1965, and many Protestant bodies have renounced Luther's anti-Jewish writings. That repentance was right, and it was late.

## Under Islam: Protection and Subordination

The Jewish experience under Islamic rule was different from the experience in Christian Europe, though it was not the golden harmony sometimes claimed. In Medina, Muhammad came into conflict with the Jewish tribes of the city. Two were expelled, and in 627 the men of the third, the Banu Qurayza, were executed. The Quran contains passages that honor the Children of Israel and others that condemn them, and later polemicists drew on the harsher ones.

Under the caliphates, Jews and Christians became *dhimmis*, protected but subordinate peoples. They paid a special poll tax, the *jizya*, and lived under restrictions on dress, building, and public worship, traditionally attributed to the "Pact of Umar." In practice, enforcement varied widely by ruler and century. In many periods Jews under Islam were safer than Jews in Christian Europe. Jewish learning flourished in Baghdad, Cairo, and Muslim Spain.

The protection had limits. In 1066 a mob in Granada killed the Jewish vizier Joseph ibn Naghrela and massacred much of the city's Jewish community. In the twelfth century the Almohad rulers of North Africa and Spain forced Jews to convert, flee, or die. Maimonides was among those who fled. In Morocco, Jews were later confined to walled quarters called *mellahs*, and in Yemen and Persia they suffered periodic forced conversions and humiliating laws.

In the modern era, European conspiracy theories entered the Muslim world. The Damascus blood libel of 1840 was instigated with the help of the French consul. In the twentieth century the Grand Mufti of Jerusalem, Haj Amin al-Husseini, allied with Hitler and broadcast Nazi propaganda in Arabic. In June 1941 a pogrom in Baghdad, the Farhud, killed about 180 Jews. After Israel's founding in 1948, roughly 850,000 Jews left or were driven out of Arab countries and Iran, ending communities that had lived there for two thousand years or more. Hamas's 1988 charter cited the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* as evidence of a Jewish plot.

The cartoon's label is "Radical Islam," not Islam, and that distinction is right. Many Muslims reject this hatred, and some have risked their lives to shelter Jews. But Islamist movements today are among the most organized and violent carriers of it.

## From Religion to Race

The Enlightenment promised to end the old hatred. France made its Jews full citizens in 1791, and other nations followed over the next century. Jews left the ghettos and entered law, medicine, science, journalism, and finance in large numbers. The hatred did not end. It changed its reason.

A medieval Jew could escape persecution by baptism. A modern Jew could not, because the new hatred was based on blood. In 1879 the German journalist Wilhelm Marr popularized the word "antisemitism" and founded a League of Antisemites. The word was chosen to sound scientific. It described hostility to the Jews as a race, regardless of what they believed or how fully they had assimilated.

The new form produced a contradiction that has never gone away. The Jew was blamed as the capitalist who controlled the banks and as the revolutionary who wanted to destroy them. He was condemned as too clannish and also as too eager to blend in. A hatred that can hold both sides of every contradiction is not a response to anything Jews actually do.

The major events of this period:


 	- **Russian pogroms, 1881–1906.** Waves of mob violence followed the assassination of Tsar Alexander II. The Kishinev pogrom of 1903 shocked the world. Two million Jews emigrated from Eastern Europe, most to America.

 	- **The Dreyfus Affair, 1894–1906.** A Jewish French army captain, Alfred Dreyfus, was falsely convicted of treason. Crowds in Paris shouted "Death to the Jews." A journalist covering the affair, Theodor Herzl, concluded that Jews would never be safe without a state of their own, and launched modern political Zionism.

 	- **The Protocols of the Elders of Zion, around 1903.** A forgery produced in Russia, largely plagiarized from a French satire that had nothing to do with Jews, claimed to reveal a Jewish plan for world domination. It was exposed as a fraud in 1921. Henry Ford promoted it in America anyway, and it remains in print around the world.

 	- **The Russian Civil War, 1918–1921.** Pogroms by several armies killed somewhere between 50,000 and 200,000 Jews, mostly in Ukraine.

 	- **The Holocaust, 1933–1945.** Nazi Germany stripped Jews of citizenship under the Nuremberg Laws of 1935, burned synagogues on Kristallnacht in 1938, and then carried out the systematic murder of about six million Jews, roughly two-thirds of the Jews of Europe. It was the full logic of racial antisemitism carried out with modern industry and bureaucracy.

 	- **Soviet antisemitism, 1948–1953 and after.** Stalin purged Jews as "rootless cosmopolitans," executed leading Yiddish writers in 1952, and fabricated the "Doctors' Plot" against Jewish physicians in 1953. Soviet Jews were denied emigration for decades. The communist at the cartoon's table belongs there.


The lesson of this century is that education and progress did not cure the hatred. Germany was among the most educated nations on earth. The hatred adapted to science, and then used it.

## After Israel: The Newest Costume

After the Holocaust, open racial antisemitism became disreputable in the West, and many believed the hatred was finally dying. It was not. The founding of Israel in 1948 gave it a new object and a new vocabulary.

I want to be careful here, because the line matters. Criticizing the policies of the Israeli government is not antisemitism. Israelis criticize their own government constantly, and some Jews are anti-Zionist on religious grounds. But criticism crosses into the old hatred when it does what Natan Sharansky called the "three D's." It *demonizes* Israel as uniquely evil. It *delegitimizes* the Jewish state's right to exist when no other nation's right is questioned. And it applies a *double standard* demanded of no other people. Another clear sign is when Jews in New York, Paris, or Sydney are held responsible for decisions made in Jerusalem. That is the ancient charge of collective guilt in modern dress.

On 7 October 2023, Hamas killed about 1,200 people in southern Israel and took about 250 hostages, the largest killing of Jews in a single day since the Holocaust. Within days, before Israel's response in Gaza had fully begun, antisemitic incidents surged worldwide. Since then the record has been grim:


 	- In 2025, ADL counted [6,274 antisemitic incidents in the United States](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/storage/adl-audit-2025.pdf). That was 33 percent below the 9,354 of 2024 but five times the level of a decade earlier, and the third-highest year since tracking began in 1979.

 	- Incidents fell, but violence rose. Physical assaults reached a [record 203](https://www.adl.org/glossary/ye), and about 45 percent of incidents were [related to Israel or Zionism](https://www.adl.org/resources/press-release/adl-records-historic-high-antisemitic-assaults-and-attacks-deadly-weapons).

 	- Worldwide, [20 people were killed in four antisemitic attacks](https://www.thejc.com/news/world/last-year-worst-for-antisemitic-violence-in-west-in-30-years-ddgzcopf) in Western countries in 2025, the highest toll in over three decades. Fifteen died at a Hanukkah celebration at Bondi Beach in Sydney. Two died at a Manchester synagogue on Yom Kippur. Two young Israeli embassy staff were shot outside the Capital Jewish Museum in Washington, D.C., and an elderly woman died after the firebombing of a march for the hostages in Boulder, Colorado. It was the [deadliest year for Jews outside Israel](https://www.jns.org/news/world/2025-deadliest-year-for-jews-outside-israel-since-buenos-aires-bombing-report-says) since the 1994 bombing of the Jewish community center in Buenos Aires.


The hatred also persists on the right. The deadliest attack on Jews in American history, at the Tree of Life synagogue in Pittsburgh in 2018, was carried out by a white nationalist who believed Jews were bringing in immigrants to replace white Americans. The "Jews run everything" chair at the cartoon's table is filled online every day.

That is why the cartoon is true. The Islamist, the anti-Zionist activist, the neo-Nazi, and the conspiracy theorist disagree about almost everything. They agree that the Jews are the problem.

## Why the Jews?

The commenter's list is a good account of why people hate. Scapegoating, envy, the need to feel elevated, powerlessness, the wish to make a complicated world simple: all of these are real, and each has driven persecution of the Jews in some age. But the list explains the motive of the hater, not the choice of target. Every society has scapegoats. Why has this one people been chosen in nearly every age, by civilizations and ideologies that otherwise hate each other?

Scholars have offered several answers, and I think each holds part of the truth:


 	- **The permanent minority.** Since Rome scattered them, Jews have lived almost everywhere as a visible, distinct minority without an army. A scapegoat must be close enough to blame and weak enough to punish, and for nearly two thousand years Jews were both.

 	- **Separateness and success.** Jews kept their own law, their own calendar, and their own schools, and their emphasis on literacy often brought success out of proportion to their numbers. Distinctness invites suspicion, and success invites envy. Together they produce the charge of a hidden, clannish power.

 	- **The conspiracy habit.** When suffering is large and its causes are complex, people reach for a hidden hand. Plague, defeat in war, economic collapse, and social change have all been blamed on Jews, because one secret enemy is easier to understand than many impersonal forces.

 	- **The bearers of the moral law.** Several Jewish writers have argued that the deepest source is what Judaism brought into the world: one God, a universal moral law, and the claim that every person is answerable to that God. People who resent being judged come to resent the people who carry the standard. On this view, it is no accident that pagan Rome, the neo-pagan Nazis, and militantly atheist communism all targeted Jews.


I stand with that last answer, and I would take it one step further. I believe this hatred is ultimately spiritual. The Jews are the people through whom God chose to reveal Himself, give His Law, and bring the Messiah. To hate them is, at bottom, to rage against the God who chose them. The psalmist saw the pattern three thousand years ago:

*"They have taken crafty counsel against thy people, and consulted against thy hidden ones. **They have said, Come, and let us cut them off from being a nation; that the name of Israel may be no more in remembrance."* **(Psalm 83:3–4)**

And God's promise to Abraham explains the stakes for every nation that takes up this hatred:

*"And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed."* **(Genesis 12:3)**

I hold this as a matter of faith, not as something history can prove. A secular reader can accept the first three explanations and reject the fourth. But I notice that the secular explanations each account for some persecutions and fail to account for others, while the spiritual explanation fits the whole record. I also notice the other half of the pattern, the one Mark Twain marveled at in 1899. Egypt, Babylon, Persia, Greece, and Rome all passed away, and the Jew remained. Twain asked: *"What is the secret of his immortality?"* I believe the answer is the faithfulness of God.

## Conclusion: Recognizing the Costume

Three thousand years of history teach one practical lesson: the hatred never announces itself by its own name. In Persia it was loyalty to the king. In Rome it was civic piety. In medieval Europe it was zeal for Christ. In the nineteenth century it was science, and in the twentieth it was the revolution or the race. Today it is often presented as justice. Each generation recognizes the costumes of the past and misses the one in front of it.

For Christians, the history carries a particular weight. The worst of it was done in the name of the Jewish Messiah we worship, by people who forgot that we were grafted into Israel's root. Our duty now is simple to state. We should refuse the hatred in whatever costume it appears, whether it comes from the right, the left, or the mosque, and whether it is shouted in a street or whispered as a joke. We can debate the policies of any government, Israel's included, honestly and on the same terms we apply to everyone else. And we should stand beside our Jewish neighbors when they are threatened, as too few Christians did when it mattered most.

The test I apply to any public act is whether my neighbor would feel loved by it. By that test, this hatred fails at every table where it is served.

## Sources and Their Limits

The ancient and medieval history in this essay is drawn from the standard historical record: Scripture, Josephus, Philo, Tacitus, and the scholarship built on them. Some numbers in that record, such as Josephus's death tolls or the casualties of the Russian Civil War pogroms, are estimates and are disputed. Where scholars give a range, I have given the range.

The figures for recent years come from these reports:


 	- [ADL, Audit of Antisemitic Incidents 2025](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/storage/adl-audit-2025.pdf) (released 6 May 2026)

 	- [ADL press release on record assaults](https://www.adl.org/resources/press-release/adl-records-historic-high-antisemitic-assaults-and-attacks-deadly-weapons)

 	- [Jewish Chronicle on the Tel Aviv University report for 2025](https://www.thejc.com/news/world/last-year-worst-for-antisemitic-violence-in-west-in-30-years-ddgzcopf)

 	- [JNS on the J7 Task Force report for 2025](https://www.jns.org/news/world/2025-deadliest-year-for-jews-outside-israel-since-buenos-aires-bombing-report-says)


These sources have limits. The ADL is an advocacy organization, and its counts depend on its definitions. Critics argue that it counts some anti-Israel protest speech as antisemitic, while the ADL says it excludes legitimate political protest and opposition to Israeli policy. The line between harsh criticism of Israel and antisemitism is itself contested, and reasonable people draw it in different places. Incident counts also depend on reporting, which rises when communities are alarmed. The death tolls from deadly attacks are not subject to these definitional disputes, and they are the strongest evidence that the danger is real and growing.

The explanation in "Why the Jews?" goes beyond what history can prove. The first three causes are well documented. The spiritual explanation is my conviction as a Christian, and I have marked it as such.
