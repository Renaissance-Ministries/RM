# 261121 - The Names of God and His Roles

# The Father Who Sends and the Word That Abides

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND · 21 November, 2026

## The card and its message

A picture recently appeared in my Facebook feed that sets the Father and the Son side by side. On the left are the four Hebrew letters of God's name, יהוה, rendered "Yahuah," with the titles "The Creator" and "The Father." On the right is the Hebrew name of the Son, יהושע, rendered "Yahusha," with the titles "The Messiah" and "The Son." Beneath them are four verses about God's name, and across the bottom is John 5:38, with a few words added in parentheses to explain it:

*"And ye have not his word abiding in you: for whom he hath sent, him ye believe not"* **(John 5:38)**.

This card can be read as an argument about pronunciation. There is a movement that argues that "Jesus" is a false name. But the card does not say that. Read on its own terms, its message is deeper and, I think, true. John 5:38 says that there is an almighty Father, the source of all truth. He sent His Son, who is His Word. The men Jesus was speaking to had neither the Word abiding in their hearts nor faith in the One the Father sent. The card is about the relationship between the Father who sends and the Son who carries His message, and about whether that message lives inside us.

The issue of the Word of God, the spirit of truth living inside us, is the subject of this essay. The question of how to pronounce the Hebrew names is a separate matter, and I have put it in a short note near the end, because it is interesting but not important. The name does not save us. What saves us, and what keeps us, is the Word planted in the heart: *"Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee"* **(Psalm 119:11)**.

## The Father who sends and the Son who carries His Word

The Gospel of John returns again and again to one relationship: the Father is the source, and the Son is the One sent to carry the Father's word into the world. Jesus never claimed to speak on His own. *"My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me"* **(John 7:16)**. *"For I have not spoken of myself; but the Father which sent me, he gave me a commandment, what I should say, and what I should speak"* **(John 12:49)**.

Yet the Son is not merely a messenger who delivers words and steps aside, the way a courier hands over a letter. John opens his Gospel by telling us that the Son *is* the Word: *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God"* **(John 1:1)**, and *"the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us"* **(John 1:14)**. The message and the Messenger are one. Jesus does not only tell us the truth; He says, *"I am the way, the truth, and the life"* **(John 14:6)**.

So the Father and the Son share one nature but hold different roles. *"I and my Father are one"* **(John 10:30)**, and also, *"my Father is greater than I"* **(John 14:28)**. I understand it this way. The Father is the All, the perfect and complete source of truth, unseen. The Son is that truth made visible and personal, the fullness of the Father's nature, law, and way embodied in a human life. *"No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him"* **(John 1:18)**. To believe the Son is to receive the Father's Word, and to receive the Word is to receive the Son.

This is the relationship the card draws in its two columns: the Father who sends, the Son who is sent, and between them a single message meant to be believed and to live in us.

## John 5:38: the Word that did not abide

Jesus spoke these words to religious leaders who knew the Scriptures better than almost anyone alive. Read the verse with the ones around it:

*"And the Father himself, which hath sent me, hath borne witness of me. Ye have neither heard his voice at any time, nor seen his shape. And ye have not his word abiding in you: for whom he hath sent, him ye believe not. Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life"* **(John 5:37–40)**.

The verse joins two failures with the word "for." They did not have the Father's word abiding in them, *for* they did not believe the One He sent. Their unbelief showed that the Word was not in them, and the absence of the Word kept them in unbelief. The two feed each other, in both directions. Where the Word is welcomed, faith grows; where faith opens the heart, the Word takes root. Jesus describes the good direction of that cycle in His prayer for His disciples: *"For I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me; and they have received them, and have known surely that I came out from thee, and they have believed that thou didst send me"* **(John 17:8)**.

What, then, is the "word" that did not abide in them? Most commentators read it as the Father's testimony written in Scripture, and verse 39 supports that reading: these men searched the Scriptures daily. But that is exactly the point. They had the written Word in their hands, in their memories, and on their phylacteries, and still did not have the Word abiding in them. They possessed the text and missed the Person to whom the text pointed. In the Gospel that begins *"In the beginning was the Word,"* I believe John intends us to see both meanings at once: the Father's message, and the Son who is that message made flesh.

This is an important distinction. Scripture is true; it is *"given by inspiration of God"* **(2 Timothy 3:16)**, and Jesus said that *"the scripture cannot be broken"* **(John 10:35)**. But Scripture is a witness, not the One witnessed. Paul says the law and its ordinances were *"a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ"* **(Colossians 2:17)**. A shadow is real, and it is cast by something real, and it can lead you to the One who casts it. But no one embraces a shadow. The written Word, at its best, is the faithful outline of the living Word, who is truth in its fullness and perfection, embodied in Jesus Christ. *"For the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life"* **(2 Corinthians 3:6)**.

The leaders in John 5 are a warning to every serious Bible reader, me included. It is possible to know the letters, the verses, and even the divine name with precision, and still not let the Word in.

## The Word hidden in the heart

If the Word did not abide in those men, what does it look like when it does? The psalmist answered centuries before Christ: *"Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee"* **(Psalm 119:11)**. The Word is not stored in the heart as information. It is planted there as a living thing, and its fruit is a life that no longer wants to sin against God.

God promised that this planting would become the mark of the New Covenant: *"I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people"* **(Jeremiah 31:33)**. And Jesus promised how it would happen. Before He left, He told His disciples, *"I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever"* **(John 14:16)**, and in the same breath, *"I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you"* **(John 14:18)**. The Spirit who would come is the One who would *"guide you into all truth"* **(John 16:13)**.

I believe that the Holy Spirit sent after Jesus ascended is, in a real sense, the Spirit of Christ Himself, the perfect One, coming to live in everyone who asks. Scripture uses the names interchangeably: *"ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his"* **(Romans 8:9)**. *"God hath sent forth the Spirit of his Son into your hearts"* **(Galatians 4:6)**. And He is given freely: *"how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?"* **(Luke 11:13)**. The Father sent the Son into the world; the Son, through the Spirit, comes into us. Paul calls it *"Christ in you, the hope of glory"* **(Colossians 1:27)**.

This is where the card's message meets what I have come to see as the center of the Gospel. God is not a distant judge watching our lives from outside. He lives inside every person's experience; in a real sense, our lives are His experience of the world. (I set out the argument in my [Premise Change handout](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/10/31/260725-premise-change-handout/) and in [Reconciliation of Man with the Father](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/07/25/260725-reconciliation-of-man-with-the-father/).) Because He lives our lives with us, He feels our choices. When we sin, we wound the One who is closest to us. That is why the psalmist hid the Word in his heart: not to pass a test, but so that he "might not sin against thee." When the Word abides in us, we keep His law because we love Him and do not want to grieve Him.

So the order in John 5:38 matters for each of us. The Father sends. The Son comes, carrying the Father's Word, and being that Word. We believe Him, and the Word takes up residence in us through His Spirit. Then we live from the inside out, as Jesus said: *"If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him"* **(John 14:23)**. The Word abiding in us and God abiding in us turn out to be the same thing.

## Many names, many aspects of one God

The card lists verses that speak of God's "name," and they are worth reading the way a Hebrew reader would. In Hebrew thought a name (*shem*) is not a sound attached to the outside of a person. It stands for his character, reputation, and authority. When God proclaimed His name to Moses, He proclaimed His character: *"The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth"* **(Exodus 34:6)**.

That is why God has many names. Each one shows a different aspect of a God too great for any single word:


 	- *El Shaddai*, "God Almighty" **(Genesis 17:1)**: His power to keep His promises.

 	- *Jehovah-jireh*, "the LORD will provide" **(Genesis 22:14)**: His provision, named on the mountain where He supplied the ram in Isaac's place.

 	- *Jehovah-nissi*, "the LORD my banner" **(Exodus 17:15)**: His leadership in battle.

 	- *Jehovah-shalom*, "the LORD is peace" **(Judges 6:24)**: the peace He gives.

 	- *"Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace"* **(Isaiah 9:6)**: the names of the promised Son.

 	- *Emmanuel*, *"God with us"* **(Matthew 1:23)**, and *Yehoshua*, Jesus, "the LORD saves" **(Matthew 1:21)**.


The Son is also called the Word, the Lamb of God, the Good Shepherd, the Bread of Life, and the Light of the World. No single name holds Him. The very fact that there are many names tells us that there are many aspects of His fullness, and that every name is a window onto Someone larger than the window.

And that brings a warning. A name, or a page of Scripture, points to a heavenly, spiritual, eternal reality that we cannot see. *"For the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal"* **(2 Corinthians 4:18)**. When we forget that, and treat the name itself or the text itself as the object of worship, we make it a small, earthbound thing, and it can become an idol, a graven image of a subtler kind, standing in the place of the true God.

Israel did exactly this once with something God Himself had commanded. In the wilderness God told Moses to make a bronze serpent, and those who looked at it were healed. Seven centuries later, King Hezekiah had to break it in pieces, *"for unto those days the children of Israel did burn incense to it"* **(2 Kings 18:4)**. A gift of God had become a substitute for God. The same can happen with a name, a translation, a creed, or a verse we love. Jesus said that the Father seeks those who *"worship him in spirit and in truth"* **(John 4:24)**. The names and the Scriptures are given to lead us there. They are signposts, and the destination is the living God.

## A side note on pronunciation

The spellings "Yahuah" and "Yahusha" come from what is called the *Sacred Name movement*, a set of groups that began in the 1930s and teach that God and His Son must be called only by their Hebrew names. Some in the movement go further and call "Jesus" a false or pagan name. The card does not make that argument, and the question has nothing to do with its message. But readers who meet the argument elsewhere may want to know where it stands, so I give a short account here.


 	- **God has a personal name, and tradition hid it.** True. The four letters יהוה appear in the Hebrew Bible roughly 6,800 times. Out of reverence, Jewish readers came to say *Adonai*, "my Lord," in its place, and English Bibles followed with "LORD." Most Christians never learn that God told us His name. That loss is real.

 	- **The exact pronunciation is uncertain.** Hebrew was written without vowels, and the vowel marks in our Hebrew Bibles were added between about AD 600 and 1000. Early Greek transcriptions point toward "Yahweh," which most scholars accept as the best reconstruction. "Yahuah" has much less support. No one alive can say for certain how Moses said it.

 	- **The Son's Hebrew name** is the same as Joshua's, *Yehoshua*, often shortened to *Yeshua* after the Exile (Nehemiah calls Joshua son of Nun "Jeshua" **(Nehemiah 8:17)**). "Yahusha" is a modern reconstruction.

 	- **"Jesus" is not a pagan name.** Jewish translators rendered *Yehoshua* into Greek as *Iēsous* two centuries before Christ, and the apostles, writing under the inspiration of the Spirit, used that same Greek name. They also rendered God's name as *Kyrios*, "Lord," when they quoted the Old Testament. "Jesus" is simply that Greek name as it came through Latin into English.


My conclusion is that knowing God's Hebrew name is a blessing, and anyone who finds that it draws him closer to God should use it with joy. But it is external to the heart of the matter. The name does not save. If a pronunciation could save, the leaders of John 5, who knew the name and the Scriptures perfectly, would have been saved. What saves is believing the One the Father sent, and letting His Word abide in us.

The historical sources lean on mainstream scholarship, which works within a consensus and has its own interests. The evidence about ancient pronunciation is thin on every side, so I hold "Yahweh" as the best-supported guess, not a certainty. The main argument of this essay does not depend on that guess. It rests on Scripture's own account of the Father, the Son, and the Word abiding in the heart.
