# Five Models of Witness: A Problem-Solving Synthesis of the Fellowship's Evangelism Discussion

*Fellowship Discussion Essay | 2 August 2026*

*And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature.* — Mark 16:15

**Occasion.** The fellowship met on the morning of 2 August 2026 to work the question left open by last week's essay, "Developing the Courage and Certainty to Evangelize": granted the diagnosis — that Christian silence is more often a crisis of grounds than a crisis of nerve — what is the method? Present were Charlie and Susan Gutierrez, Leonard Hofheins with Debbie, Michael Sherman, Armond Boulware, and myself. This document is not a transcript and not a devotional. It is a problem-solving synthesis, written to academic purpose: to identify the distinct positions that surfaced, to test them against Scripture and against each other, to state what works, and to specify the way forward for the group. Where I judge a position weak, I say so; the participants named here are friends of long standing, and the record will show that none of them softened their claims for my sake either.

The discussion divided naturally into seven topics. I take them in order.

## I. The Problem as Posed: Method, Not Motivation

The session opened with confirmation of the prior essay's premise. Charlie, reflecting on his years as a Mormon missionary, named the failure mode with precision: the effort was idealistic, altruistic, and well-intentioned, and it did not convey the message of Christ — it conveyed the message of a church. The organization was the content; Christ was the warrant. He called the edifice he served a counterfeit of Christ's church, and his regret was not that he taught, but that what he taught brought his friends to an institution rather than to a Person. This is a datum of the first importance for our method question, because it comes from inside the most operationally successful evangelistic machine in American religious history. The Mormon missionary program solves the mobilization problem completely — every nineteen-year-old goes — and Charlie's testimony is that mobilization without the right message produces converts to the wrong thing. Whatever method we adopt must therefore be evaluated on two axes, not one: does it reach people, and does it deliver Christ rather than an edifice, a personality, or a movement.

Charlie also posed the question that frames everything downstream: he and Susan have begun asking, in his words, what God wants them to do with this — move somewhere, become missionaries where they are, teach their friends. The obligation is felt; the form is undetermined. That is exactly the state of the group as a whole, and it is the right state from which to do this work. A fellowship that felt no obligation would have nothing to decide, and a fellowship certain of its form would have nothing to discuss.

## II. The Science Objection Revisited: Deep Time and the Text

The first substantive obstacle examined was the one last week's essay predicted: science as the stumbling block that intimidates the witness before the conversation begins. Charlie supplied a concrete instance from the previous evening — an art history volume opening with a fertility figurine dated to 28,000 BC, and the conversation it forced with his daughter about whether such a date can coexist with Genesis. Leonard added the meteorite he had held at astronomy club, dated in the billions of years. The question was put to me directly: does the theory address the age of the earth?

The position I gave, and which I here formalize, rests on the Hebrew text itself. The word rendered "day" in Genesis 1 — *yom* — is not confined to a twenty-four-hour period; it denotes a span, an age, a completed phase of work. The six-thousand-year figure derives not from the creation account but from summing the genealogies from Adam forward, which is a chronology of covenant man, not a chronology of the cosmos. And the internal evidence of the text supports the unbounded reading: a twenty-four-hour day is defined by the rotation of an earth relative to a sun, and the narrative places the ordering of sun and earth *inside* the creation week, not before it. One cannot use the clock to time the building of the clock. On this reading the days are periods of unspecified duration, the deep-time measurements of geology and astronomy present no contradiction, and Adam and Eve stand as the crown of the process — a spiritual endowment, an overlay of the divine image upon a prepared earth that had borne other creatures before them. Charlie's own answer to his daughter ran the same direction: we do not know how long the garden stood before the disobedience; the text does not say; carbon dating could simply be correct.

This resolution works, and the fellowship should note *why* it works: it concedes nothing to materialism and nothing to a wooden literalism that the Hebrew does not require. It is a philological argument, not an accommodation. The witness equipped with it can hold the figurine, hold the meteorite, and hold Genesis, without flinching and without special pleading.

One caution belongs in the record. Leonard raised the anomalous-artifact literature — Michael Cremo's *Forbidden Archeology* and the grooved metallic spheres reported from South African mines — as evidence that establishment science suppresses inconvenient finds. The claim that institutional science shelves anomalies that violate its paradigm is, as a general sociological observation, defensible and well documented in the history of science. But the specific artifacts in that literature are contested, and several — the Klerksdorp spheres among them — have credible conventional explanations as natural mineral concretions. The methodological rule the fellowship has already adopted elsewhere applies here: we do not build the case for Scripture on evidence that our own standards of verification would reject if it were offered against us. The *yom* argument of the preceding paragraph needs no help from disputed artifacts, and an apologetic that leans on them hands the skeptic an easy refutation and forfeits the credibility the strong argument earned. Use the philology; hold the anomalies loosely.

## III. The Scope of the Commission: An Exegetical Dispute

The sharpest disagreement of the session was Leonard's claim that the Great Commission of Matthew 28:19 was addressed to the eleven and to them only — that it is a commission to the apostles, not a standing order to every believer. Charlie objected immediately that Christ was always addressing particular hearers, that He never spoke to the camera, and that the whole interpretive tradition would collapse if direct address limited application. The dispute deserves a careful verdict rather than a vote, because both men are holding something true.

The restrictive reading fails on the evidence of the apostolic generation itself. When persecution scattered the Jerusalem church, the text records that "they that were scattered abroad went every where preaching the word" (Acts 8:4) — and these were expressly *not* the apostles, who remained at Jerusalem (Acts 8:1). Paul assigns the ministry of reconciliation to the whole church: "God... hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation... Now then we are ambassadors for Christ" (2 Corinthians 5:18-20). Peter commands every believer to "be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you" (1 Peter 3:15). The first generation, which heard the commission in living memory, did not read it as an apostolic monopoly, and neither can we.

But Leonard's restrictive reading was guarding something the fellowship must not lose, and his own practice reveals it. His model — worn lightly as a hat with a small emblem at a breakfast buffet, noticed by a cashier, explained when asked — is the *providential encounter* model: the Lord does the work, arranges the meetings, and does not trifle with souls; our part is availability, not campaign. His warning had a text behind it even though he did not cite the chapter: we cannot steady the ark. Uzzah's hand on the ark of God (2 Samuel 6:6-7) is the standing rebuke to every servant who concludes that God's work needs man's forcing. The verdict, then: the commission binds every believer — Leonard's exegesis is wrong — but Leonard's *operational theology* is substantially right, and the fellowship should receive it as a constraint on method rather than a release from duty. Nothing is accidental when it comes to the Lord; and the commission still says go.

Moses gives us the reconciliation in a single verse, which Charlie quoted from memory: "would God that all the LORD'S people were prophets" (Numbers 11:29). The commission is universal precisely because the new covenant makes the prophetic burden universal.

## IV. Five Models of Witness

With the scope question settled, the session's principal yield can be stated. Five distinct models of evangelism surfaced, each championed by a different member, and the analytical finding of this essay is that they are not competitors. They are layers of a single method, ordered from the interior outward.

**The mission field of the self (Charlie, Susan).** Charlie coined the phrase "the missionary field of myself," and Susan supplied its charter text: "first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother's eye" (Matthew 7:3-5). Her argument was rigorous, not sentimental: sanctification is not preparation for witness as training is preparation for a race — it is the *generative source* of witness. The believer who addresses the log finds that the fruits of the Spirit, the words, the vision for how to reach out, arrive as byproducts of the relationship; and conversely, the believer who feels witness as an external obligation ("I had better go share") is thereby diagnosed as still needing the interior work. This inverts the usual evangelism-program logic, which recruits first and forms later, and it explains Charlie's Mormon datum from Section I: a machine that mobilizes the unformed transmits the machine.

**The household field (Armond).** Armond argued that the decisive battles are not the abstract hypotheticals people rehearse — the imagined street-corner confrontation with the sophisticated skeptic — but the daily engagements in one's own house, with one's own family, which are harder precisely because they cannot be walked away from. It is easier to serve a stranger than a son; the stranger's case closes, the son's case is carried day and night. His practice with his own son supplies the tactical doctrine: Ephesians 6:12 deployed not as private consolation but as a *stated frame of the relationship* — "we wrestle not against flesh and blood" — spoken aloud to the son so that correction registers as alliance against the spirits rather than attack upon the person, the mother, the friends, or the coaches. This is the most operationally specific contribution of the session and it generalizes: any witness that cannot name a common enemy other than the hearer will be received as assault.

**Radical openness (Michael, endorsed by Charlie).** Michael's refusal to close any question — his objection to the "black box" that declares *it cannot be* — was identified by Charlie as the exact posture the evangelist must possess: complete openness and willingness to talk to anybody about anything, within which the Spirit of the conversation, what Charlie and Susan call revelation by conversation, can operate. The Jehovah's Witnesses who expect a shut door and find themselves in Michael's living room discussing theology are the demonstration. The analytical point is that openness here is not relativism; it is the entry condition for exchange. A closed man cannot be spoken to, and a closed evangelist cannot speak.

**Value demonstration (Michael).** Michael's second contribution answers Leonard's central question — "what's the value of believing as we believe?" — with a principle drawn from two secular sources and one dominical text. From Jobs: show them what they do not yet know they want, and when they see it, they will want it. From the film he cited: "tell me what you don't see" — elucidate the vision that is not yet there. And from the Sermon on the Mount, the enforcement clause: "by their fruits ye shall know them" (Matthew 7:20), with Michael's gloss that the principle runs broader than its false-prophet context — the life visibly lived is the biggest convincer, and a person only mildly interested in religion who sees something truly admirable says *I want some of that*. His summary formulation deserves the record verbatim in substance: we are not conquering them; we want them with us, not under us — join the celebration, not submit to the conqueror. This is the Christian answer to coercion-based systems stated as method.

**Providential encounter (Leonard).** Already treated in Section III: availability, marked identity (the hat, the shirt), hospitality (the monthly potluck that fed and received the homeless man who walked in), and confidence that the Lord arranges the meetings. Leonard added the sower (Matthew 13) as the governing parable for the distribution problem: the seed goes everywhere, the soils sort themselves, and the modern network — the very medium the meeting was conducted on — is a global broadcast tool for seed. His charge to me was specific: boil the message down to the doctrine of Christ; make everything produced point to Him and not to us; and make it personal, because a message thrown out for everyone lands on no one.

The layering is now visible. Sanctification forms the witness (model one); the household proves and tempers him (model two); openness gets him into the conversation (model three); the demonstrated life and the articulated value give the conversation its content (model four); and providence supplies the appointments (model five). A method missing the first layer transmits an edifice; missing the last, it becomes campaign and steadies the ark. The five members were not disagreeing. They were each holding one section of a pipeline.

## V. The Reliability of the Text: Charlie's Workstream

Charlie disclosed a private research program of direct consequence to the group. Provoked by the Mormon axiom that the Bible is the word of God only "as far as it is translated correctly" — a formulation he rightly identified as a monkey wrench thrown into the text's authority, a seed of doubt that licenses the supplement — he has undertaken a study of textual criticism to answer the question on the evidence: how trustworthy is the transmitted text? His current exercise is exemplary in design: reading the Great Isaiah Scroll of the Dead Sea corpus — a complete Isaiah predating Christ, effectively the Isaiah the Lord Himself quoted — in a modern scholarly translation (the Dead Sea Scrolls translation team member he named is almost certainly Donald W. Parry of the scrolls editorial project) while listening in parallel to the King James, chapter by chapter. His finding through seven chapters: the texts are extremely close. Leonard corroborated from direct observation, having seen the scroll exhibited, and having judged the same.

The significance should be stated plainly. This is precisely the grounds-work that last week's essay argued produces courage — not exhortation to boldness, but evidence that removes the reason for timidity. A two-thousand-year-old manuscript matching the received text collapses the "translated correctly" escape hatch empirically, one chapter at a time. It also bears directly on the standing disagreement Charlie and Leonard named openly: Charlie holds that the Book of Mormon contradicts the Bible; Leonard holds the two testify hand in hand; and Leonard, to his great credit, asked to see Charlie's notes of the differences rather than deflecting the challenge. Armond asked to see them as well. That exchange — a disputed claim, a request for the evidence, an agreement to deliver it — is the fellowship's epistemic discipline working exactly as designed, and it must not be allowed to evaporate. The action item is recorded in Section VIII.

Leonard's counter-position was also given its full statement: that the received Old Testament excludes material the prophets possessed, that the pre-advent prophets knew the Name, and that the Joseph Smith Translation — printed at last by his covenant Christian community — restores the fullness of the Scriptures. I record the claim as held, note that it is load-bearing for his tradition and contested by the rest of the table, and mark it as the natural subject of the future session that Charlie's notes will convene. The Isaiah exercise gives that future session its method: claims about textual corruption and restoration are checkable against manuscripts, and the fellowship checks.

## VI. Two Deeper Currents: Jeroboam's Method and the Covenant of Immediacy

Susan contributed two arguments that operate beneath the method question and deserve separate statement.

The first arrived through a dream and resolved into 1 Kings 12. Jeroboam, fearing the loss of his kingdom if the people returned to Jerusalem to worship, manufactured a more convenient religion: shrines closer to home, a priesthood open to any man rather than the Levites, festival dates moved — each change flattering, each change welcomed, each change a departure from the commandment, and the whole package retained *because the people grew to like it*. Susan's application: the mechanism is not ancient. Governments and cultures cause the problem, provide the solution, and collect the dependency; and the believer raised inside the arrangement inherits convictions that feel normal and right and are contrary to the Word — discoverable as such only by the personal combing of the pages that she described, with the intensity of the treasure-hunter of Proverbs 2:4. Her methodological corollary is important for our purposes: this discovery cannot be forced on others by public campaigns against particular errors, which merely raise opposition; it is produced person by person, as a byproduct of the relationship with God opening the individual's eyes. The Jeroboam analysis thus lands as a sixth confirmation of the sanctification-first model — the log in the eye is, in many cases, a culturally installed log, and only the Word removes it.

The second current answered Leonard directly. Leonard's stated reason for his path is "more — more light, more knowledge, more understanding," and his tradition locates the more in latter-day revelation through a prophet. Susan's reply was a covenant-theology argument of real force: the arrangement in which the people receive the word through a prophet because they cannot approach God themselves is the *old* covenant arrangement — the arrangement Israel chose at Sinai when they refused the direct encounter — and the new covenant is constitutionally better on exactly this point: "let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:16). Every believer is invited into direct revelatory relationship; the edification of the body then flows from each member's firsthand walk rather than from a single spiritual virtuoso heard at second hand. Leonard himself supplied the confirming observations: God wanted it for the Israelites and they refused it; and the Son of God Himself withdrew to pray in almost every chapter of the Gospels — "if the Son of God has to go off and pray to his Father, what's stopping us?" On this the whole table stood together, whatever remains disputed about prophets: the more that Leonard rightly hungers for is not structurally locked behind an intermediary. It is offered at the throne.

## VII. Baptism in the Name: The Commission's Content

My own contribution closed the circle to last week's essay. The commission commands baptism "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" (Matthew 28:19), and the ordinary reading — water, administered once, formula pronounced — is not wrong but is not exhaustive. In the Hebrew and Greek frame, the *name* is not a label; it is the character, the nature, the who-someone-is. To baptize is to immerse. The commission read at depth is therefore a command to immerse the nations *in the character of God* — an ongoing condition, not a punctiliar rite. Charlie immediately identified the parallel I intended: this is the Baptist's own distinction — "I indeed baptize you with water... he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire" (Matthew 3:11). My testimony on this point is autobiographical: I spent a season of my search pursuing the dramatic single event — the Pentecostal experience, the decisive fire — and the resolution I now hold is that the baptism of fire is infinitely expansible, available moment by moment, an immersion in the Spirit's guidance renewable at will rather than a threshold crossed once and either possessed or missed. This reading also dissolves an old wound in Christendom: the sprinkling-versus-immersion division that has split communions loses its force when the water rite is understood as the sign of an immersion that is actually in the Name.

The systematic statement, which the discussion forced into clarity: there are two mission fields because there are two phases of the one salvation. Justification is the initiation — the short game, the entry, the moment the person says *I want to make Jesus my Lord*. Sanctification is the long game — the immersion, the daily baptism of fire, the mission field of the self. Evangelism as commonly conceived addresses only the first and then abandons the convert at the font. The commission as actually written — teach all nations, baptizing them into the Name, teaching them to observe all things — assigns us both.

## VIII. Findings and the Way Forward

What can we learn; what works; what follows. I state the conclusions of the session as findings, and the commitments as actions.

**Finding 1.** The science objection is answerable at the level of the Hebrew text, without concession and without contested artifacts. The *yom*-as-period argument should become standard equipment for every member; the anomalous-artifact literature should not.

**Finding 2.** The Great Commission binds every believer — the restrictive apostolic reading fails against Acts 8:4, 2 Corinthians 5, and 1 Peter 3:15 — but the providential-encounter constraint survives: the Lord arranges; we do not steady the ark.

**Finding 3.** The five models are one pipeline: sanctification forms the witness, the household proves him, openness admits him to the conversation, demonstrated value gives the conversation content, providence supplies the appointments. The group's method question is answered not by choosing among the members' positions but by ordering them.

**Finding 4.** Message discipline is the non-negotiable, certified by Charlie's missionary testimony from inside the alternative: everything produced must point to Christ and His doctrine, not to the group, the theory, or the founder. He must increase; we must decrease (John 3:30).

**Finding 5.** The fellowship itself is the sanctification forum the culture lacks. The session surfaced honestly the question of whether a mixed forum is safe for confession-depth sharing; Susan's observation that the deepest sharing occurs in same-sex small groups within larger bodies is the likely design answer if the group grows.

**Actions.** First, the essay distribution schedule changes: Charlie's Saturday is a workday, and a Saturday delivery guarantees an unread essay; the preview will go out early in the week, targeted Monday, which requires drafting a week ahead — accepted. Second, Charlie delivers his notes on the Book of Mormon–Bible differences to Leonard and Armond as promised, and the material is queued as the subject of a structured future session under the fellowship's evidence disciplines, with the Isaiah-scroll method as the model for how such claims get tested. Third, Charlie's textual-criticism findings will be brought back to the group as they mature; the Great Isaiah Scroll comparison is exactly the grounds-work the evangelization essay called for and should be shared, not private. Fourth, Michael's proposal that the forum extend its reach through YouTube is registered as a live infrastructure question — the sower's field is now global, and the parable instructs us to sow broadly and let the soils sort — with the message-discipline finding above as its precondition: we broadcast nothing that points at us. Fifth, each member carries the household assignment: the nearest field first, Ephesians 6:12 stated aloud as the frame, the log addressed before the mote.

The session began by asking how to share the word and ended by discovering that every member already held a piece of the answer, and that the pieces fit. That is what a fellowship is for.

*But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.* — James 1:22
