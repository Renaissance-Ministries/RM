### 3. Pricing Settled at $12,000 MSRP (Working Number)
Thomas acknowledged $16,000 is "optimistic" and not competitive. $12,000 is where other players are. Working economics at $12K:
- Medicare pays 75% = $9,000
- DME takes 30% = $2,700
- Ideomotion gets = $6,300
- Minus COGS ($3,500) = **$2,800 profit per unit**

If selling through someone else's DME (not owning one): Ideomotion wholesale at $6,000, making ~$2,500 per unit after COGS.

### 4. Buyback Program (Joelle's Idea)
Sell direct now with financing. When Medicare/DME is ready, offer to **buy back the used unit at $6,000 (wholesale)**, give the customer a new prescribed unit through Medicare, then refurbish and resell the returned unit for $9,000. Thomas: "That is thinking outside the box."

## Notable Ideas

**DME as investment:** If it doesn't work for selling walkers, hold it 36 months and sell the enrollment for $1M+. Zero downside.

**Ambassadors:** Direct-sale customers become ambassadors to their own doctors. They have a warm market — "I got this thing, it's fantastic, you should know about it so you can tell other people."

**Medicare annual wheelchair re-prescription:** Joelle's friend has to take her wheelchair-bound son to a doctor every year to get the wheelchair re-prescribed for Medicare. This means there's a **specific type of doctor who sees wheelchair patients annually** — that's a targetable prescriber population.

**Anti-kickback note:** Thomas shared story about Margo receiving a $4,000 drug kickback check despite never prescribing drugs. Confirms kickbacks happen but are illegal (Anti-Kickback Statute). Don't go there.
