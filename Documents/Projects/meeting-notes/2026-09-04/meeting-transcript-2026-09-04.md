## Part 4 — Non-Device Path Analysis (~7:19–7:21)

**Isak:** *(reading Claude analysis)* The test isn't the product — it's the claims. Registration is required of anyone who manufactures a "device," and "device" is defined by intended use. Something intended for diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease, or intended to affect the structure or function of the body.

A treadmill sold for fitness isn't a device. The same treadmill sold for gait rehabilitation is.

To be a non-device, everything below has to go: any mention of disability, mobility impairment, spinal cord injury, stroke, any condition, prescription, clinics, therapists, rehab, hospitals...

It's basically cutting our legs off and then putting us in a walker.

Dad's pitch — "if you've been in a wheelchair, let me show you how to walk" — that's the mitigation claim in plain language.

We'd also give up HSA and FSA eligibility, which requires the item to be medical. Any insurance path, now or later, gets given up.

## Part 7 — DME Supplier Deep Dive (~9:42–10:57)

**Thomas:** After Joelle left — the issue of whether there's anything that's a bridge too far on the medical approach, either Medicare or doing the DME. Both would be ideal. But there might be something that's just too much.

### DME Facility Requirements (from Claude analysis)

**Isak:** *(reading)* No retail showroom required. The regulation explicitly permits a closed-door business. Online-only doesn't work — there's no version that runs from a laptop. But the useful carve-out: locations used solely as warehouses need no separate enrollment, bond, or accreditation. That's a meaningful simplification for a manufacturer.

Full checklist: 200 square feet minimum, public access, staffed during posted hours, signage, record space, minimum 30 hours/week open to public, cannot share location with another Medicare supplier, landline telephone (no cell as primary), must permit unannounced CMS inspections.

### Cost Analysis

**Isak:** One-time roughly $9,500 to $25,000. Annual $50,000 to $78,000 — staff, rent, compliance, accreditation. Break-even is roughly 30 units a year, about 2.5 a month. Below that, the dealer is cheaper. Above it, self-enrollment starts to pay. Current volume is near zero.

### Moratorium

**Isak:** There was a nationwide DME POS enrollment moratorium February 27 through August 27, 2026. It expired — CMS decided not to extend it. The door is open. But it can come back — the authority runs in 6-month increments.

### DME Acquisition

**Isak:** There's an active market on dmelisting.com. Two types:
- Brand new shell companies at $175K — clean PTAN, zero billing history. But under 36 months, buying majority control triggers fresh enrollment. Defeats the purpose.
- Operating businesses at $300K — e.g., Van Nuys, LA. 15 years operating, licensed, accredited, Medicare + Medi-Cal, 40+ HMO/PPO contracts. Some of those HMOs no longer award contracts to new suppliers.

The acquisition target you want is an old, boring, long-enrolled operator — not a new clean one. Asset purchases don't transfer enrollment at all — must be equity deal.

**Thomas:** So really, if you're going to buy a business, you have to do all the due diligence on whether this is a real business. Why are they selling it?

### Medi-Cal Pricing Analysis

**Isak:** *(reading)* Medicare publishes fee schedule rates — take it or leave it. For listed codes like E0135, E0149, there are set allowables. Trap is the "least costly alternative" — unjustified features on a walker get paid at E0143 allowance regardless of what the device costs.

For miscellaneous codes, no published rate. It's individual consideration. For codes with no maximum allowable rate, reimbursement is the lowest of several ceilings: usual charges to general public, net purchase price plus 100% markup, acquisition cost plus state markup, a percentage of MSRP documented by catalog, or 80% of Medicare rural fee schedule for same or similar item.

**If DHCS deems a lifting walker "same or similar" to a walker code, the ceiling is a few hundred dollars, and no amount of MSRP documentation helps.**
