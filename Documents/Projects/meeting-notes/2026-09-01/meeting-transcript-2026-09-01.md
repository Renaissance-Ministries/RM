## 7. Competitor Analysis Review (~7:50–8:21)

**Thomas:** What is it that Rifton, Biodex, and the others — what's their angle on making their product desirable?

**Isak:** *(reading from Claude Code analysis)*

**Rifton:** Designed for dignity. Leads with human stories, independence, inclusion. Strong pediatric focus. Only company covering pediatric to adult with one product family. Bluetooth scale with mobile app for tracking weight-bearing progress. US-made. Heavy educational content, therapist video testimonials, insurance navigation help, free shipping. No e-commerce — phone/email sales.

**Biodex:** Biomechanical precision and clinical data — the "science company." Facilities only. Patented 4-bar and 6-bar linkage systems that emulate natural sit-to-stand kinematics. 70+ years of clinical brand credibility. Targets Safe Patient Handling laws in 11 states as market driver. Price ~$5,900.

**Mobility Research (HugN-Go / LiteGait):** 3-in-1 home viability — ambulation, sit-to-stand, and seated transfers in one compact device. Both facilities and home users. Small enough for home doorways and bathrooms. One-caregiver operation. Rechargeable battery. Price quote-gated, estimated $7,000–$12,000.

**Arjo (Hi-Lo Walker):** Outcomes-based ecosystem selling — not just a device but a full Safe Patient Handling & Mobility program. Enterprise facilities exclusively. Mobility Gallery — a 5-level patient assessment framework. Facilities adopt the framework, then need Arjo equipment to implement it. Consultative lock-in strategy. Price $6,000–$12,000.

**Bure (Rise & Go):** Cost-effective 3-in-1 — replaces separate hoists and walkers. Facilities primarily. Patented power rise. Electrically driven frame widening (26"–42") to fit around beds and wheelchairs — unique feature nobody else has. Swedish hospital clinical validation. Price $7,000–$9,000.

**Key insight from analysis:** Rifton's content marketing at industrial scale. Education center holds sample letters of medical necessity, funding guide workbooks, evaluating forms, competency checklists, in-service videos, customer story roundups, and a newsletter. Their Pacer LMN page explicitly explains that each unique feature must be separately justified as medically necessary.

**Arjo:** Trademarked both "Positive Eight" and "Mobility Gallery." The Mobility Gallery sorts residents into levels A–E with names and personas, and every suitable brochure states which persona it serves. The Sara Flex brochure literally says "suitable for Carl." The effect is the sales conversation stops being "do you want a lift?" and becomes "what is your resident mix and where are the gaps?"

**Mobility vs transfer — the real split:** Transfer products are sold on caregiver injury and liability. The buyer is a risk manager or facility administrator. The argument is workers' comp, OSHA zero-lift policy, staff injury rates — a cost-avoidance calculation, not a clinical one. Mobility and gait products are sold on patient outcome and funding justification. The buyer is the therapist acting as prescriber with a payer or IEP behind them.

**Biodex shows you can take the caregiver safety benefit without taking the transfer device identity.** Their copy says "no patient lifting required, mitigates injury to staff, reduces risk of falls" — while the product is still named "Mobility Assist" and described as a stand assist that leads into ambulation. That is precisely the needle we need to thread.

**Rifton collapsed their transfer device (TRAM) into their gait trainer line (E-Pacer) in June 2025.** They moved toward our positioning, not away from it.
