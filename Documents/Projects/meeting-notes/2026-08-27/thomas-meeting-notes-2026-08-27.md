### 1. Where the business stands

Ten Traveler lift-walkers are within a couple of weeks of final assembly and testing. Total investment to date is roughly $300,000–$400,000 (parts, ~$56,000 of development labor at $1,400/week over ~10 months, ten motors at $5,500, patents, Cal Poly sponsorships). The foregone return on that capital is about $24,000/year at a 6% money-market benchmark — the real cost of every year the business idles. Structure: Thomas as active venture partner at 50/50; operating agreement drafted and awaiting signatures.

Unit economics (working figures): COGS ≈ $2,980 per walker as counted on the call; round to **$3,500–$4,000** until soft-goods labor is priced. Historic selling price was $6,000. The Redding school offered $10,000 fifteen years ago and was blocked only by the absence of liability insurance.

Competitive landscape (from Isak's research): Rifton advertises a ~$5,670 base and ships a fully equipped adult unit at ~$10,225 — accessories are roughly 45–49% of their revenue. Mobility Research sells the LiteGait 700 at ~$21,000, with models ranging toward $38,000. Their business model is a cheap-looking base, expensive accessories, and parts revenue from components designed to break. Ours is the opposite: built to last, minimal plastic, jet-fighter-grade switches, no planned obsolescence.

### 2. The two prime directives

Everything below serves these two, in this order.

**Directive 1 — Bank the inventions.** Every product, invention, and accessory in Charlie's head gets converted to drawings and CAD files in the repository. This is the asset that makes the business survivable for a century and survivable of any one man's death. Thomas's standard: whether we sell another walker before this is done is less important than getting it done.

**Directive 2 — Sell the ten.** Convert the ten built walkers into revenue within roughly 60 days, establishing price, invoice, pipeline, and process along the way. The first ten sales are the template for the next hundred.

### 5. Phase 2 — The AI-first marketing engine (Months 2–4)

The founding principle, learned from the failed P-company venture: **no employee sprawl.** That business sold plenty of product and died anyway, because headcount ate the capital. Ideomotion runs on minimal staff, maximal AI, and (when available, perhaps within a year or two) robots for packing and shipping. Before any hire, the question is: can AI or process do this?

1. **Video library first — everything else hangs on it.** The AI assistant's entire job is walking prospects through videos, so the videos are the foundation: unboxing, setup, initial patient fitting, gait training, transfers (table, toilet, bed), maintenance, and patient-progress stories. Production method: Charlie and Isak talk it through on a recording; convert the conversation to a shot list; shoot in the shop. [Isak, Charlie]
2. **Email marketing, fully CAN-SPAM compliant.** Unsolicited commercial email is legal with: accurate headers and sender identity, no deceptive subject lines, clear identification as advertising, a valid physical postal address, a working unsubscribe honored within 10 business days. Violations run to ~$50,000 per email, so compliance is built into the template, not bolted on. Totally straight up. The list: the several hundred thousand physical therapists in the U.S., segmented and contacted at intervals — each touch carrying *news* (what's happened since last contact), never a repeat of the same message. [Isak]
3. **Social presence.** Clips and shorts for Facebook, TikTok, and Instagram; follow and engage the mobility community; build the account before we need it. [Isak]
4. **Virtual assistant strategy.** Near term: trial a commercial VA service (~$5,000/month — the cost of "3.3 employees," which we cannot yet justify on workload alone) *with intent to learn*, i.e., to discover what a mature VA offering includes before building our own. Decision point at 60–90 days: continue, cancel, or begin in-house build. The in-house VA's spec is already known: present the video curriculum, answer from the recorded-call knowledge base, and hand hot prospects to the human line. [Isak proposes; Thomas decides on spend]
5. **The training corpus.** Every recorded Charlie call feeds the database. The explicit goal: an AI that sells the way Charlie sells — 38 years of conviction that the only question is *when do you get one*, not *whether you need it*. Fifteen units a month at Mobility Research is the benchmark of what that voice can do. [Isak]
6. **Future sales hire (only when call volume demands it).** Salaried (~$78,000-class) rather than 30–40% commission; hourly until it's a real position; a small ~1% closing bonus for care and incentive. Not before the AI funnel proves volume. [Decision deferred]

Custom development is quoted at a premium (~$15,000-class, honestly framed as invention on demand); if declined, the sale proceeds with the standard configuration and the idea enters R&D anyway — then we tell the customer when it exists. [Charlie → Leonard → contract mfr; Isak tracks]
