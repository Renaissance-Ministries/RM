# 261115 - Abortion as Demonic Ritual and Child Sacrifice

# Modern Demonic Rituals and the Embrace of Human Sacrifice in Trump's America

*Reflections on Tucker Carlson's interview with abolitionist attorney Bradley Pierce*

**Thomas Lee Abshier, ND** November 15, 2026

Resource: interview transcript — [Tucker Carlson and Bradley Pierce on abortion (Sing Ju Post transcript)](https://singjupost.com/?p=65165)


---


## I. Where I Stand

I stand with Bradley Pierce. Abortion is the intentional killing of an innocent human being, and Scripture's name for killing children for the parents' benefit is *child sacrifice*. I hold that the United States, in 2026, still practices it at a scale few nations in history have matched, and that it does so under a president elected largely by people who believed he would help end it. I also hold that the pro-life establishment, which has done much good, has for decades worked against the one kind of law that would actually treat abortion as what it is.

I will make that case below. There are places where the evidence is incomplete, where the interview overstated its facts, and where sincere Christians disagree about penalties. I name each of them. None of them moves the center: a child in the womb is a child, God made him, and a nation that kills its children by the million cannot expect to escape the consequences.

Tucker Carlson's interview with Bradley Pierce, president of the Foundation to Abolish Abortion and of Abolish Abortion Texas, is one of the clearest public conversations on this subject I have heard. Pierce is a constitutional attorney who has drafted equal-protection bills in dozens of states. He is also, as the end of the interview reveals, the father of twelve children with a thirteenth on the way. He speaks as a lawyer, a Christian, and a father, and those three voices agree with each other. That agreement is itself part of his argument.

## II. Why I Use the Words "Sacrifice" and "Demonic"

I chose the title deliberately, and I owe the reader a reason for it, because a strong word must be proportioned to the thing it names.

Very few women who take an abortion pill think of themselves as worshiping anything. There are no altars, priests, or incantations in a telehealth consultation. If "ritual" meant only candles and chants, the word would be hyperbole. But that is not what makes a sacrifice a sacrifice. A sacrifice is the giving up of a life in exchange for something the giver wants: safety, prosperity, favor, freedom from a burden. Carlson put it in one line early in the interview: human sacrifice is always done in exchange for power. The ancient Canaanite gave his child to Molech hoping for rain, harvest, and victory. The modern parent gives up the child hoping for an education finished, a career kept, a relationship preserved, a future unencumbered. The god has changed its name. The exchange has not.

A ritual is a repeatable, socially sanctioned act with its own liturgy. Abortion has that too. The liturgy is euphemism: "health care," "reproductive freedom," "terminating a pregnancy," "my body, my choice." Each phrase is designed so that the participant does not have to look at what is being done. Scripture warns against exactly this: *"Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness"* **(Isaiah 5:20)**.

As for "demonic," that word is Scripture's, not mine. The psalmist, looking back on Israel's fall into Canaanite practice, wrote: *"Yea, they sacrificed their sons and their daughters unto devils, and shed innocent blood, even the blood of their sons and of their daughters, whom they sacrificed unto the idols of Canaan: and the land was polluted with blood"* **(Psalm 106:37–38)**. Pierce made the same point: the idols are demons, and the demons call for blood. I do not claim that every woman who aborts is consciously serving a demon. I claim that the practice serves the one who, in Jesus' words, *"was a murderer from the beginning"* **(John 8:44)**, whether or not the participants know whose work they are doing. Naming what God detests is not exaggeration. It is the truth stated at the size God states it.

## III. What the Interview Covered


### What abortion is

Carlson opened by asking Pierce to strip away the euphemisms. Pierce answered on two levels. Biologically, abortion destroys a distinct human life that has had its own DNA from fertilization and needs only what every human needs: oxygen, nourishment, and a safe place to grow. Spiritually, it is an attack on the image of God, because every human being is made in that image. Asked whether it differs from other murder, Pierce said it is worse: the victim is the most innocent person there is, killed in the place meant to be the safest, by the person he should most be able to trust. He added that the womb is the place Christ Himself chose to enter the world.

### The historical consensus

Pierce noted that the Hippocratic Oath, which medicine still invokes as its moral foundation, contains an explicit promise not to give a woman a pessary to cause abortion. Modern versions dropped the line and kept the phrase "do no harm." He described pagan Rome's practice of exposing unwanted infants, and the early Christians who rescued and adopted them. As the West was Christianized, the church fathers' judgment that abortion is murder became the settled law and conscience of Christendom for nearly two thousand years.

Asked how that consensus collapsed so fast, Pierce answered that whenever man is cut off from his Creator and redefined as a product of chance, his value falls with him. Carlson noted that one of the Soviet Union's early acts, alongside closing churches and killing priests, was to legalize abortion (it did so in 1920, the first modern state to do so). Pierce's summary was that humanism, by trying to make man his own god, ends up making him disposable.

### Why every ancient culture did it, and what God said about it

Carlson raised a question I find important. Human sacrifice, including the sacrifice of children, appears across the ancient world on every inhabited continent, among peoples who never met each other. It makes no evolutionary sense: a society that kills its healthy young is undermining its own survival. Why was it universal?

Pierce's answer was spiritual. Fallen man is bound to sin, and the one who rules sin loves to destroy God's image. Where people are lost, they take up their master's appetites.

He then walked through the Old Testament record. When God brought Israel into Canaan, He said He was driving out the inhabitants not because Israel was righteous but because the Canaanites were wicked. The lists of their sins vary, but child sacrifice appears in each. *"And thou shalt not let any of thy seed pass through the fire to Molech, neither shalt thou profane the name of thy God: I am the LORD"* **(Leviticus 18:21)**. *"For even their sons and their daughters they have burnt in the fire to their gods"* **(Deuteronomy 12:31)**. And the warning to Israel: *"Defile not ye yourselves in any of these things: for in all these the nations are defiled which I cast out before you: And the land is defiled: therefore I do visit the iniquity thereof upon it, and the land itself vomiteth out her inhabitants"* **(Leviticus 18:24–25)**.

Pierce traced the consequences through Israel's history. Solomon, led astray in old age, built a high place for Molech **(1 Kings 11:7)**, and God tore the kingdom from his son. Manasseh sacrificed his own son **(2 Kings 21:6)**, and Scripture says the Babylonian destruction came *"for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he did; And also for the innocent blood that he shed: for he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood; which the LORD would not pardon"* **(2 Kings 24:3–4)**. Josiah's reforms delayed the judgment but did not cancel it.

Carlson asked how any Christian or religious Jew could support abortion given these texts. Pierce answered that such a person must be either deceived or biblically illiterate, and that there are many pastors who either avoid the subject or teach falsely on it.

### National judgment

Carlson then asked whether Pierce sees a connection between abortion and the wars, disorder, and despair of the present age, and said on the record that he himself does. Pierce answered that God governs the affairs of men and judges nations as well as individuals: *"Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap"* **(Galatians 6:7)**. He pointed to Romans 1, where the refusal to honor God as Creator leads to God *"giving them over to a reprobate mind"* **(Romans 1:28)**, and suggested that the anxiety, depression, and confusion now called a "mental health crisis" fit that description. Carlson observed that whatever one thinks of the pharmaceutical response, it is plainly not working, and the cause may be deeper than individual psychology.

### Roe, Dobbs, and the numbers today

The two agreed that Roe v. Wade was a political decision dressed as constitutional law, that the "right to privacy" invented in Griswold had no real connection to abortion, and that Dobbs was right to overturn it. Carlson noted the irony that "my body, my choice" was abandoned by its own champions the moment COVID vaccine mandates arrived.

But Dobbs did not end abortion. Pierce cited over 1.1 million abortions in 2025, and the data confirm him: the Society of Family Planning's #WeCount project counted 1,126,760 abortions provided through the U.S. health-care system in 2025, more than before Dobbs. That figure excludes abortions with no licensed clinician involved, so the true number is higher. Twenty-eight to twenty-nine percent were done by telehealth by year's end, and in states with total bans, nearly all abortions are now pills mailed in under "shield laws" from clinicians in other states.

Pierce explained the mechanism. Thirteen states ban abortion providers, and others ban abortion after a heartbeat is detectable. But those laws restrain the doctor, not the act. Pills mailed from outside the state are a federal matter, because the mail is federal, and the federal government is not stopping them. He described the regimen: mifepristone to end the child's life, then misoprostol to expel him. He said his organization once ordered pills to see what came, and the instructions for pregnancies past thirteen weeks were simply to keep taking doses until the baby comes out, with no upper limit. He mentioned twins in Texas aborted this way at about twenty-one weeks.

### Trump's America

This is the part of the interview that gives my title its last words. Carlson asked plainly whether the current administration, with the White House, Supreme Court, and both chambers of Congress, has done anything about mailed abortion pills. Pierce said nothing substantial. His organization wrote to President Trump on the anniversary of his inauguration, calling on him to repent of statements favorable to abortion and listing actions he could take. Pierce recalled that after the 2022 midterms, Trump blamed Republican losses on being too strong on abortion; that the 2024 Republican platform was stripped and softened at his direction; and that he spoke favorably of Florida's abortion-rights amendment before backlash pushed him to a weak opposition. Carlson, going further, said Trump "loves abortion" and that pro-life voters do not want to hear it.

The central legal point is the Comstock Act. Passed in 1873 and still on the books, it forbids mailing any article intended to produce abortion. In December 2022 the Biden Justice Department issued an opinion saying the law applies only when the sender intends the drugs to be used unlawfully, which in practice makes it unenforceable. Pierce said the Trump administration has simply continued the Biden policy. Carlson asked whether a president can choose not to enforce a law. Pierce said yes, as long as Congress and the people do not hold him to account, and right now no one is.

### The pro-life movement against abolition

The most startling part of the interview concerned the pro-life movement itself. Carlson said he had rarely met a larger group of frauds than professional pro-life leadership, apart from some major exceptions, and that he says it with pain because he believes in the cause. Pierce gave the evidence.

In Texas, a 2003 law redefined an "individual" as a human being from fertilization, which by itself would have made abortion criminal homicide. But the same bill added Penal Code §19.06, which says the homicide chapter does not apply to the death of an unborn child caused by conduct of the mother (or to lawful medical procedures and lawfully prescribed drugs). Pierce said pro-life organizations wanted that exemption, pro-life Republicans passed it, and a pro-life governor signed it. In 2015, when a Texas legislator floated simply outlawing abortion, the state's pro-life groups told him they would not just decline to support such a bill. They would oppose it.

Louisiana in 2022 showed the same pattern. The Abolition of Abortion in Louisiana Act (HB 813) cleared committee 7–2 just as the Dobbs draft opinion leaked. Pierce's colleague Brian Gunter counted enough votes to pass it. Then, hours before the floor vote, a letter from roughly seventy national and state pro-life organizations, including National Right to Life and Susan B. Anthony Pro-Life America, landed on legislators' desks declaring that any measure criminalizing or punishing women is "not pro-life." The House adopted an amendment gutting the bill, 65–26.

The groups' reasoning is that the woman who aborts is a "second victim." Carlson and Pierce both agreed that women are often victims as well as perpetrators: lied to, pressured, abandoned. Pierce's answer is that being lied to does not excuse committing a crime, and that legally and morally a person can be both. Carlson added that law teaches. When something is illegal, many people simply will not do it, and when it is made legal, it becomes common. He admitted that his own views on drug criminalization had changed, after meeting recovering addicts who thanked God they were arrested. Pierce said many post-abortive women in the abolitionist movement say, through tears, that they wish it had been illegal, because then they would not have done it.

Equal-protection bills have now been filed in dozens of states, and Pierce said over 130 legislators signed onto the bills his group supported this year. Tennessee's 2026 bill drew national attention. Pierce also described a Georgia hearing where a Jewish legislator said her faith permits abortion, and he and Carlson discussed why Christians and religious Jews read the same Torah differently on this point (more on that in Section IX).

### The secular argument

Asked how he argues with a secular American, Pierce refused the usual strategy of setting the Bible aside. God's word is true whether or not the listener believes it, and the claim "my body, my choice" fails on its own terms: your body was made by God, and the child is not your body. Then he adds the science: from fertilization, a self-directing human organism with its own unique DNA. Carlson noted that the separate DNA alone ends the "my body" argument, and that since slavery is outlawed, one human being cannot own another. A parent is a steward of his child, not his owner. Carlson added that "human rights" is itself a Christian idea. Rights attach to you as a human because God made every human, not by race, faith, or usefulness. Without God, Carlson said, the only alternative authority left is the powerful: "God or Larry Fink."

Pierce's most unsettling observation was that some abortion advocates are no longer denying the child's humanity. They now concede the child is a human being and claim the right to kill him anyway. Carlson's response was that such a claim takes nerve, and that in his experience people on their deathbeds, past all self-deception, see clearly that taking a life is the gravest thing a person can do.

### Thirteen children

The interview ended with joy. Pierce, at 43, has six boys and six girls, born in alternating order, girl-boy, eleven times in a row, a pattern that may set a world record. That morning his wife had called to say a thirteenth child was coming. They live in a three-bedroom house. Visitors from Mozambique walked in and remarked on how big it was. Asked what he would say to couples afraid they cannot afford two children, he said that if God provides the children He will provide for them, and that Americans are the richest people in history. If we cannot afford children, none of our ancestors could have. Near tears, he recited the repeated blessing of Genesis, given to Adam, to Noah, and to Jacob: *"Be fruitful, and multiply"* **(Genesis 1:28)**.

## IV. The Question Under the Question

Behind the interview is a question it never quite asked out loud: **Why does a nation that, in its gut, knows abortion is killing keep doing it more than a million times a year, and why will even its pro-life leaders not say so in law?**

Carlson is right that almost no one can describe an abortion in plain terms at a dinner party without the room recoiling. The conscience knows. Yet the practice goes on, and those most opposed to it write laws that regulate its timing, its location, and its paperwork while leaving the act itself unpunished for the one who chooses it.

My answer is that we want the benefit of the sacrifice without owning the act. That is the logic of every sacrificial system. The worshiper did not think of himself as a murderer. He thought he was doing what was necessary, and the priest bore the ritual weight. Our laws are built the same way. The provider-ban model punishes the doctor (the priest) and absolves the parent (the worshiper). It lets everyone say, "We are against abortion," while the sacrifice continues by mail. Pierce's comparison was apt: no one proposes to regulate adultery by setting the hours and places at which it is permitted. When law treats a sin as a matter of circumstances, it has already conceded that the act itself is acceptable.

Politics follows the same logic. A party that wants pro-life votes without paying the cost of pro-life law will fund studies, issue proclamations, and point to the courts, but it will not use the power it has. The voter who accepts that arrangement shares in it.

## V. A Fair Accounting of the Trump Record

I do not know Donald Trump's heart, and I will not say, as Carlson did, that he "loves abortion." What I can judge is the record, and it has two sides.

**To his credit:** Trump appointed the justices who made Dobbs possible, ending a fifty-year constitutional lie. His administration has restored some pro-life funding policies, and his January 2026 Sanctity of Human Life Day message affirmed that every human being is endowed by God with the unalienable right to life.

**Against him:**


 	- The 2024 Republican platform, written by his campaign and approved 84–18 behind closed doors, dropped the decades-old call for a human life amendment and a national standard, and mentioned abortion once where the 2016 platform mentioned it thirty-five times.

 	- In January 2026 he told House Republicans to be "a little flexible" on the Hyde Amendment, which bars taxpayer funding of abortion.

 	- His FDA approved another generic mifepristone. The safety review promised in 2025 has produced no action. Commissioner Makary was removed in May 2026, partly over the slow pace, and in October the agency said it would not act until March 2027.

 	- When the Supreme Court considered Louisiana's challenge to mail-order mifepristone in May 2026, his administration filed nothing, and the Court left mailing in place.

 	- The Biden-era opinion that neutralizes the Comstock Act still stands. Attorney General Todd Blanche pledged at his July 2026 confirmation hearing to review it, and a coalition of pro-life groups has pressed him since. As of the latest reports I found, no change has been announced.


Here is my judgment. The administration treats the killing of over a million children a year as a political liability to be managed, not an injustice to be ended. That is the sin of omission, and in a president with the power to act, omission is a choice. *"Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin"* **(James 4:17)**. But "Trump's America" does not mean only Trump. It means a nation where both parties, the medical establishment, the pharmaceutical supply chain, many pulpits, and many voters have arranged things so that the sacrifice continues and no one has to own it. The president is the most visible participant, not the only one.

## VI. The Hard Question: Equal Protection and the Mother

The deepest disagreement in the interview is not between pro-life and pro-choice. It is between abolitionists and the pro-life establishment over whether the law should treat the mother who aborts as having committed homicide. I take this seriously because good people are on both sides, and because how the law treats a frightened, pressured woman is a question about love as well as justice.

**The strongest case for the establishment position** runs like this. Many women who abort are coerced by boyfriends, husbands, parents, or employers. Many have been told by doctors, teachers, and culture that the child is tissue. Prosecuting them would add a second tragedy to the first. It could make women afraid to seek help after a botched abortion. It could lead prosecutors to investigate grieving mothers after miscarriages. It would hand abortion advocates the image they most want, a woman in handcuffs, and could cost the movement the public support it needs to save any children at all. Tennessee's 2026 bill, which made abortion potentially eligible for the death penalty, was rejected in a House committee in March and attacked nationally for exactly these reasons. Its sponsor said he did not intend women to be executed, but the law said what it said.

These are real concerns, and some of them are wise. But they do not answer the central point. Under Texas §19.06 and laws like it, the exemption is not for the coerced, the deceived, or the desperate. It is blanket immunity for every mother who kills her unborn child, for any reason, at any stage. No other homicide law in America works this way. We do not exempt whole categories of killers because some of them were pressured. We let the facts of each case decide.

I apply my three questions to any law:


 	- **Is it righteous?** God's standard is impartial justice: *"Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honour the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour"* **(Leviticus 19:15)**. A law that protects a child from everyone except the person most likely to end his life is not impartial. The Fourteenth Amendment says the same thing in constitutional language: no state may deny any person the equal protection of the laws.

 	- **If so, is it loving?** It is loving to the child, whose life it protects. It is also loving to the mother, in the way Carlson and Pierce described: the law that forbids her act may be the thing that stops her from committing it and carrying it for the rest of her life. Humoring a neighbor in a deadly choice is not love. *"Thou shalt not hate thy brother in thine heart: thou shalt in any wise rebuke thy neighbour, and not suffer sin upon him"* **(Leviticus 19:17)**.

 	- **Would I want it done to me?** If I were the child, I would want the law to protect me from everyone. If I were the mother, measured not by my panic in the moment but by the self I would want to be across a lifetime and an eternity, I believe I would want a law strong enough to stop me.


So I stand on the side of equal protection: the same homicide law that protects a born child should protect an unborn child, with no class of killer exempted. **But equal protection does not mean equal sentences.** Our justice system already weighs coercion, duress, deception, diminished capacity, and remorse in every homicide. Prosecutors use discretion. Judges sentence according to culpability. Above all, juries stand as the conscience of the community. I regard the jury's power to refuse to convict where the law's application would be unjust as a cornerstone of American liberty. A frightened nineteen-year-old pressured into an abortion by the man who got her pregnant will meet twelve of her neighbors, and I trust them to see the whole person. I do not advocate the death penalty for such a mother. What I advocate is that the law stop pretending her child is not a person.

To any woman reading this who has had an abortion: the purpose of naming the sin is not to crush you. It is the first step toward the freedom Christ purchased. *"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness"* **(1 John 1:9)**. Many of the most powerful voices in the abolitionist movement are women who have walked that road.

## VII. God Feels Every Life

There is one more reason this matters to me, and it goes beyond what Pierce or Carlson said.

I hold that God does not watch human life from outside, as a judge watching a trial. He experiences every person's life from the inside. We are, in a real sense, God's experience of the world. That is why He commands us as He does. His law is not arbitrary. It is the description of what does not wound Him, because He lives our joys and our injuries along with us.

If that is true, every abortion is felt by God twice: once in the child, whose life He was living and whose development He was knitting together—*"For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb"* **(Psalm 139:13)**—and once in the mother, whose fear, pressure, and later grief He also carries. The child is not merely His creation. He calls the sacrificed children His own: *"Moreover thou hast taken thy sons and thy daughters, whom thou hast borne unto me, and these hast thou sacrificed unto them to be devoured…** That thou hast slain my children"* **(Ezekiel 16:20–21)**. *"Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee"* **(Jeremiah 1:5)**. John the Baptist *"leaped in [his mother's] womb for joy"* **(Luke 1:44)** in the presence of the unborn Christ.

On this view, a nation that kills a million children a year is not only breaking a rule. It is wounding the One who lives within each of them. That is why I take the warnings of national judgment seriously, and why I believe repentance, not strategy, has to come first.

## VIII. Why the Concern of National Judgment Remains Plausible

I cannot prove with statistics that America's troubles are God's judgment for abortion. No study can measure that, and I will not pretend otherwise. But I believe the concern is plausible, and not because I am looking for signs. Here is why.

**The intuitive sense of wrongness.** Carlson is right that no one can describe an abortion plainly without revulsion, whatever their politics. A practice that requires that much euphemism to survive is one the conscience is resisting. A people that silences its conscience on the gravest question will find it harder to hear that conscience on every other question.

**The money.** Abortion is an industry: pill manufacturers overseas, telehealth providers, shield-law networks, the organizations that fund them, and the political machinery built around them. Where money flows from killing, there is a standing incentive to keep the killing going and to keep the language clean.

**The links between doctrine, system, law, and behavior.** The doctrine is that man is self-made and his body is his own. The system is a medical and pharmaceutical apparatus that accepts that doctrine. The law, both the shield laws in blue states and the mother-exemptions in red ones, protects the system. The behavior follows: abortions rose after Dobbs, not fell, because the law was aimed at providers while the doctrine and the demand went untouched. Romans 1 describes exactly this sequence: the refusal to acknowledge the Creator, then a darkened mind, then behavior that no longer sees itself.

**The biblical pattern.** Scripture repeatedly ties the shedding of innocent blood to national judgment, from Canaan to Jerusalem. That a secular friend of Carlson's volunteered, unprompted, that America's troubles look like punishment for abortion suggests the intuition is not confined to the devout.

None of this is proof. It is a convergence of reasons, and I find it strong enough to act on.

## IX. Notes and Corrections

I want this essay to be trustworthy, so I note where the interview went beyond the evidence.
> **Note 1: Pills and doctors.** Pierce said 65–80% of abortions are now self-induced with "not a doctor" involved. Most medication abortions are still prescribed by a licensed clinician, in person or by telehealth, even though the woman takes the pills at home. Medication abortion made up about 63% of U.S. abortions in 2023, and telehealth about 28–29% by the end of 2025. Pierce's underlying point holds: in most abortions today, the mother herself performs the final act. But the "no doctor" figure overstates it.

> **Note 2: The Torah and Jewish law.** Carlson said the Torah plainly prohibits abortion. The Molech passages forbid sacrificing born children. The Torah's only direct text on fetal death, Exodus 21:22, prescribes a fine when a pregnant woman is struck and miscarries, and Jewish tradition built its law on that verse. The Mishnah (Oholot 7:6) requires ending a pregnancy to save the mother's life until the child's head emerges, and authorities differ widely beyond that case, from near-prohibition to considerable permission. The Jewish legislator in Georgia was drawing on a real, long-standing tradition, not ignorance of her faith. The Christian case against abortion rests on firm ground—the image of God **(Genesis 9:6)**, God's knowledge of the unborn **(Psalm 139; Jeremiah 1:5)**, and the Incarnation itself—but we should state it from those texts rather than claim the Torah says something it does not say in so many words.

> **Note 3: Tennessee.** Pierce said the planned committee hearing on Tennessee's 2026 bill "didn't end up happening." Contemporary reporting says a House committee took it up and rejected it in March 2026, and supporters plan to bring it back in 2027. I could not verify the polling figures Pierce cited (16% among conservatives, then 24% in a Vanderbilt poll) and treat them as his report.

> **Note 4: Who wrote the Texas exemption.** Texas Penal Code §19.06, enacted in 2003, does exempt the mother's conduct from the homicide chapter. Pierce's statement that pro-life organizations put that language in is his firsthand account, which I have not independently confirmed. I treat it as a serious lead, consistent with the open opposition of those same organizations to equal-protection bills since.

> **Note 5: Human sacrifice abroad.** Carlson said in passing that human sacrifice is still common in Haiti and Africa. Ritual killings are documented in some places, but I know of no evidence that the practice is "common" across those regions, and I do not adopt the claim.

> **Note 6: Trump "loves abortion."** This was Carlson's judgment, not a fact established in the interview. The record in Section V supports the conclusion that the administration treats abortion as a political liability. It does not tell us what is in the president's heart.


## X. What I Would Do

I offer these as a position, held firmly and open to a better argument:


 	- **Use the bully pulpit.** A president who said plainly, every month, that a child in the womb is a child and that killing him is wrong would change the national conversation more than any study. Leadership is voice before it is policy.

 	- **Enforce the law already on the books.** Direct the Attorney General to withdraw the 2022 opinion that rewrote the Comstock Act, and enforce the statute as Congress wrote it, evenly, against manufacturers and distributors who ship abortion drugs through the mail. A president does not get to choose which laws are inconvenient.

 	- **Call for equal protection.** Urge every state to repeal the mother-exemption provisions in its homicide laws and extend the same protection to the unborn that it gives the born. Ask Congress to use its power under Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment to secure equal protection for all persons from fertilization.

 	- **Build the love that makes the law bearable.** Equal protection must come with real help: churches opening homes to mothers, adoption made simple and affordable, fathers held responsible for supporting their children, and pastors willing to preach both the gravity of the sin and the completeness of Christ's forgiveness.

 	- **Repent first.** Before any of this, the church has to stop treating abortion as one political issue among many and grieve over it as God does. Judgment begins at the house of God **(1 Peter 4:17)**.


## XI. Conclusion

The interview's last image is its best argument: a father, choked up, describing a full house in a small home, and a wife praying for one more child. *"Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward"* **(Psalm 127:3)**. The ancient world sacrificed its children to gods that promised prosperity and never delivered it. We are doing the same, with better euphemisms and a mail-order system, under leaders of both parties who prefer the arrangement to the cost of ending it.

The way out is the same as it always was: tell the truth about what we are doing, stop it by law, care for the mothers and the children, and turn back to the God who made us all and lives in every one of us. *"If thou forbear to deliver them that are drawn unto death, and those that are ready to be slain; If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not; doth not he that pondereth the heart consider it?"* **(Proverbs 24:11–12)**. We do know it. The question is what we will do now.

**Limits of these sources.** The abortion counts come from the Society of Family Planning, an organization that supports abortion access. Its numbers are the best national count available and are used by both sides, but they exclude abortions with no licensed clinician involved, so the true total is higher. Much of the reporting on the Trump administration comes from mainstream outlets and from advocacy groups on both sides, each of which has a reason to describe the record in a particular way. Official statements from the FDA and Justice Department are the testimony of interested parties, not neutral findings. Pierce's accounts of private conversations with Texas pro-life groups and legislators are firsthand reports I could not independently confirm, and I treat them as leads. I did not find independent records of his organization's January 2026 letter to the president or the polling figures he cited. Where the public record is thin, curation by any of these sources could have hidden evidence on either side, and I have tried to say so wherever it matters.
