# 261118 - Satan's War Against the Jews

# The Title, Not the Character:


### Scott Lively on Satan's War Against the Jews

Thomas Lee Abshier, ND · Nov 18, 2026

## Introduction

I stand with Scott Lively on his central claim: the hatred of the Jewish people as a class is the accuser's work, and a Christian who joins it is fighting on the wrong side of a covenant God has not cancelled. I also think his case is stronger than the framework he hangs it on, and I want to separate the two.

Lively writes as a Christian attorney. His September 20 email, "Why Satan's Main Target Has Always Been the Jews," condenses a longer series. His thesis is that most of the Christian quarrel over "Zionism" is argued on the wrong docket. It treats a question of title (who owns the land promise, and under what instrument) as a question of character (whether the Jews, or the State of Israel, are good). Satan, in his telling, has always pursued the people who carry the promise. Christians who condemn the Jews as a whole, he says, are doing the prosecutor's job against defendants God has not dismissed.

This matters now because the argument among Christians has turned sharp. In 2026 the debate over Christian Zionism split Catholics and evangelicals in public, and a strand of the young right has openly embraced contempt for Jews. I want to weigh Lively's brief on its merits: what holds, what I question, and what I believe a Christian should conclude.

## What Lively Gets Right: Title, Not Character

Lively's best move is the lawyer's question. Before asking whether the Jews are good, he asks who holds title, under what instrument, and who has standing to cancel it. That question is the right one, and Scripture answers it plainly.

In Genesis 15, God alone passes between the pieces of the sacrifice while Abraham sleeps. In the covenant custom of the day, the one who walked that path took the penalty for breach on himself. God bound Himself, not Abraham. The writer of Hebrews draws the conclusion: *"For when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself"* **(Hebrews 6:13)**.

Paul applies the same logic to the Jews who had not believed in Jesus. He does not say they lost their standing. He says the opposite: *"As concerning the gospel, they are enemies for your sakes: but as touching the election, they are beloved for the fathers' sakes.* *For the gifts and calling of God are without repentance"* **(Romans 11:28–29)**. "Without repentance" means God does not take it back.

This is the point that settles the question for me. A promise God swore by Himself does not depend on the moral record of the people who hold it. Israel's prophets condemned Israel's sins in the harshest terms, yet they never said the promise was void. They said God would discipline His people and then restore them. If my theology requires God to have broken an oath He swore by His own name, I have put a flaw in the title I claim to share as an adopted heir.

## Why the Line of Promise Is the Target

Lively's second claim is that Satan's malice toward the Hebrews began when the promise was sworn, not at Calvary or in 1948. I find this persuasive, and the biblical record supports it.

The pattern runs through the whole story. Pharaoh ordered the Hebrew sons drowned. Haman plotted to destroy every Jew in the Persian Empire in a single day. Herod killed the infants of Bethlehem to reach one child. Lively reads these as attacks on the chain of title, not as random cruelty, and I agree. Each one aimed at the line through which the Redeemer would come.

Revelation gives the same picture in a vision. The dragon stands before the woman to devour her child, and when he fails, he turns on her and on the rest of her offspring. Scripture names him: *"for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night"* **(Revelation 12:10)**. An accuser does not need a people to be innocent in order to attack them. He needs them to be guilty of something, and then he argues that their guilt cancels what God promised. That is precisely the argument Lively says many Christians are now making about the Jews.

I would add one thing Lively does not say. Antisemitism has a peculiar persistence that ordinary hatreds lack. It has survived the end of every empire that practiced it, and it changes its stated reasons from age to age: Christ-killers, then poisoners of wells, then a race, then bankers, then colonizers. When the reasons keep changing and the target stays the same, I conclude that the reasons are not the cause. That is the intuitive sense behind Lively's thesis, and I share it.

## A Class, Not a Hive Mind

Lively makes a distinction I consider the most practically useful line in his email. God chose a class, he writes, not a hive mind. Scripture shows that class at its best and its worst: Abraham and the brothers who sold Joseph, David and the kings who filled Jerusalem with blood, the faithful remnant and the crowd before Pilate. To treat "the Jews" as one moral corporation is a category error.

The law of God says the same thing in its own words: *"The soul that sinneth, it shall die.* *The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son"* **(Ezekiel 18:20)**. Collective guilt by blood is what God forbids. The church learned this at terrible cost. For centuries, Christians blamed every Jew in every generation for the crucifixion, though Jesus Himself prayed for those who crucified Him: *"Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do"* **(Luke 23:34)**.

This principle cuts in two directions, and I want to state both.


 	- **It forbids** condemning Jews as a people because of what some Jews have done: the bankers, the revolutionaries of 1917, the authors of offensive passages in the Talmud, or the conduct of a war in Gaza. Each of these is a claim about particular persons or a particular government. None of them is a verdict on the class.

 	- **It permits** judging persons and acts. A Christian may judge a policy of the Israeli government by God's law, as he would judge his own government's. A title deed does not make every act of the titleholder righteous. Lively says as much: present possession of the land is not a referendum on present righteousness, and he points to Leviticus 18, where the land expels nations that defile it.


I think this second direction needs more emphasis than Lively gives it. Some Christians hear any defense of the Jewish people as a demand to approve everything the State of Israel does. When they refuse, they are told they are antisemites, and some of them then conclude the charge is meaningless. The honest line is the one Lively himself draws: judge persons, not the class. A critic who holds that line is not doing the accuser's work. A critic who crosses it is.

## A Question I Hold Open: The Two-House Frame

Lively calls himself a "Two-House Millennialist." Two-House theology holds that the ten northern tribes of Israel, scattered by Assyria, lost their identity among the Gentiles. In Lively's version, the church is that northern house restored: the divorced wife brought home through Christ. Judah, the Jews, remains a separate house whose national recognition of Jesus is still to come.

I respect the purpose of this frame. It explains why the church has not replaced Judah. The idea that the church has replaced Israel is called supersessionism, and Lively is right to reject it. But I do not think the Two-House frame is needed to reject it, and I see three difficulties with it.


 	- **The northern tribes were not entirely lost.** When the kingdom split, faithful members of the northern tribes moved south: *"And after them out of all the tribes of Israel such as set their hearts to seek the LORD God of Israel came to Jerusalem"* **(2 Chronicles 11:16)**. In the New Testament, Anna the prophetess is *"of the tribe of Aser"* **(Luke 2:36)**, a northern tribe. Paul speaks of *"our twelve tribes, instantly serving God day and night"* **(Acts 26:7)**. The Jews of Jesus' day already carried members of all twelve.

 	- **Paul calls the grafted branches Gentiles.** In the olive-tree passage he writes, *"For I speak to you Gentiles"* **(Romans 11:13)**. He does not tell them they are hidden Israelites rediscovering their birthright. He tells them they are wild branches grafted in by grace, and he warns them: *"Boast not against the branches.** But if thou boast, thou bearest not the root, but the root thee"* **(Romans 11:18)**.

 	- **The argument does not need it.** Everything Lively wants to prove, that the promise to the natural branches stands and Christians must not curse them, follows from Romans 9–11 alone. Tying that conclusion to a contested reading of tribal history makes it easier for an opponent to dismiss the whole case by attacking its weakest link.


I hold this as a disagreement over framework, not over the conclusion. On the conclusion, Lively and I stand together.

## Why the Concern Is Real Now

I believe Lively's warning is timely, not alarmist. The line between criticizing Israel and despising Jews is being crossed in Christian circles, and the evidence points that way.


 	- On January 17, 2026, the Patriarchs and Heads of Churches in Jerusalem issued a short statement calling Christian Zionism a "harmful and damaging ideology" ([townhall.com](https://townhall.com/author/print/2670429)). It set off a public quarrel among Catholics, Orthodox, and evangelicals over whether Christians may support Israel at all.

 	- In February 2026, a member of a federal religious-liberty commission said at a hearing on antisemitism that Catholics do not embrace Zionism, without defining the word, and was removed after the exchange ([Catholic Review](https://catholicreview.org/experts-debates-about-zionism-even-by-catholics-often-at-odds-with-catholic-understanding/)).

 	- On the young right, a movement openly hostile to Jews has kept its hold on a following, which observers at Baylor's Religion Watch noted had split conservatives ([Religion Watch](https://religionwatch.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2026/zionism-charges-antisemitism-dividing-american-right)).

 	- A July 2026 study of about 2,000 self-identified American Christians found anti-Zionist sentiment and antisemitic attitudes often go together, most strongly at the extremes ([Religions, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/17/7/829)). The authors note their data show association, not cause.


The question under the question is worth answering. Many Christian anti-Zionists are not reaching for hatred. They are reaching for real concerns: the suffering of Palestinian civilians, including Christians; suspicion of political money and lobbying; and discomfort with a kind of Christian Zionism that seems to approve whatever Israel does. Those concerns deserve honest answers.

But the concern Lively raises is plausible for reasons that go deeper than any one statistic. The doctrine of replacement, once accepted, supplies the theology: if God is finished with the Jews, their claims are void and their sins are unanswered. Grievance supplies the emotion. Social media rewards the most contemptuous voice. And money and power on every side give people motives to blur the line between a government and a people. Where doctrine, emotion, and incentive line up, history tells me what usually follows.

## What I Ask of Christians

Lively argues as a lawyer, and his legal argument is sound. I want to add the reason underneath the law.

I believe God lives inside every human life. He is not watching us from a distance; He experiences the world through each of us, from the inside. That is why He gave His law: not as an arbitrary set of rules, but because He feels what our choices do to one another. It is also why the Cross was necessary. God took into Himself the full weight of what our sin had done to the lives He shares.

If that is true, then contempt for the Jewish people is not only an error about a covenant. It is contempt aimed at the lives God is living. Every Jewish child taunted on a street, every family that hides its faith to stay safe, is a life God experiences from within. Jesus said it plainly: *"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me"* **(Matthew 25:40)**. He was born a Jew, and He never stopped being one.

The ancient promise to Abraham states the same principle in the language of blessing: *"And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse him that curseth thee: and in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed"* **(Genesis 12:3)**. I read that not as a magical charm for nations that vote with Israel, but as a description of moral reality. The one who curses the people of the promise sets himself against the Promiser.

So I ask three things of my fellow Christians.


 	- **Judge persons and acts, never the class.** Criticize a government's policy by God's law if you must, and criticize your own by the same standard.

 	- **Refuse the accuser's shortcut.** When an argument against the Jews depends on proving them unworthy rather than on what God actually promised, recognize whose argument it is.

 	- **Apply the neighbor test.** Ask whether your Jewish neighbor, hearing what you said about his people, would feel loved by you. If not, and if your words were about his people rather than his deeds, you have said something God did not authorize.


We Gentile believers were grafted into a tree whose root we did not plant. Heirs by adoption should not slander the family they were adopted into. Preach Christ to the Jews, as Paul did. But do it as a brother, not as a prosecutor.
