# A REASON TO ACT RIGHT — Book Outline & Skeleton

**Working title:** *A Reason to Act Right*
**Provenance:** Title proposed by Isak Gutierrez, 12 Aug 2026, in the
"void" conversation with Thomas Abshier — arising directly from
Thomas's own summary of the whole programme: *"We take Bible and we
tell story, and we have Conscious Point Physics and we tell a story,
and we look at society and interaction and feeling and psychology and
economics and politics, and we tell a story, and we go: You see, you
should act right."* Isak: *"You can just lay it out however you want
to, but that's kind of the intention."* This document lays it out.
**Status:** v0.1 skeleton for Thomas's review. Renaissance Ministries
book project; sibling to *Tetrahedrons All the Way Down* (TATWD).
**Prepared:** 13 Aug 2026.

---

## 1. What this book is

The apologetics-and-culture flagship of Renaissance Ministries: one
continuous argument that three independent stories — the story
physics tells, the story Scripture tells, and the story society tells
when it keeps or abandons its anchor — converge on a single
conclusion: there is a real reason, grounded in the structure of
existence itself, to get up each day and do the right thing.

It is NOT the physics book (that is TATWD), not a systematic
theology, and not a policy tract. It is the convergence book: the one
a reader can finish in a week and come away with a defensible answer
to "why be good?" that neither hand-waves the mystery nor hides the
physics nor moralizes without warrant.

**Thesis in one sentence:** The universe is a moral fact before it is
a physical one — the same Source that declares each Moment of
existence declares the difference between right and wrong, and a life
aligned with that declaration is the only one that fits reality.

## 2. The reader

Drawn from life — the transcript names them:

- **The honest hesitator** (the Charlie reader): intelligent,
  accomplished elsewhere, willing to say "well, I don't know," wary
  of hanging his hat on what might be a crackpot idea. He needs
  respect for his hesitancy, logical forks laid out cleanly, and
  permission to proceed on a wager rather than a proof.
- **The stubborn empiricist** (the Mike Sherman reader): believes
  there is always "a way of understanding," resists any claim of an
  in-principle mystery. He needs the regress argument done with full
  rigor — and the concession that the mystery cuts against every
  worldview equally, including his.
- **The seeking synthesizer** (the Isak reader): already senses that
  the universe itself is at least the higher power, wants direction
  more than persuasion, wrestles in good faith. He needs the bridge
  from "the universe is God's body" to "therefore my Tuesday
  matters."
- **The anchored believer** who has never had an answer for the
  skeptic at work: gets vocabulary and structure for what they
  already live.

Register throughout: the Rovelli/Scientific-American voice for
physics, the fellowship-table voice for theology — plain, warm,
unembarrassed. First person where it earns its keep (the vision, the
practice, the conversations).

## 3. Architecture at a glance

Prologue + five parts + epilogue; 22 chapters; ~78,000–88,000 words
(chapters 3,000–4,000 words; trade-book length). Each Part ends with
a one-page interlude titled **"The Reason, So Far"** — the argument's
running distillation, so a reader can always answer "what has been
established?" Discussion-question appendix makes every chapter a
fellowship session (Isak: "at the very least a good fellowship
discussion" — by design, exactly that).

## PART I — THE FLOOR OF THE MYSTERY (4 chapters, ~14,000 words)
*What can and cannot be known about why there is anything at all.*

**Ch. 1 — The Something.** The one datum no worldview escapes: a
universe exists. Every philosophy begins in media res. Depth: the
chapter does phenomenology, not physics — the sheer scandal of
existence. Ends by framing the honest question: something rather
than nothing — why?

**Ch. 2 — The Void, Pregnant and Barren.** The Kabbalah teacher's
distinction (Daniel Bloch): the nothing from which nothing can come
vs the fertile nothing that birthed a world. Why the distinction is
"almost moot, except for the idea" — and why the idea still does
work: it names the minimum miracle every worldview must buy. Depth:
comparative — ex nihilo traditions, quantum-vacuum evasions (and why
"the vacuum" is a something), the materialist's own pregnant void.

**Ch. 3 — Turtles All the Way Down.** The regress in full rigor: God
from the void, the void from the pre-void, the pre-void's paramour.
The elbow-in-the-ear reader gets his chapter: the regress is not a
puzzle awaiting cleverness; it is structural. AND the even-handed
turn: eternal matter, eternal law, eternal God — all three terminate
in an unexplained eternal something. The mystery taxes every account
equally; the question is which eternal something explains the most
of what we actually find. (Cross-reference the sibling book's title
with a wink.)

**Ch. 4 — Polarity: The Signature of the Made World.** Ecclesiastes
3 as ontology: a time for war and peace, embrace and refraining —
everything that exists, exists against its opposite. Motion is
relative; so is everything else. God and not-God as the primal
polarity; devil-talk explicitly deferred as "tertiary" (the
transcript's own move — keeps Part I clean of theodicy). Sets up the
wager: a world structured by polarity is a world in which choosing
sides is not optional.

*Interlude: The Reason, So Far — honesty about the bottom turtle is
not agnosticism's victory; it is the shared starting line.*

## PART II — THE STORY PHYSICS TELLS (5 chapters, ~19,000 words)
*The universe behaves like an ongoing declaration, not an accident.*

**Ch. 5 — Let There Be Light, As Mechanism.** The Beginning as the
initiation of communication among Conscious Points — thirteen grid
points, one command, and every Moment since as renewed obedience to
the rules. The Genesis verb and the substrate's first Moment told as
one event. Depth: fully accessible; zero equations; the 13-GP
cohort, the Moment, the Sea — imagery only.

**Ch. 6 — A Universe That Is Computed Is a Universe That Is Willed.**
The Perceive-Compute-Displace cycle as perpetual divine attention;
existence as maintained, not launched. Contrast deism's wound-clock.
Depth: conceptual; the reader meets "the universe is God's ongoing
self-declaration" as the physics' natural reading, not a pious
gloss.

**Ch. 7 — The Skeptic's Chapter: Does This Story Pay Rent?** The
honest hesitator's due: what distinguishes a story that explains
everything (and therefore nothing) from one that predicts. The
swarm — a hundred-plus zero-parameter results from nine axioms —
presented as cumulative witness testimony, with the dark-matter and
dark-energy campaigns as the live cross-examinations, reported with
their falsifiers showing. Depth: the ONE chapter that gestures at
numbers; everything sourced to TATWD for the reader who wants the
derivations. Explicit statement of what would falsify the framework
— trust is earned by exposure.

**Ch. 8 — The Vacuum That Refuses to Thin.** Dark energy as parable:
space opens, and the Sea fills it — new pairs form so the medium
never dilutes; the not-thinning is the acceleration. A universe
whose emptiest places are ceaselessly replenished as a picture of
grace (the manna economy: enough, every day, no hoarding, no lack).
Depth: the freshest result in the corpus rendered wholly in imagery;
doubles as the book's central metaphor for providence.

**Ch. 9 — Mind at the Bottom.** Why "conscious" points: the argument
that awareness is not an awkward latecomer but the substrate's first
property; the hard problem inverted. What this does and does not
claim (panpsychism distinguished from the personal God — the points
obey; the Person commands). Depth: philosophy of mind at trade
level; Chalmers named, not litigated.

*Interlude: The Reason, So Far — physics cannot prove the Person,
but the universe it reveals is exactly what a declaring Person's
universe would look like — and it keeps passing tests.*

## PART III — THE STORY SCRIPTURE TELLS (5 chapters, ~18,000 words)
*The moral law as the same declaration, heard from the inside.*

**Ch. 10 — The God Who Declares.** From "Let there be light" to "Thou
shalt not": one voice, two registers — physical law as command to
the substrate, moral law as command to the free. Why command is the
right category for both (and why the materialist has laws with no
legislator on both counts).

**Ch. 11 — The Image: Points That Can Refuse.** Human freedom as the
substrate's one licensed exception — creatures who, unlike the
Conscious Points, may decline the rule and feel the difference.
Conscience as the felt SSV of the moral field (one metaphor, used
once, then released). Sin as acting against the grain of the
declared world.

**Ch. 12 — Christ, the Declaration Made Flesh.** The Word through
whom all things were made entering the made thing; incarnation as
the author stepping onto the substrate. The cross and resurrection
as the moral field's deepest polarity — death/life, justice/mercy —
resolved in a Person. Depth: creedal center held plainly; written so
the hesitator sees coherence even before belief.

**Ch. 13 — Theosis: What the Universe Is For.** Thomas's theology in
full voice: the universe as God's chosen arena of self-experience
toward righteousness; human life as apprenticeship in the divine
character; "act right" as participation, not rule-keeping. The
youngest-and-oldest-God intuition from the naming conversation
engaged and ordered.

**Ch. 14 — Answering the Old Objections.** Euthyphro (the
declaration IS the character of the Declarer — no dilemma); the
problem of evil at fellowship depth (polarity, freedom, the tertiary
question now given its hour); "religion is a crutch" (a reason is
not a crutch; it is a spine).

*Interlude: The Reason, So Far — the same Source, two testimonies;
inner command and outer law agree.*

## PART IV — THE STORY SOCIETY TELLS (4 chapters, ~15,000 words)
*What happens downstream of the anchor, kept or cut.*

**Ch. 15 — The World With No Moral Valence.** The materialist wager
lived out: if the world is only an "it" that does stuff randomly,
then morality is preference and power. The communist century as the
controlled experiment; the honest accounting of its fruits. Depth:
historical, sober, non-hysterical; primary sources; no straw men —
the strongest secular humanisms acknowledged and asked where their
"ought" stands.

**Ch. 16 — Psychology: The Person Without a Reason.** Meaning,
anxiety, and the therapeutic culture; what anchored purpose does in
the clinical literature (the naturopathic physician's chapter —
Thomas's decades with patients). The daily "reason to get up and do
the right thing" as load-bearing mental architecture.

**Ch. 17 — Economics and Politics: Trust Is a Theological Artifact.**
Contracts, markets, and constitutions as frozen trust; what
covenant-keeping peoples can build and what its absence costs;
liberty's dependence on self-governed citizens. Margo's research
woven here. Depth: argumentative essay, not policy platform;
Tocqueville register.

**Ch. 18 — Renaissance: What Recovery Looks Like.** The positive
program — family, fellowship, vocation, culture-making; Renaissance
Ministries' actual practice (the Sunday table, the homeschool, the
essays) as existence proof at small scale. Not nostalgia:
renaissance means rebirth forward.

*Interlude: The Reason, So Far — societies run on the answer to this
book's question, whether they admit it or not.*

---

## PART V — THE REASON (3 chapters, ~11,000 words)
*Convergence, wager, practice.*

**Ch. 19 — Three Stories, One Voice.** The convergence argument
assembled formally: independent lines — cosmological, scriptural,
civilizational — triangulating one conclusion (the swarm method
applied to the whole worldview: no single line is proof; the
concurrence is the evidence). The bottom turtle revisited: still
mysterious, now inhabited.

**Ch. 20 — The Wager Reframed.** Beyond Pascal: not fire insurance
but fit — choosing to live with the grain of a declared universe.
The hesitator addressed by name (with permission): what "putting
your weight on it" costs and buys; provisional commitment as the
honest posture ("proceed as if true, audit the fruits").

**Ch. 21 — How to Act Right.** The practical chapter: the shape of a
rightly-acting ordinary life — truth-telling, promise-keeping, work,
marriage and family, money, speech, civic duty, sabbath, repair
after failure. Concrete, warm, unsentimental. Each practice tied
back to its reason — this is where the title lands its full weight:
not "act right because I said so" but "act right because reality
does."

**EPILOGUE — The Table (~2,000 words).** Back to the fellowship: the
book as an open chair; what happened to the conversations that made
it (Charlie, Mike, Isak — with their blessing); the invitation to
start a table of one's own. Final line returns the title as
benediction.

## 4. Breadth and depth controls (what stays out)

- **Physics depth:** capped at imagery + the single skeptic's
  chapter; every derivation points to TATWD. No equations anywhere.
- **Theology depth:** creedal center, fellowship register; no
  denominational disputes; Snuffer-adjacent controversies stay in
  the essay series, not here.
- **Politics depth:** civilizational argument only; no elections, no
  policy prescriptions, nothing datable within a decade.
- **Autobiography:** rationed to where it authenticates (the vision
  in St. Paul appears once, briefly, in Ch. 5 or the Prologue; the
  clinic in Ch. 16; the table in the Epilogue).

## 5. Subtitle candidates (title stands as proposed)

4. *A Reason to Act Right: Three Stories That End in One Command* —
