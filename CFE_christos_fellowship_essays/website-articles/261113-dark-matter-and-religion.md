# 261113 - Dark Matter and Religion

# Through a Glass, Darkly — and Then Face to Face

*A response to Michael Guillén on dark matter, dark energy, and the credibility of faith*

By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 13 November 2026

On November 8, 2026, physicist Michael Guillén published an essay at Fox News arguing that science's discoveries of dark matter and dark energy are making religion more credible, not less. I agree with him, and I want to take his argument one step further than he did. The 95 percent of the cosmos that science cannot see is not a hiding place for God. It is a signpost pointing to what the physical world actually is: the moment-by-moment expression of a conscious God, who is not standing outside His creation but living inside it, and inside each of us.

Guillén's essay is worth reading in full. He was trained in physics, mathematics, and astronomy at Cornell, taught physics at Harvard, and spent years as ABC News's science editor. He came to faith by way of science, not in spite of it. That gives his testimony weight, and it is why I want to engage his argument carefully rather than simply cheer it.

## What Guillén got right

Guillén's history is sound. In 1933 the Swiss astronomer Fritz Zwicky measured how fast galaxies move inside the Coma cluster and found they were moving far too fast to be held together by the matter that shines. Something unseen was supplying the extra gravity. He called it *dunkle Materie*, dark matter. Nearly a century later, no laboratory has caught a single particle of it.

In 1929 Edwin Hubble showed the universe is expanding, and for seventy years astronomers assumed gravity was slowing that expansion down. In 1998 two independent teams found the opposite: the expansion is speeding up. Michael Turner named the cause "dark energy" that same year. Naming it did not explain it.

Guillén is also right that the mystery has deepened since 1998, not shrunk. The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), which has now mapped more than 47 million galaxies and quasars, has reported growing hints that dark energy may be weakening over time. If that holds, even the simplest working picture of dark energy, a fixed "cosmological constant," would have to be replaced. The full five-year DESI results are expected in 2027. The honest summary is the one Guillén gives: the more powerful our instruments become, the more clearly we see how much we do not know.

He is right, finally, about the cultural story. For much of the twentieth century, educated people were told that science was steadily retiring God — that each discovery shrank the territory left for faith. Carl Sagan, who taught Guillén at Cornell, gave that hope its most eloquent form: science as a candle pushing back the dark. That story has not come true. What I want to add is why it was never going to.

## The mystery is not 95 percent. It is 100 percent.

Guillén's sharpest line is that dark matter and dark energy together make up about 95 percent of the cosmos, so all of science rests on the remaining 5 percent. I want to tighten that claim, because tightened it becomes stronger.

First, a point of accuracy. The 95 percent figure is itself a product of science. We know dark matter is there because ordinary physics, applied to ordinary matter, tells us the visible mass is not enough. We know dark energy is there because our measurements of distant exploding stars and of the pattern of galaxies require it. Science has not failed at 95 percent of reality; it has detected 95 percent of reality and cannot yet say what it is. The laws we built from the visible 5 percent are not overturned by this. They are what revealed the gap.

Second, and more important: even the 5 percent we "understand" we only describe. Physics can tell me, to many decimal places, how an electron behaves. It cannot tell me what an electron *is* — why there is charge at all, why it attracts and repels, why anything persists from one instant to the next. Every equation describes a regularity; none supplies the reason there is a regularity. Isaac Newton knew this about his own law of gravity and refused to pretend otherwise.

So I would put Guillén's point this way: science has not left us ignorant of 95 percent of reality. It has left us ignorant of the *being* of all of it, and dark matter and dark energy have simply made that ignorance impossible to ignore.

## Faith cannot rest on the gaps

There is a danger in Guillén's framing that I think he would recognize. If religion becomes more credible because science cannot explain dark matter, then religion becomes less credible the day science does explain it. Suppose a detector finally catches a dark matter particle, or a theorist writes an equation that accounts for dark energy. Has God then retreated by 27 or 68 percent? Believers made this mistake for centuries, placing God in whatever nature had not yet explained, and they watched that territory shrink with every discovery. Christians have a name for it: the "God of the gaps."

The Bible never asked us to find God in what we cannot explain. It asks us to find Him in what we *can* see:

*"For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse."* **(Romans 1:20)**

Paul's argument is not that the universe has holes in it. It is that the universe, fully seen, testifies to its Maker. A faith worth holding must survive explanation. It must be just as true of the 5 percent we have measured as of the 95 percent we have not. The question underneath Guillén's essay is not "what is science missing?" It is "what is the physical world made of, at bottom, and why does it exist at all?" That is the question I have spent my life on.

## What the darkness is pointing to

In March 1987 I received the picture that became the work of my life, which I call Conscious Point Physics. Its premise is simple to state. The smallest units of reality are not dead specks of stuff. They are *Conscious Points* — points of awareness, created and sustained by God, each one doing three things over and over: perceiving its surroundings, computing a response, and moving accordingly. Everything we call matter, light, force, and space is built from the collective behavior of these points. In plain words, the physical world is God's mind in action, expressed at the smallest scale.

That premise is not a sermon dressed as physics; it has to earn its keep as physics. Over roughly forty years, and especially in the last several with the help of AI collaborators, I have worked to derive the known laws from a small set of axioms, and the program has produced more than a hundred predictions that use no adjustable parameters. Dark matter and dark energy are active areas of that work. On dark energy, my analysis concludes that the substrate of space produces a constant push, with an equation-of-state value of w = −1.00 ± 0.02 and no weakening over time. That puts my theory squarely on the opposite side of DESI's current hints. I welcome that. When the full DESI results arrive in 2027, either the hints will fade and the prediction will stand, or they will firm up and I will have to find my error. That is how it should be. Dark matter I am still working through, and I will not claim a result I do not have.

I mention this not to ask anyone to accept my physics, but to show what a different starting point does. If reality is made of God's own conscious action, then the question "what is dark matter?" is not a threat to faith or a gift to it. It is a question about how God's points of awareness organize themselves in ways that do not emit light. The darkness is not where God hides. It is more of His handiwork, not yet read.

*"And he is before all things, and by him all things consist."* **(Colossians 1:17)**

## God is not outside looking in

Here is where I part from conventional thinking on both sides of the science-and-religion debate. The skeptic pictures a universe that runs itself, with no one home. Many believers picture a universe God built and now watches from outside, like an engineer observing a machine. If the foundation of reality is God's own awareness, both pictures are wrong. God is not outside the world looking in. He is inside it, living it.

That includes us. I believe God inhabits and experiences every person's life from the inside. We are, in a real sense, God's experience of the world. When I choose kindness, He feels it. When I choose cruelty, He feels that too — both in me and in the one I wound. This is why His law is not arbitrary. He gave it because He loves us and because He bears every consequence of how we treat one another. We keep it, in the end, not out of fear but out of love for the One who feels our choices.

This is also, I am convinced, the key to why the Cross was necessary: a God who lives inside every life must reconcile Himself with the sin He has experienced from within His creation. I have laid that argument out more fully in ["Reconciliation of Man with the Father"](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/07/25/260725-reconciliation-of-man-with-the-father/) and in a one-page summary, the [Premise Change handout](https://renaissance-ministries.com/2026/10/31/260725-premise-change-handout/).

Seen this way, Guillén's dark cosmos becomes something more than evidence against materialism. It is a reminder that the God who sustains every unseen particle in the most distant galaxy is the same God who is present in the person sitting across from you.

## Then face to face

Guillén ends with the first half of a verse from Paul. I want to finish it, because the second half is the point:

*"For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known."* **(1 Corinthians 13:12)**

Paul is not celebrating darkness. He is describing a temporary condition on the way to full sight. That is the right posture for both the scientist and the believer. We should not pretend to know what dark matter is, and we should not treat the not-knowing as proof of anything. We should keep looking, with every instrument and every honest argument we have, expecting that what we find will be more of God's work, not less.

*"Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear."* **(Hebrews 11:3)**

The author of Hebrews wrote that the visible world was made from what is not visible, some nineteen centuries before Zwicky pointed his telescope at the Coma cluster. Sagan's candle has not banished the dark. It has shown us that the dark is full — and, I believe, full of Him.
