# 261119 - Demons, Disease, and Exorcism

# The Powers, the Cross, and the Commission


### *Why the Church's Warfare Is Neither Exorcism Nor Denial*

By Thomas Lee Abshier, ND | 19 November 2026

## A Physician's Two Temptations

I have spent my working life as a physician, and I have watched two kinds of patients walk through the door carrying the same mistake from opposite directions. The first has been told by someone at church that his depression, his migraines, or his son's seizures are a spirit that needs to be rebuked. He has stopped his medication, or never started it, because taking it felt like a lack of faith. The second has been told by someone with a degree that the spiritual world is a pre-scientific story, and that anything a Gospel writer called a demon was really epilepsy or psychosis that the ancients did not have the vocabulary to name. He has a pharmacy in his cabinet and no category in his mind for evil that has a will.

I stand on the side of neither. I believe Scripture describes a world in which spiritual powers are real, personal, and hostile, and in which disease is also real, physical, and treatable. I also believe that the war against those powers has already been decided, and that the weapon the Church has been handed to finish it is not a deliverance technique but the proclamation of the Gospel to the nations. That is the case I want to make here.

## The Powers Are Real

The New Testament does not speak of spiritual evil as a figure of speech. Paul writes, *"For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places"* **(Ephesians 6:12)**. Those words — rulers, authorities, powers — were the vocabulary of government in Paul's world. He is describing an administration, not a mood.

The Old Testament tells us where that administration came from. In the oldest manuscripts of Deuteronomy, Moses says that when the Most High divided the nations, *"he fixed the borders of the peoples according to the number of the sons of God"* **(Deuteronomy 32:8)**, keeping Israel as His own portion. The nations were placed under spiritual stewards. Psalm 82 shows us how that stewardship went. God stands in the divine council and indicts those stewards for judging unjustly and favoring the wicked, and He pronounces sentence: *"I said, 'You are gods, sons of the Most High, all of you; nevertheless, like men you shall die'"* **(Psalm 82:6–7)**. Daniel gives us a glimpse of that rebellion in operation. The messenger sent to answer his prayer tells him, *"The prince of the kingdom of Persia withstood me twenty-one days"* **(Daniel 10:13)**.

The picture is consistent from the Law to the Prophets to the Apostles: the nations were handed to spiritual authorities, those authorities went bad, and the history of the world bears the marks of their corruption.

## Why Their Rebellion Matters to God

Here I want to say something that I believe the Church has largely missed, and which is central to everything I teach. God does not watch His creation from a distance. He inhabits it. He lives every life from the inside. We are, in a real sense, God's experience of the world. When a child is abused, God feels it. When a nation is led into idolatry by a corrupt power, God experiences the degradation of every person in it.

This is why rebellion is not a minor administrative failure in God's eyes. A steward who leads the nations into unGodly worship, injustice, and cruelty is not simply breaking a rule; he is inflicting pain on the One who feels every one of those lives. And it is why God's law is not arbitrary. He asks us to keep it because He feels the consequences of our choices, and love does not willingly wound the one it loves. The powers chose otherwise. They used their authority over the nations to produce suffering that God Himself bore.

## The Sentence Was Carried Out at the Cross

The verdict of Psalm 82 did not stay on paper. Paul tells us that at the cross God *"disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, by triumphing over them in him"* **(Colossians 2:15)**. John says the same thing in plainer words: *"The reason the Son of God appeared was to destroy the works of the devil"* **(1 John 3:8)**.

The cross was necessary because the breach between God and man was real and had to be healed from the inside — in a human life, by One who bore the full weight of what sin does to God and to us. In that act the powers lost their legal claim. They are still active, but they are active the way an army is active after its capital has fallen: still dangerous, still capable of doing damage in the provinces, but fighting a war that is already lost.

## The Calibration: Neither Superstition Nor Denial

This is where I want to be careful, because both errors I described at the beginning collapse here.

Illness is real. Trauma is real. Chemical imbalance, nutrient deficiency, toxic exposure, genetic disorders, brain injury — I have treated all of them, and none of them is cured by shouting at it. To withhold care from a sick person in order to stage a deliverance is not faith; it is neglect, and the one who does it will answer for it. Jesus said, *"Those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick"* **(Matthew 9:12)**. He was making a spiritual point, but He made it with an image that assumes physicians do good work. Paul traveled with *"Luke the beloved physician"* **(Colossians 4:14)**. Scripture never asks us to choose between the doctor and God.

But the opposite reflex is no wiser. A world with no real spiritual agency is not the world of the Bible; it is the world of modern materialism laid over the Bible like a stencil. The Gospels distinguish between healing the sick and casting out demons **(Mark 1:34)**; the writers knew the difference. When I see a person whose torment does not respond to anything I can do, who is drawn compulsively toward destruction, whose life is bound up with occult practice, I do not dismiss the possibility that something more than chemistry is at work. Wisdom treats the body and resists the devil, and does not pretend either one away.

## The War We Are Actually Called to Wage

If the powers are real and already defeated, how does the Church finish the work? The answer is in the last words Jesus spoke before He ascended: *"All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.** Go therefore and make disciples of all nations"* **(Matthew 28:18–19)**.

Read that against Deuteronomy 32 and Psalm 82 and it takes on a different weight. The nations that were handed over to corrupt stewards are being taken back. Jesus is not starting a membership drive; He is announcing a change of government. The authority the powers once held over the nations now belongs to Him, and He sends His people to carry the news across every border the darkness once controlled. When Paul described his own commission, he said he was sent *"to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God"* **(Acts 26:18)**. That is a transfer of allegiance, nation by nation, person by person.

This is what much of the deliverance movement misses. The powers are not dislodged by mapping the demonic hierarchy over a city or by staging confrontations with territorial spirits. Scripture gives no such method. What it gives every believer is the same plain armor: truth, righteousness, readiness from the Gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the word of God, and prayer **(Ephesians 6:13–18)**. And it gives a plain instruction: *"Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you"* **(James 4:7)**. The weapons of this war are not the techniques of specialists. They are the obedience, the honesty, and the witness of ordinary Christians.

I would add one thing. Because God lives inside every life, the Gospel is not only a legal announcement; it is an invitation for each person to stop wounding the One who feels his choices, and to begin living in a way that gives God joy rather than grief. Every person who turns to Christ is a province returned to its rightful King — and a part of God's own experience of the world restored to what He intended.

## The Gates Will Not Hold

Jesus promised that *"the gates of hell shall not prevail against it"* **(Matthew 16:18)**. Gates do not attack. They defend. The image tells us who is on the offensive. The Church is not a garrison waiting out a siege; it is the advancing force, and hell is the city behind the wall.

So when someone tells you that the Christian life is mostly a matter of binding spirits, or that a serious modern believer should not think about spirits at all, you can answer with confidence that Scripture says neither. The powers of darkness are real, they are hostile, and they are already judged. They were disarmed at a Roman cross two thousand years ago, and the campaign that finishes them has a name. It is the Great Commission. Every believer who carries the Gospel into a life, a family, or a nation the darkness once ruled is fighting in the only war whose outcome has already been declared.
