# FDA DICE Call — Summary
**Date:** September 1, 2026
**Participants:** Isak Gutierrez, Joelle Ater, FDA DICE agent

---

## Key Findings

### 1. If it lifts, it's a lift
The FDA agent was clear: **if the device has lift capability, the agency would still consider it to be a lift**, regardless of whether the primary function is walking/therapy. Marketing it as "just a walker" while it has a powered lift column does not avoid the lift classification. Intended use matters, but physical capability matters too.

### 2. Dual product codes are allowed
A device with more than one function can have **more than one product code** — a primary and secondary. The FDA guidance on product codes explains this "subsequent product codes" concept. The company determines the primary code.

### 3. No pre-market authorization needed for any of the lift codes
- **FSA** (Lift, patient, non-AC-powered): Class I, 510(k) exempt
- **FNG** (Lift, patient, AC-powered): Class II, but **510(k) exempt**
- Neither requires a pre-market submission before selling

### 4. The 513(g) process exists but is optional
For $7,820 (or $4,298 small business), the FDA will give a **suggested classification** — not binding, but an official artifact. It's a convenience, not a requirement.

### 5. Risk of wrong classification
If you classify under a code that doesn't require pre-market authorization but the FDA later determines it should have been under one that does, they can **make you stop selling** until the correct submission is filed and authorized.

## What This Changes

**The ITJ-only strategy is weaker than we thought.** The FDA agent essentially said that a device with a powered lift column cannot avoid the lift classification through marketing language alone. This aligns with what the decision tree flagged as the root question.

**However, the practical impact may be smaller than feared:**
- FSA (the most likely lift code) is still Class I, still 510(k) exempt
- The difference is **GMP requirements** — FSA requires a full quality system (ISO 13485-level), while ITJ is GMP-exempt
- No pre-market submission needed either way

**The Biodex BXB (powered exerciser) path is still worth exploring** — the FDA agent didn't mention it, and it wasn't asked about directly. A 513(g) submission would settle this definitively.
