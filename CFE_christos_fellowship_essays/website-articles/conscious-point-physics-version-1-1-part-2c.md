### 4.107.2 Experimental Setup

- **Photon Source and Entanglement:** A laser pumps a nonlinear crystal (e.g., BBO) to produce entangled photon pairs via spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC). One photon (signal) goes to the double-slit, while the other (idler) is sent to a separate detection path.

- **Double-Slit and Signal Detection:** The signal photon passes through a double-slit setup, and its position is recorded on a screen (detector D0), potentially showing an interference pattern (wave-like) or a clump pattern (particle-like).

- **Which-Path Information:** The idler photon’s path is manipulated using beam splitters (BS) and mirrors to provide “which-path” information. Detectors (D1, D2) record whether the idler came from slit A or B, collapsing the signal photon’s state to particle-like behavior.

- **Quantum Eraser:** Additional beam splitters (BSA, BSB) can erase which-path information by mixing idler paths, directing them to detectors (D3, D4). This restores interference patterns in the signal photon’s data when correlated with D3/D4, even if the idler is detected after the signal.

- **Delayed Choice:** The idler’s detection can be delayed (e.g., by longer optical paths), meaning the choice to retain or erase which-path information occurs after the signal photon is detected, suggesting retrocausal influence.
